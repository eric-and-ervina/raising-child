# Milestones — The First Year, Observed Not Graded

> **For:** Eldric (born 26 Oct 2026) and his parents. This is the file the observation log was waiting for: **what typically shows up and when, what is worth mentioning at a PD visit, what is genuinely worth acting on, and the log to record it in.**
> **Read this instead of a milestone app.** Apps send a notification when he's a day "late" and call it a delay; ranges below are wider than most parents expect and everything in the middle is normal.
> **Companions:** the practical 0–3 months layer (tummy time, play, the month-by-month) is [`first-three-months.md`](first-three-months.md); the long-range plan is [`../parenting/parenting-guide-raising-a-healthy-grounded-boy.md`](../parenting/parenting-guide-raising-a-healthy-grounded-boy.md) §10; appointments are [`../health/pd-visits-and-vaccinations.md`](../health/pd-visits-and-vaccinations.md).
> *General information, not medical advice. Milestones are for observation and conversation with the PD — never for drilling a baby.*

---

## 0. How to use this file (four rules)

1. **Ranges, not deadlines.** Every "by" in this file is roughly the age at which **most** babies do the thing. A baby who does it three months later is usually a normal baby who does it three months later.
2. **Four domains, and they don't move together.** Motor, language, social/emotional, thinking. A baby can walk early and talk late and be entirely ordinary. **Look at the pattern, not one box.**
3. **Observe, don't drill.** You are not training a puppy. Tummy time and talking to him are the whole curriculum; flashcards and "milestone exercises" are not.
4. **The one thing that is always urgent: losing a skill he already had.** Rolling then stopping, babbling then going silent, reaching then not. That's a same-week call, every time — not "he's just lazy today".

**And the honest caveat:** the ranges here come from population data (CDC/AAP, WHO). Eldric was born at term, average size, no complications — which means the ranges apply to him well. Premature babies get corrected ages; he doesn't need one.

## 1. 0–3 months (the recap)

Full detail lives in [`first-three-months.md`](first-three-months.md). The short version: sleep, eat, cry, plus startles, a grasp reflex, focusing on faces at 20–30 cm, quieting to your voice, and **the first real social smile around 6 weeks** (earlier "smiles" are usually reflex — enjoy them anyway).

**What to do:** tummy time on your chest from week 1, narrate everything, respond when he cries, keep the sleep and feeding logs. **Nothing else is required of him or you.**

## 2. 3–6 months (26 Jan – 26 Apr 2027)

**This is the stretch where he becomes a person you can play with.** It's also the stretch where a lot of parents suddenly compare him to other babies on Instagram — don't.

| Domain | Typically appears | Most babies can by | Ask the PD if not by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Motor** | Holds head steady when upright (~3–4 m); pushes up on forearms in tummy time; **rolls tummy → back** (~4–5 m), then back → tummy (~5–6 m); brings hands together and to mouth; reaches and grabs a toy (~3–5 m); sits with support, leaning on hands (~6 m) | 5 months | **6 months** |
| **Language** | Coos and "talks" back and forth with you (~3–4 m); laughs aloud (~4 m); squeals, blows raspberries (~5 m); turns toward your voice and to sound | 5 months | **6 months** |
| **Social** | **Social smile on purpose** (already by ~6–8 weeks); recognises you and reacts differently to strangers (~5–6 m); enjoys peekaboo; smiles to get your attention | 4 months | **5 months** |
| **Thinking** | Watches his own hands, stares at a toy and swipes at it, mouthing everything, anticipates a feed when he sees the pillow | 6 months | **7 months** |
| **Self-feeding/feeding** | Feeds get faster and more efficient; distractible at the breast (a normal, annoying phase — feed in a dim quiet room) | — | — |

**Play:** black-and-white and then coloured cards, a play gym or a few links over the bouncer, your face, mirrors, songs with actions, board books he can grab and chew. **A rattle does the job of most of the toy aisle.**

**What's coming that nobody warns you about:** the **4-month sleep regression** ([`../health/night-and-sleep.md`](../health/night-and-sleep.md) §9), swaddle-out at the first roll, and the start of **distracted feeding** — a baby who pops off at every sound is not rejecting you.

## 3. 6–9 months (26 Apr – 26 Jul 2027)

| Domain | Typically appears | Most babies can by | Ask the PD if not by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Motor** | **Sits without support** (~6–8 m); rolls both ways confidently; **crawls** (~7–10 m — some babies bottom-shuffle or skip it entirely, which is fine); pulls to stand (~8–9 m); **rakes small objects** and develops a **pincer grip** (~8–9 m); transfers a toy hand to hand | 8 months | **9 months** (sitting); 12 months (pulling to stand) |
| **Language** | **Babbles with consonants** — "ba-ba", "da-da", varied sounds (~6–9 m); takes turns in a "conversation" of sounds; responds to his **name** (~8–9 m); understands "no" and simple words with gestures (~9 m) | 8 months | **9 months** (no babbling) |
| **Social** | **Stranger anxiety** and **separation anxiety** begin (~7–9 m) — this is a milestone, not a setback; laughs at peekaboo; starts to test your reaction | 9 months | — |
| **Thinking** | Looks for a dropped toy; **object permanence** begins (looks for a hidden toy ~8–9 m); bangs objects together; understands cause and effect (push the button, it lights) | 9 months | **10 months** |
| **Feeding** | Around **6 months: readiness for solids** — good head control, sits with support, shows interest in food, tongue-thrust reflex fading. **This is the next big project** — see the note below | 6 months | **7 months** |

**Solids at ~6 months is a real milestone and a whole topic.** It isn't covered in depth in this repo yet (the feeding files are milk-only by design) — the short version: **start around 6 months, not before 4**, iron-rich first foods, milk stays the main source for months, no honey, no salt, no choking-shape foods, **never in a bottle**, and he stays upright and supervised. **Ask the PD at the 4-month visit** and get their preferred approach on record.

**Baby-proofing happens now, not later:** he will be mobile before you think he is. Outlet covers, cabinet locks, cot mattress lowered, cords and curtain ties up, hot drinks never at floor level ([`../health/dos-and-donts.md`](../health/dos-and-donts.md) §Home safety).

## 4. 9–12 months (26 Jul – 26 Oct 2027)

| Domain | Typically appears | Most babies can by | Ask the PD if not by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Motor** | **Cruises** along furniture (~9–11 m); stands alone briefly (~11 m); **first steps** (9–15 m — walking at 15 months is still within normal); picks up a pea with thumb and finger; bangs two toys together | 12 months | **15 months** (walking); **12 months** (pincer) |
| **Language** | **First real word** — used consistently and with meaning (~10–15 m); **points** to show you things (~9–14 m); shakes head "no"; follows a simple instruction with a gesture ("give it to me") | 12 months | **15 months** (no words); **15 months** (no pointing/waving) |
| **Social** | **Waves bye-bye** (~9–12 m); gives you a toy when asked; shows you things; clear preference for familiar adults; may hand you a book to read | 12 months | **15 months** |
| **Thinking** | **Finds a hidden toy** (~10–12 m); puts things into a container and tips them out; imitates actions (stirring, wiping); knows what everyday objects are for | 12 months | **15 months** |
| **Self-help** | Finger-feeds himself; holds a spoon (messily); drinks from an open cup with help; helps push his arm into a sleeve | 12 months | — |

**What this year is actually building:** not skills, but **security**. Every time he crawls away and comes back to check you're still there, and you are, he's learning the thing that the whole parenting guide is about ([`../parenting/parenting-guide-raising-a-healthy-grounded-boy.md`](../parenting/parenting-guide-raising-a-healthy-grounded-boy.md) §1).

## 5. The one-year check, at a glance

By **~26 Oct 2027** most babies: sit unsupported, pull to stand, cruise or walk, pick up small things with finger and thumb, look when you call their name, babble in sentences of sounds, say 1–2 words, point, wave, copy you, and look for a toy you hide under a cloth.

**If he isn't doing several of those at 12 months, that's a conversation, not a catastrophe** — the PD will check hearing, vision, tone, and development, and early support (if it's ever needed) works best when it starts early. **Say it at the visit; don't sit on it for six months.**

## 6. When to raise it — sorted by how much noise to make

### 6.1 Urgent — same week, and today if it's a loss of skill

- **Any skill he had and lost** — stopped rolling, stopped babbling, stopped reaching, stopped smiling at you.
- **A seizure, or odd repetitive movements** you can't interrupt ([`../health/red-flags-and-emergencies.md`](../health/red-flags-and-emergencies.md)).
- **Very stiff** (arched, legs crossing, hard to bend) or **very floppy** (slides through your hands, head lags badly at 4+ months).
- **Always using one side only**, or one hand permanently fisted past ~6 months, or a **hand preference before 12 months**.
- **No response to sound** at any age, or no startle to a loud noise.
- **Head growth** — a head that's growing too fast or has stopped (the PD measures it; that's why head circumference is in the log).

### 6.2 Ask at the next visit — not emergencies, but don't sit on them

- No social smile by ~3 months.
- Not holding his head steady by ~4 months.
- Not reaching for or grabbing toys by ~5 months.
- Not sitting without support by ~9 months.
- No consonant babbling by ~9 months.
- No pointing, waving, or gestures by ~12–15 months.
- No words by ~15 months.
- **Feeding, growth, or weight concerns at any age** ([`../health/weight-and-growth.md`](../health/weight-and-growth.md)).
- Strong asymmetry — always turning his head one way, or using one arm.

### 6.3 Normal variation — do not spend worry on these

- Rolling at 3 months or at 7 months.
- Crawling late, or **never crawling** (bottom-shuffling, "commando" crawling, and standing straight up are all fine).
- Walking at 10 months or 16 months.
- One or two words at 12 months, or five.
- Being the smallest or the biggest in the room, as long as he follows his own growth line ([`../health/weight-and-growth.md`](../health/weight-and-growth.md) §8).
- A bilingual baby who is "behind" in English specifically.

## 7. Three languages — how milestones work in this house

English, Mandarin, and Indonesian/Bahasa will all be in his ears from both parents and grandparents ([`first-three-months.md`](first-three-months.md) §What he needs).

- **Count words across all three languages, not in English.** A child who says "mama", "makan", and "milk" has three words.
- **Total vocabulary is what gets assessed**, and bilingual children's *total* vocabulary is comparable to monolingual peers even when each single language is smaller.
- **Bilingual children can start talking slightly later** — a small, common effect. It matters only if he's late *and* not catching up, or if he's late in **all** languages.
- **Never drop a language "to help him catch up".** The evidence doesn't support it, and it costs him his grandparents.
- **A speech referral is not a reason to stop** — tell the therapist all three languages; a good one will work with them.
- **What actually builds language:** face-to-face talking, narrating what you're doing, songs, books, and **responding to his sounds**. Screens don't, at this age ([`first-three-months.md`](first-three-months.md) §Play).

## 9. Play that builds the milestones (and gear you don't need)

| Age | What does the work | What to skip |
|---|---|---|
| **0–3 m** | Your face at 20–30 cm, your voice, tummy time on your chest, a black-and-white card, being carried around the flat | Play gym, toys, screens, "educational" videos |
| **3–6 m** | Play gym with a few hanging things, a mirror, rattles, songs with actions, board books to grab and chew, sitting propped with you | Battery toys with lights and music (overstimulating, and they play *at* him rather than with him) |
| **6–9 m** | **The floor**, safe household objects (a wooden spoon, a plastic tub, a scarf), peekaboo, stacking and knocking over, "where's it gone?", crawling space, books | Walkers (no benefit, real injury risk), jumpers, anything he can't get out of himself |
| **9–12 m** | Containers and things to fill them, a ball, push-along toys once he's cruising, imitation games, naming everything, board books he can turn | Screens, flashcards, "teach your baby to read" products |

**The unfashionable truth:** the highest-value plaything in a 689 sqft flat is **you, on the floor, talking.** Second is a cardboard box.

## 10. Sources and honesty notes

- **Milestones:** framed on the **CDC "Learn the Signs. Act Early."** checklists (2022 revision, which deliberately widened the "most babies" ages to reduce false alarm) and **WHO motor development** reference ranges. Where the two differ, the wider range is used here.
- **Walking:** the widely cited normal range is 9–18 months; this file uses 9–15 with a "ask by 15" line, matching common paediatric practice.
- **Bilingual language development:** the "count total vocabulary across languages" and "don't drop a language" guidance reflects the standard speech-therapy position (ASHA and Singapore practice).
- **"Loss of skills is urgent"** is a consistent red flag across developmental paediatrics, at any age.
- **This is general information.** It cannot examine Eldric, and it deliberately does not try to diagnose. The PD's assessment, plus the log you keep, is what turns a worry into an answer.
