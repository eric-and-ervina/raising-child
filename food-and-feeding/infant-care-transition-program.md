# Infant-Care Bottle Transition Program — the step-by-step version

> **For:** Eric + Ervina, first-time parents. Eldric, born Mon 26 Oct 2026.
> **What this is:** a day-by-day program that takes Eldric from 100% direct breastfeeding to drinking full feeds from a bottle calmly — so that when infant care starts (early January, 07:00–19:00 days), bottles are already routine and only the place is new.
> **How to use it:** do the phases in order. Each phase says what it's for, what you need, and the exact steps. Don't skip ahead — each phase makes the next one easy. If a day goes badly, repeat the day; the calendar has slack built in.
> **Background reading (only if you want it):** safe formula prep is [`formula-and-bottles.md`](formula-and-bottles.md) §2, milk storage is [`breastfeeding.md`](breastfeeding.md) §6. Everything you *must* do is written out in this file — you don't need to hop between files mid-feed.

---

## The five ideas to understand first (plain words)

1. **A bottle is a skill, not just a container.** Sucking from a breast and sucking from a bottle use different mouth movements, and the milk comes at a different speed. A baby who has only ever breastfed usually needs days of calm practice to accept a bottle — that's normal, not stubbornness. Babies introduced to a bottle gradually, starting around 3–6 weeks old, the vast majority learn it. Waiting until the week before care starts is what makes transitions hard.
2. **Nobody knows how much he drinks at the breast — and that's fine.** Breasts don't have measurement lines. The program *finds* his number: you offer a bottle with a known amount, see what he takes, and write it down. After a week of bottles, you'll know his per-feed volume better than any textbook table.
3. **Milk is made on demand.** Ervina's body makes roughly the amount that gets removed each day. So whenever a bottle *replaces* a breastfeed, she needs to pump or hand-express at about that time — otherwise her body reads the missed feed as "make less," and she can get uncomfortably full. This is why the program and the pump arrive together.
4. **Slow is the whole technique.** A bottle can pour milk into a baby much faster than a breast does. Fed too fast, babies gulp, take too much, get windy and miserable — and start preferring the bottle's easy flow. "Paced feeding" (Part 2 below) is how you keep the bottle at breast speed.
5. **Write it down or it didn't happen.** Sleep-deprived memory invents numbers. The log (Part 6) takes ten seconds per feed and becomes the exact document you hand the infant-care centre in January.

**One scope rule for the whole program: night feeds stay at the breast, start to finish.** Bottles are a daytime, care-hours skill — nobody is practising bottles at 3 a.m.

## What you need

**Already in the house:** 2 bottles (1 big, 1 small), a bottle steriliser (this is what the S$150 company newborn gift bought — not a pump), bottle brush, formula + tin scoop, and a **pre-loved breast pump** obtained through a pre-loved items app. No pump purchase is needed — but read the check-over below before relying on it.

**Buy in November (one shopping round):**
- **10 × 150 ml bottles** with slow-flow (newborn) teats — needed from Phase 2 (1 Dec). Buy the same brand as each other so parts interchange.
- **Breast-milk storage bags** (for freezing flat 120 ml portions) + a marker for labelling.
- Spare teats ×4.
- **Fresh membranes/valves for the pump** (the model-specific kit), and flange inserts in Ervina's sizes if the included flanges don't match (see check-over).

### The pre-loved pump — check it over before Phase 2
A pre-loved pump is fine, but pumps have wear parts, and a tired part quietly weakens suction.

1. Wash and sterilise every washable part before first use.
2. **Replace the membranes/valves** with a fresh set for that model — they're cheap, and they are the #1 cause of a "weak" used pump.
3. Inspect the tubing: any moisture, cloudiness or mould inside → replace the tubing.
4. Check the flange sizes against Ervina's measurements (19 mm / 21 mm, fitted at Thomson) and buy inserts if needed.
5. Test-run it once before Phase 2, so the first real session isn't the first time it's switched on.

Why this matters here: a worn membrane makes pumped output look low — and this program judges supply by nappies and weight, never by pump output. Don't let a cheap membrane write a false story.

**Buy in December:** a cooler bag + 2 ice packs (for the January milk run to the centre), and later (around month 4–5) 5 × 260 ml bottles when his feeds outgrow 150 ml.

## Part 2 — How to give a bottle (paced feeding, the full steps)

Do this the same way every time, whoever feeds him:

1. **Timing:** offer the bottle when he is *mildly* hungry — stirring, rooting, hands coming to his mouth. Not asleep (wake him fully first), and not screaming — a frantic baby can't learn anything; breastfeed and try the bottle at the next feed instead.
2. **Wash hands.** Make the bottle (formula steps are in Part 3, step A; expressed milk just needs warming — stand the bottle in a bowl of warm water for a few minutes, never microwave).
3. **Test the temperature:** a few drops on the inside of your wrist — warm, not hot.
4. **Hold him semi-upright** in your arms, head and neck supported, facing you. Never lying flat with the bottle tipped over him, and never prop the bottle.
5. **Touch the teat to his lips** and let him draw it into his mouth himself. Don't push it in.
6. **Hold the bottle almost horizontal** — just tilted enough that the teat stays full of milk. Gravity should not be doing the work; he should have to suck, like at the breast.
7. **Pause every 20–30 sucks:** tip the bottle down (or slide it out gently) for a few seconds and let him breathe and catch up.
8. **Halfway through, stop and burp him** — upright over your shoulder, a few minutes of gentle back pats/rubs.
9. **Swap arms/sides halfway**, like changing breasts.
10. **Let him decide when it's over.** Stop signs: turning his head away, pushing the teat out with his tongue, hands splaying, milk dribbling from the mouth, going sleepy. Never coax the last 10–20 ml down — an unfinished bottle is information, not a problem.
11. **Burp again at the end**, then **write the feed in the log** (Part 6) while it's fresh.

A good bottle feed takes **15–20 minutes**. Five minutes means the flow is too fast; 40 minutes of struggle means stop and treat it as a practice day (Part 5).

## Part 3 — Making a formula bottle (the short version)

Full rules and reasons: [`formula-and-bottles.md`](formula-and-bottles.md) §2. The steps:

1. Boil fresh water in the kettle. Let it stand **no more than ~30 minutes** (it must still be at least ~70 °C when it meets the powder — this is what makes powdered formula safe).
2. **Water in the bottle first**, measured to the line.
3. Add **level scoops** of powder — fill loosely, level off with the flat back of the scoop. Use only the scoop from that tin. Never heap, never pack, never add extra "to make it stronger."
4. Cap it and **swirl/roll** between your palms until dissolved (shaking whips in air = more wind later).
5. Cool the bottle standing in cold water until it's warm-not-hot on your wrist.
6. **Clocks to respect:** use within 2 hours of making; once he has started drinking, finish within 1 hour; whatever is left after that goes down the sink — never save a part-drunk bottle for later.

## Phase 1 — Learn the bottle, find his number
**Sat 14 Nov → Mon 30 Nov (weeks 3–5). One bottle a day. Eric gives it.**

*On the start date: he begins at 2 weeks 5 days, a few days under the usual 3-week guideline. That's fine — Week A is 30 ml practice, not meals, and he turns 3 weeks (Mon 16 Nov) during it. Nothing in Week A replaces a feed.*

### Week A (Sat 14 – Tue 17 Nov): practice, not meals
The goal this week is only familiarity: teat in mouth, milk tastes fine, nothing bad happens. He still gets all his real nutrition at the breast.

1. Pick **one calm slot a day** — late morning or early evening tends to work. Same rough time daily helps it become a routine.
2. Make **30 ml** of formula (Part 3). On day 3–4, if the previous day went calmly, make **45 ml**, then **60 ml**.
3. Feed it with the Part 2 steps. Ervina stays out of the room if she can — many babies refuse a bottle from the person who has the breast, or with her in sight, because they know the preferred option is *right there*. Eric giving the bottle is not a preference, it's the method. Being sent out of the room can feel strange for Ervina — it's temporary, and it's the method, not a judgement; once bottles are routine (Phase 2 onward), she can be nearby again.
4. **Success looks like:** any amount drunk calmly — even 10 ml on day 1. Pushes it away after a few sucks on day 1–2 is also normal.
5. **If he refuses:** don't push, don't let it become crying. End the session, breastfeed as normal, and change **one** thing tomorrow — the timing (earlier, at the first hunger cues), the temperature (a little warmer), or the teat position. One variable at a time, or you'll never know what fixed it.
6. Log every session, including refusals (Part 6).

### Weeks B–C (Wed 18 – Mon 30 Nov): one full feed a day
Once he takes 60 ml calmly, the daily bottle becomes a **real feed that replaces one breastfeed** — pick a consistent one (the early-evening feed works well: Eric does it, Ervina gets a break).

1. **Offer 90 ml.** That's the expected full feed at his age (the table in [`formula-and-bottles.md`](formula-and-bottles.md) §3 says 60–90 ml at weeks 2–4, growing).
2. Feed with Part 2 steps. Expect 15–20 minutes.
3. **Log it precisely:** offered / taken / minutes / the time he next showed hunger. The gap until the next hunger cue is the proof the feed was enough: a full feed should hold him roughly as long as a breastfeed does (at this age, very roughly 1½–3 hours — his own breastfed gaps are the comparison, not a book number).
4. **Ervina, at that same feed time:** hand-express or pump for ~10–15 minutes. Reasons: comfort (a skipped feed can leave her sore and full), and supply (Part 1, idea 3). Hand expression is enough at this one-feed scale; once the pre-loved pump's parts are refreshed (see "What you need"), she can use it instead. What she expresses can be tomorrow's bottle, refrigerated — or freeze it.
5. **By ~25 Nov you'll have about 7 logged full feeds. Average the "taken" column: that average is "his number."** Write it at the top of the log.
6. **Thu 26 Nov — the PD visit (Dr Leo) is the checkpoint:** tell him the number and show the log. Cross-check together: a baby needs roughly **150 ml per kg per day** in total, so (his weight × 150) ÷ (feeds per day) should land near his number. If it's wildly off, or his weight gain is off, the PD adjusts the plan — that's what the visit is for.

**Phase 1 is done when:** he finishes a full bottle calmly most days, and you have a written per-feed number you trust.

## Phase 2 — Match the care rhythm, build the freezer stash
**Tue 1 Dec → Mon 21 Dec (weeks 5–8). Two bottles a day. The pre-loved pump goes into daily service. The 10-bottle set arrives.**

### The daily shape
- **Two full bottle feeds a day, at care-like times: ~10:00 and ~14:30.** All other feeds stay at the breast.
- Bottle contents now shift from formula to **expressed milk as the main fill** (formula remains the backup and the pressure valve — a formula bottle on a low-pumping day is a fine outcome, not a failed day).

### Pumping — Ervina's new skill, step by step
Do this at each replaced feed (~10:00 and ~14:30), while Eric gives the bottle:

1. Wash hands. Assemble the pump clean (parts washed and sterilised after each use, air-dried).
2. **Flange fit matters more than pump power.** The flange (the funnel part) should let the nipple move freely without rubbing and without pulling in a lot of the surrounding area. Ervina's nipples were measured at Thomson at **19 mm and 21 mm** — use that fitting when choosing flange/insert sizes. Pain or blanched nipples = wrong fit, not "pumping is just like that."
3. Sit comfortably, warm compress or a minute of hand expression first helps the milk start.
4. Pump **both sides for ~15 minutes** (a double pump does both at once). Gentle massage toward the end helps empty.
5. **Write down what came out** (total ml, both sides) in the log, next to what he drank. Most mothers produce *less* than a full feed per session in the first week of pumping and catch up over 1–2 weeks as the body learns the pump's pattern. **A low number in week 1 is normal and says nothing about her supply** — the baby extracts milk better than any pump.
6. Pour into a storage bottle/bag, **label with date + time**, fridge it. That's tomorrow's bottle.

### The freezer stash
- When a day's pumping gives more than the next day's bottles need, freeze the extra as **one 120 ml portion in a storage bag, laid flat** (flat bags thaw fast and stack like files).
- **Target: 10 frozen portions by Mon 21 Dec** — that's two full care days, plus the emergency stash the centre keeps.
- Storage limits (healthy full-term baby): fridge **4 days**, freezer **best within 6 months**, thawed milk **24 hours in the fridge and never refreeze**, room temperature **4 hours at most — and in a warm HDB, treat that as a ceiling, fridge things early**.
- Use the **oldest** frozen portion first.

### Volumes in this phase
- He grows; the bottles grow with him. Expect his number to move from ~90 ml toward **90–120 ml per feed**.
- **The +15 rule:** if he finishes the whole bottle three feeds in a row and still roots around for more, offer 15 ml more from then on. If he's consistently leaving 20+ ml, drop the offer back — leftover expressed milk is the only genuinely sad waste in this program.

**Phase 2 is done when:** the two-bottle day runs without drama, Ervina's pumped output per session roughly matches what he drinks per feed (±20 ml), and the freezer holds ~10 portions.


## Phase 3 — Rehearse the real day
**Tue 22 Dec → first care day (he turns 2 months Sat 26 Dec; centres take babies from 2 months, so the start is late Dec / first week of Jan).**

### Three dress-rehearsal days (do them consecutively, before the centre starts)
Run the actual January day at home, end to end:

1. **~06:30** — breastfeed at home. (This is the "edge feed" — the 12 care hours sit between this and the evening home feed.)
2. **Morning pack (Eric, timed — see the checklist below).** Bottles packed in the cooler bag with ice packs, labelled with his name and the date/time the milk was expressed.
3. **Bottles at ~09:00, ~12:00, ~15:00, ~18:00** — fed from the packed bottles, by Eric or whoever is home, *not* in the usual feeding chair if you can vary it. This is the rhythm the centre will run: 4 feeds inside the 12 hours.
4. **Ervina pumps on a work-day schedule** (~10:30, ~13:30, ~16:30) — her output those days is what a real work day produces, and it becomes the next day's bottles. Now you know whether the milk loop closes or whether 1 bottle a day needs to be formula. Do that arithmetic honestly: 3 pumps at ~120–150 ml each give ~360–450 ml against ~480 ml of bottles — so unless her per-pump output runs above ~150 ml, **plan on about 1 formula bottle a day** (or add a 4th pump early in the morning). That is a normal shape for a 12-hour care day, not a shortfall.
5. **~19:00 onwards** — home, breastfeed as normal. Note the gap from the 18:00 bottle is only an hour: this feed may be small, or mostly comfort — if he's not hungry, that's fine. The 18:00 bottle was the last full feed; the evening breastfeed is also the reconnection feed, and its size doesn't have to prove anything.
6. **Evening:** all bottles washed, sterilised, air-drying for tomorrow. Note how long the full loop (pack + wash) actually takes — that's your real January morning/evening cost, measured instead of guessed.

**Expected totals per care day:** 4 feeds × ~120 ml ≈ **480 ml**, plus the spare bottle in rotation.

### The morning pack checklist (for a 07:00–19:00 care day)
- [ ] **5 bottles**, filled and labelled (4 feeds + 1 spare) — the centre does **not** wash bottles, so every feed needs its own (confirm at intake; it's the centre's own policy to state)
- [ ] Cooler bag + 2 ice packs
- [ ] **Nappies ×8** (he goes through roughly 6–8 a day; the centre will tell you their resupply rhythm)
- [ ] His own wipes (99% water, fragrance-free — the house standard)
- [ ] 2 spare outfits + a small blanket
- [ ] BioGaia drops, if the centre is to give them (confirm with the centre; some won't handle medication/supplements — then give them at home instead)

The centre gives you their own list at intake — merge it with this one and follow theirs where they differ.

### The intake sheet (what you tell the centre, all from your log)
- Feeds: **~4 bottles in the 12 hours**, at roughly **09:00 / 12:00 / 15:00 / 18:00**, **~120 ml each** (replace with his measured number), plus home breastfeeds at the edges
- How he shows hunger (rooting, hands to mouth — crying is late), and how he shows he's done
- Burp after every feed; he must be fully awake to drink
- Nappy habits and counts from the log
- Medical notes: vaccinations (booklet), the BioGaia drops, jaundice history, the blue-grey patch on his foot
- What soothes him — write down what actually works by December, not what books say

### Timing around the start
- **2-month vaccinations** in this window, ideally just before the first care day (book at the 26 Nov PD visit).
- **Ramp-up:** most centres settle babies in with short days first (e.g., a few hours, then half days) across the first week. Take that option — by then, the bottle is routine and only the place and the people are new, which is exactly the state this program was built to reach.

## Part 5 — Troubleshooting (the normal problems, and what each one means)

| What happens | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Pushes the teat out, turns away, day 1–3 | Normal novelty — the skill isn't there yet | End calmly, breastfeed, retry tomorrow. This is the expected shape of Week A |
| Takes the bottle from Eric, refuses with Ervina nearby | He knows the breast is available and prefers it | Ervina out of the room for bottle sessions (Part 3, Week A). It's a compliment to her, not a failure |
| Starts fussing or refusing at the **breast** after bottle-heavy days | Possible flow preference — the bottle is delivering faster than the breast, and he's learned to expect the easy flow | Drop back to one bottle a day for a week, and make that bottle slower: more horizontal, pauses more often, check the teat is still slow-flow. Breast offered first at all other feeds. Usually settles within days; tell Dr Leo if it doesn't |
| Gulps, splutters, milk spills from his mouth, feed done in 5 minutes | Flow too fast | Check the teat is slow-flow/newborn; hold the bottle more horizontal; pause more often |
| Falls asleep mid-bottle | Very normal at this age | Wake fully (nappy change, sit him up, cool flannel), then continue. The full-wake rule exists because sleepy babies under-drink and then wake hungry an hour later |
| Cries the moment the bottle appears, several days running | Something specific is wrong, or he was too hungry when offered | Offer earlier, at the first cues; check temperature (some babies want it warmer); try a different teat shape; drop back to 30 ml practice days for 3–4 days |
| Refuses bottles entirely for a week+ | Rare at this starting age, but possible | Tell Dr Leo at the 26 Nov visit (or call sooner) — he'll check mouth/tongue and suggest variants. Don't escalate force at home; feeding battles teach the opposite lesson |
| Ervina's pump output is small in the first week | Normal — pumps underperform babies early on | Keep sessions regular, check flange fit, don't conclude anything about supply until ~2 weeks of pumping. Judge supply by his nappies and weight, never by the pump bottle |
| Ervina gets painfully full at a replaced feed | The replacement pumping hasn't happened or was too short | Pump/hand-express to comfort at that feed time; don't skip the session two days running |
| He drinks bottles fine but wants the breast constantly afterwards | Comfort, not hunger — check the log: if the bottle volume matched his number and the hunger gap was normal, he's asking for closeness, not milk | Give the closeness. It's not a sign the bottle failed |

**The gauges that override everything:** 6+ wet nappies and 3+ dirty nappies a day, and steady weight gain. If those hold, the program is working even on days that *feel* messy. If they slip for more than a day, call the PD — don't troubleshoot feeding alone.

## Done looks like (the whole program, by the first care day)
- A full bottle finished calmly in 15–20 minutes, three days running, from a bottle packed that morning
- His per-feed volume and the day's total written down, from measurements — the intake sheet fills itself
- Ervina pumping roughly what he drinks, with ~10 frozen portions banked behind it
- A morning pack + evening wash routine you've both actually done three times, with real timings
