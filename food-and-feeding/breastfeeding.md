## 1. How it works — the 5 things to actually remember

1. **Colostrum first (Day 1–~3).** Thick, yellowish, teaspoons per feed — not ounces. That is the design, not "no milk yet": concentrated nutrition + antibodies, matched to a stomach that is marble-sized on Day 1, walnut-sized by Day 3, egg-sized by ~Day 10.
2. **Mature milk "comes in" around Day 2–4.** Breasts feel fuller, heavier, sometimes hard and hot (see engorgement, §5). Swallowing becomes audible around then.
3. **Supply = demand.** Milk is made when milk is removed. Frequent feeds in the first weeks are placing the order for later supply — long gaps early on can quietly lower it.
4. **8–12 feeds / 24 hours is normal**, every 2–3 hours including at night, 10–40 minutes each. **Cluster feeding** (near-constant feeding for hours, usually evening) is normal and builds supply — it is not by itself a sign of low supply.
5. **Feed on early cues, not crying.** Rooting, hands to mouth, restlessness = green light. Crying is a late cue and makes latching harder.

## 3. A good latch

- Mouth **wide open**, lips flanged out (fish lips, not tucked in), chin pressed into the breast, nose free or just touching.
- **More areola visible above his mouth than below** — it's an asymmetric, deep latch, not nipple-only.
- Sound: rhythmic **suck–swallow–breathe**. Clicking/smacking = losing suction = shallow latch.
- Feel: firm tugging is fine. **Pain past the first ~30 seconds, or through the whole feed, is a latch problem, not a rite of passage.** Break suction with a clean finger in the corner of his mouth and re-latch — never pull him off directly.

**Positions — rotate them, especially with a perineal stitch:**

- **Cross-cradle** — most control of his head/neck; best for practising latch in the early weeks.
- **Side-lying** — both on your sides, tummy to tummy. Often the most comfortable with a sore perineum and for night feeds — only on a firm, flat bed, no pillows/blankets near him, and only if Ervina is awake enough to stay aware. If sleep is likely, feed sitting up.
- **Laid-back / semi-reclined** — gravity helps a deep latch and takes pressure off a sore bottom.
- **Cradle** and **football/clutch** (baby tucked under the arm) — useful variety; football keeps weight off the belly.
- Sitting on the stitch hurts for many in Week 1: a cushion, perching slightly to one side, or side-lying for more feeds is normal — mention worsening perineal pain at the postnatal check, don't just endure it.

## 4. Is he getting enough? — diapers are the gauge

Count in 24 hours. This table matters more than how long a feed lasted or how full a breast feels.

| Age | Wet diapers (minimum) | Dirty diapers (minimum) | Stool colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | 1 | 1 | Black, tarry (meconium) |
| Day 2 | 2 | 2 | Black → dark green |
| Day 3 | 3 | 3 | Green-brown, transitioning |
| Day 4 | 4–5 | 3 | Turning yellow |
| Day 5 onwards | **6+ pale wet** | **3+** | Mustard-yellow, seedy |

**Also reassuring:** audible swallowing, content/sleepy after most feeds, steady weight gain after the initial dip.

**Weight:** losing up to ~7% of birth weight in the first days is common; **>10% loss, still losing at Day 5, or not back to 3,590 g by ~2 weeks = call the paediatrician / lactation consultant promptly.** Don't wait for the next scheduled visit.

**The full numbers are in [`../health/weight-and-growth.md`](../health/weight-and-growth.md)** — his expected track pre-filled from 3,590 g, the thresholds that need a call, how to weigh at home (and why you shouldn't weigh daily), how to tell intake without a bottle, and the ml/kg volume maths. **Reminder of the four things that are *not* evidence of low supply:** how often he cries, how long he feeds, how soft your breasts feel, and **how much you pump**.

**Call promptly if:** far fewer diapers than the table by Day 4–5, baby too sleepy to wake for feeds, jaundice spreading / deepening yellow with poor feeding, no stool in 24h in the first days, or persistent vomiting (projectile, green/bile, or blood — that last group is urgent).

## 5. Common problems — fix early, don't push through

| Problem | What it looks like | What helps first | Escalate when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Engorgement** (Day 2–4) | Hard, hot, painful breasts; nipples flatten so he can't latch | Feed frequently, don't skip. Warm compress / warm shower *before* a feed; cold compress *between* feeds. Hand-express or pump just enough to soften the areola so he can latch | Not easing in 24–48h, or redness + fever appears |
| **Sore / cracked nipples** | Tenderness early is common; cracks/blisters/bleeding are not | Fix the latch first (§3) — cream on a bad latch doesn't work. Between feeds: a smear of her own milk air-dried, or lanolin; change damp breast pads. No "toughening" | Pain through whole feeds, cracks worsening, or Ervina dreads feeds → lactation consultant |
| **Blocked duct** | A tender lump / wedge that doesn't clear after a feed | Keep feeding on that side, gentle warmth before feeds, vary positions so different ducts drain, rest. Don't stop feeding on that side | Lump + fever / flu feeling / spreading redness → mastitis pathway below |
| **Mastitis** | Red, hot, painful wedge + **fever, chills, flu-like ache** | — | **Same day: GP / doctor.** Usually needs prompt treatment (often antibiotics). **Keep feeding / expressing unless told otherwise** — stopping makes it worse |
| **Worries about low supply** | Constant feeding alone ≠ low supply — check §4 first (diapers + weight are the evidence) | More frequent, effective removal (feeds + check latch); rest, food, fluids for Ervina | Poor weight gain or low diaper counts → lactation consultant / PD before adding regular top-ups, so top-ups don't quietly cut supply further |
| **Oversupply / forceful let-down** | Milk sprays or drips fast; he pulls off coughing, splutters, clamps down; very gassy and arching after feeds; green watery/frothy stools; engorgement that doesn't settle; recurring blocked ducts | Laid-back / semi-reclined feeding so gravity slows the flow; feed **one side per 2–3 hour block** so the breast empties before switching; **don't pump extra** (pumping "to empty" tells the body to make more); hand-express a little if the let-down is overwhelming him | Recurring blocked ducts or mastitis, weight well above the curve, or it isn't improving in a week → lactation consultant. **Don't** cut feeds or switch to formula to "reset" |
| **Sleepy baby / won't latch** | Misses cues, falls asleep minutes into feeds | Skin-to-skin, undress him for feeds, nappy change mid-feed, express colostrum and feed by spoon/syringe if he won't latch in the first 24h | Repeatedly unable to wake/feed, or jaundice + sleepiness → PD promptly |
| **Possible tongue-tie** | Persistent shallow latch, clicking, nipple damage despite good positioning, poor transfer | Ask for an oral check — TMC lactation / PD | Don't self-diagnose; a check is quick and settles it |

## 6. Expressing, pumping & storage

**When it's useful:** relieving engorgement, building a small stash for Eric's night shift or an outing, separation, and later, returning to work. Hand expression is worth learning early (ask TMC lactation to demo) — it's the most reliable tool for colostrum and for softening an engorged breast.

**Storage (CDC — for a healthy, full-term baby):**

| Milk | Room temp | Fridge (≤4 °C, back, not door) | Freezer (≤−18 °C) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Freshly expressed | Up to **4 hours** — only at ≤25 °C | Up to **4 days** | Best within **6 months**, up to 12 acceptable |
| Thawed (was frozen) | 1–2 hours | Up to **24 hours** from fully thawed | **Never refreeze** |
| Leftover from a feed | Use within **2 hours** of him finishing, then discard | — | — |

- **Singapore note:** a non-air-conditioned HDB room is often >25 °C — treat the 4-hour room window as a ceiling, not a target. Fridge it as soon as you reasonably can.
- Store in small portions (60–120 ml), label with date + time, leave headroom (milk expands when frozen). Cool fresh milk in the fridge before adding it to already-chilled milk; date the batch by the oldest milk.
- Thaw in the fridge overnight or under warm running water. **Never microwave** (hot spots scald; heat damages protective components). Swirl, don't shake. 
- Wash hands before expressing/handling; inspect and clean pump parts each use, discard mouldy tubing.

**Bottles of expressed milk:** use paced feeding — baby semi-upright, bottle near-horizontal so he works for the milk, pauses, let him stop when done. It prevents overfeeding and a fast-flow bottle preference if you're mixing breast and bottle.

## 7. Eric's job — the support role is the job

If Ervina feeds, she does the feeding and Eric runs everything else. Concretely:

1. **Water + a snack at the feeding spot before every feed** — don't make her ask.
2. **Pillows / positioning / footstool**; watch her shoulders and back.
3. **Burp, change, and resettle him after feeds** — especially at night, so her awake stretch per feed stays as short as possible (bring him to her, take him after).
4. **Log feeds + diapers** (side, time, duration, wet/dirty) — sleep-deprived memory doesn't count, and the PD will ask. Simple notebook/app by the feeding spot is enough.
5. **Protect her rest and run the house** in the first weeks: confinement cooking, laundry, visitors (short, not during feeds unless she wants company, nobody with a cold holds him).
6. **Be the one who calls for help** — lactation, PD, GP — when §4/§5 says to. She shouldn't have to research her own rescue at 3 a.m.

Mixed feeding is covered in the source chapter and is not a failure state: if you use formula at any point, follow the safe-prep steps there exactly (water ≥70 °C, exact scoops, use within 2 hours, never dilute) — [pregnancy Ch. 6](https://github.com/eric-and-ervina/pregnancy/blob/main/course/06-feeding-your-baby.md).
