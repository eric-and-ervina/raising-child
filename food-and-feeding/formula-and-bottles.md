# Formula & Bottle Feeding — The Safe-Prep Manual

> **For:** Eric + Ervina. Eldric, born Mon 26 Oct 2026 (TMC, 3,590 g). This file exists because the repo's feeding note is breastfeeding-first, and the reality of the first weeks is usually *some* bottle — expressed milk, a top-up, or formula.
> **Why it gets its own file:** formula prep is the one newborn task where a small slip (too little water, a heaped scoop, a bottle left out) is a genuine risk, and it happens at 3 a.m. when nobody wants to read a tin. The rest of the feeding picture — supply, latch, diapers, storage — is in [`breastfeeding.md`](breastfeeding.md).
> *General information, not medical advice. Your PD and the tin's own instructions override anything here — and read the tin every time, because scoop sizes differ between brands.*

---

## 1. First, the guilt check

- **A bottle is not a failure.** Mixed feeding, topping up, and exclusive formula are all normal ways to feed a baby. What matters is that he is fed, gaining, and his parents are not falling apart.
- **Do not stop breastfeeding abruptly to "switch".** If you are moving toward more formula, drop breastfeeds gradually (over days to weeks) or Ervina risks engorgement, blocked ducts, and mastitis — see [`breastfeeding.md`](breastfeeding.md) §5. Ask the TMC lactation consultant or PD for a wind-down plan.
- **Top-ups can quietly shrink supply.** Milk is made on demand, so a regular bottle that replaces a feed tells the body to make less. If the goal is to keep breastfeeding, feed at the breast first and top up after — not instead. If it is not the goal, that is a legitimate decision and nobody needs to justify it.
- **Decide with evidence, not fear.** "Am I making enough?" is answered by diapers and weight ([`breastfeeding.md`](breastfeeding.md) §4), not by how full a breast feels or how often he cries.

## 2. Safe preparation — the eight rules that actually matter

Powdered formula is **not sterile**. That is the whole reason for the rules below.

1. **Boil the water and let it cool to no less than 70 °C.** Boil a kettle, then let it stand for **no more than ~30 minutes** (about 70–80 °C). This temperature kills *Cronobacter*, the bacterium that occasionally causes serious infection in newborns. **Never** mix powder with lukewarm or room-temperature water.
2. **Water first, then powder.** Put the measured water in the bottle, then add the scoops. Doing it the other way round gives the wrong concentration.
3. **Level scoops, from the scoop that came in that tin.** Loosely fill, then level off against the tin's edge with the flat side of the scoop's back — never a heaped or packed scoop. Different brands have different scoop sizes, so never mix scoops between tins.
4. **Never dilute and never concentrate.** Extra water ("to stretch it" or "for hydration") can cause dangerous electrolyte imbalance; extra powder causes dehydration and constipation. The ratio on the tin is the ratio.
5. **Swirl, don't shake.** Roll the bottle between your palms, or stir with the scoop handle. Shaking whips in air, which means more wind and more spit-up.
6. **Cool it fast, then test it.** Stand the bottle in a bowl of cold or running water. Test a few drops on the inside of your wrist — it should feel warm, not hot. **Never microwave** (hot spots scald even when the bottle feels cool).
7. **Use it within 2 hours of making it** — and **within 1 hour of him starting the feed**. Whatever he doesn't finish in that window goes down the sink. Do not refrigerate and re-offer a part-drunk bottle: saliva plus warm milk is a bacterial culture.
8. **Make each bottle fresh.** Made-up bottles can be kept in the fridge for up to 24 hours if they were prepared safely and never fed from, but in practice the simplest safe rule for two tired parents is: **make one, feed one, wash one.**

## 3. How much — and how to stop forcing the last 10 ml

Newborn stomachs are tiny, which is why "only 30 ml" at Day 2 is normal and not starvation.

| Age | Approximate per feed | Feeds / 24h | Stomach size (for perspective) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | 5–15 ml | 8–12+ | Cherry |
| Day 2 | 15–30 ml | 8–12 | — |
| Day 3–4 | 30–60 ml | 8–12 | Walnut |
| Day 5–7 | 45–60 ml | 8–12 | Apricot |
| Week 2–4 | 60–90 ml | 6–8 | — |
| Month 1–2 | 90–120 ml | 5–6 | Egg |

- From roughly **2 weeks**, a common working rule is **~150 ml per kg per day**, split across feeds, with the PD adjusting for his growth curve. He was 3,590 g, so ~540 ml/day is the ballpark early on — but **the PD's number for him beats any table here.**
- **Hunger cues, not the clock:** rooting, hands to mouth, stirring, turning the head. Crying is late. (Same cues as [`../health/newborn-care.md`](../health/newborn-care.md).)
- **Stop cues:** turning away, splaying hands, milk dribbling out, going limp, falling asleep. **Do not chase the last 10 ml** — overfeeding a newborn means vomit, tummy ache, and a miserable night for everyone.
- **He will not starve himself.** A baby who is gaining, wetting 6+ nappies a day from Day 5, and reasonably settled is being fed enough.

## 4. Bottles, teats and sterilising

- **Wash immediately** after a feed: rinse, then hot soapy water with a bottle brush (teats inside and out, and the teat's rim). Milk residue in a warm climate is exactly what you don't want sitting until morning.
- **Sterilise every bottle and teat** for at least the first months (manufacturers say until 12 months). Steam steriliser, microwave steam bags, or boiling — the prep list already has this scored 8/10.
- **Air-dry on a clean rack**, not a tea towel. Assemble only when you need it; a sealed, dry bottle stays clean.
- **Teat flow:** newborn/slow-flow. If milk pours out of his mouth or he gulps and splutters, the teat is too fast — size up only when he's consistently frustrated and finishing feeds fast.
- **Replace teats** that are sticky, torn, or stretched, and bottles that are scratched or cloudy.

## 5. Paced bottle feeding — the one technique to learn

This is how a bottle-fed baby avoids overfeeding and how a breastfed baby avoids preferring the bottle's easy flow.

1. **Hold him semi-upright**, head and neck supported, facing you — not flat on his back with the bottle above him.
2. **Keep the bottle nearly horizontal**, just enough tilt that the teat stays full of milk. Gravity should not be doing the feeding.
3. **Let him draw the teat in** rather than pushing it into his mouth.
4. **Pause every 20–30 sucks** — tip the bottle down or gently remove it, let him breathe.
5. **Swap sides** halfway through, like a breastfeed.
6. **Finish in ~15–20 minutes.** A bottle that disappears in 5 minutes is usually too fast.
7. **Burp** upright over your shoulder or sitting on your lap, supporting the jaw — a few minutes, not a mission.

**Never prop a bottle.** It is a choking risk, an ear-infection risk, and it removes the one thing a feed is for at this age — being held.

## 6. Mixing breast and bottle without wrecking supply

- **Breast first, bottle after** whenever the aim is to protect supply. The breast gets the order; the bottle fills the gap.
- **One bottle a day, early**, if Ervina plans to return to work at ~12 weeks: introducing a bottle around **weeks 3–4** (once breastfeeding is established) makes the eventual handover easier than introducing it in week 11.
- **Someone else gives the bottle** where possible — Eric, or a helper. Babies learn fast that a bottle means a different person, which is exactly what you want at 2 a.m.
- **Every bottle she replaces, she pumps** if the aim is to keep supply matched to demand. Not forever — just while the pattern is being established.
- **"Nipple confusion" is really flow preference.** Slow teats, paced feeding, and not using a bottle to settle every cry are the fixes.
- **Expressed milk storage and handling** is in [`breastfeeding.md`](breastfeeding.md) §6 — CDC windows, the Singapore room-temperature caveat, and never refreezing.

## 7. Ready-to-feed (liquid) formula — when it's worth the money

Ready-to-feed is **sterile**, needs no mixing, and is the safest option when you are exhausted, travelling, or in the first days when nobody has slept. It costs more per feed and takes fridge space; that is the entire trade-off. Keep **a few cartons** for the 3 a.m. disaster night and for travel to Indonesia. Once opened, follow the carton: refrigerate, and use within the stated time (usually 24 hours).

## 8. Never do this

- ❌ **Water as a drink** under 6 months — formula is made with water; that is enough. Extra water can dilute his sodium.
- ❌ **Honey** before 12 months — infant botulism risk, and it hides in "natural" cough remedies.
- ❌ **Cereal, rice, or anything else in the bottle** — choking risk, no benefit, and it does not help sleep.
- ❌ **Cow's milk, goat's milk, soy milk, or plant milks as the main drink** before 12 months.
- ❌ **Home-made formula** or "fortifying" a tin with extra powder.
- ❌ **Microwaving** bottles, or reheating a feed twice.
- ❌ **Propping the bottle**, or leaving him to feed unattended.
- ❌ **Adding sugar, condensed milk, or flavouring** — this is how a sweet tooth and a bad feeding relationship start.
- ❌ **Stockpiling one brand in bulk** before you know he tolerates it.

## 9. The station, and who does what

Set it up once, at the feeding spot, so neither of you is hunting at 3 a.m.:

- Sterilised bottles (2 ready), the tin with its own scoop, a thermos of boiled water cooled to ~70 °C **or** a kettle you can boil in 30 seconds, a jug for cooling, a bottle brush, and a small bin.
- **Eric's job at night:** make the bottle, bring it, take the baby after, burp, change, resettle. Ervina's job is to feed and go back to sleep — the same division as [`breastfeeding.md`](breastfeeding.md) §7.
- **Log every feed** (time, amount, breast/bottle) for the first weeks. The PD will ask, and sleep-deprived memory is not evidence.

## 10. When to call someone

**Same day (PD or clinic):**
- Vomiting after most feeds, especially **forceful/projectile**, or green/bile-stained.
- **Refusing feeds** or taking far less for 2+ feeds, or too sleepy to wake and feed.
- **Blood or mucus** in the stool, or persistent watery diarrhoea.
- **Fewer wet nappies** than the day-by-day table ([`breastfeeding.md`](breastfeeding.md) §4), sunken soft spot, dry mouth.
- **Hard, pellet-like stools** with real distress (formula-fed babies often have firmer stools; that alone is not constipation).
- **Rash, swelling, or blood in the stool after a feed** that looks like an allergic reaction — stop that formula and call.
- **Spitting up** that is green, bloody, or comes with arching and screaming after every feed.

**Now — 995 or A&E** for any sign in [`../health/red-flags-and-emergencies.md`](../health/red-flags-and-emergencies.md): fever ≥38 °C under 3 months, breathing trouble, blue/grey lips, limpness, seizures.

## 12. The infant-care transition program (agreed Fri 13 Nov 2026)

**Why:** feeding is almost entirely direct breastfeeding. The evening bottle (since 10 Nov) is a 30 ml practice add-on, not a feed — so Eldric has never taken a *full* feed from a bottle, and nobody knows his per-feed volume. Infant care (from ~2 months, early Jan) is bottles only, supplied by us. This program closes both gaps gradually, so the first care week contains no new feeding skill. It extends §6, it does not replace it.

**Rules throughout:** paced feeding (§5), slowest-flow teat (do not size up to finish faster), full-wake rule before a feed, burp after, Eric gives the bottles. A refused feed is data, not a battle — stop, breastfeed, retry next session, change one variable (timing, temperature, teat, place). Direct breastfeeding stays the majority until Phase 3.

### Phase 1 — Measure (Sat 14 Nov → Mon 30 Nov; weeks 3–5)
- Upgrade the evening practice bottle to **one full bottle feed per day** (formula is fine — the skill is the bottle, not the contents).
- **Offer 90 ml.** He takes what he takes; the leftover is the measurement. Log offered / taken / minutes / time until the next hunger cue.
- After ~7 logged feeds, his real per-feed number replaces all table estimates. Cross-check at the 26 Nov PD visit: ~150 ml/kg/day ÷ his weight.
- Ervina hand-expresses or pumps briefly at the replaced feed where practical, so the replaced feed doesn't quietly cut supply.

### Phase 2 — Rhythm + pumping (Tue 1 Dec → Mon 21 Dec; weeks 5–8)
- **Two full bottle feeds per day** at care-like times (e.g. ~10:00 and ~14:30), the rest direct.
- Ervina **pumps to replace each bottle feed** and logs her per-session output — that is her measurement, and it needs 1–2 weeks of practice to become representative. A low first-week pump number is normal and is *not* evidence of low supply ([`breastfeeding.md`](breastfeeding.md) §4).
- **The pump must be bought by the start of this phase** — the S$150 company newborn-gift decision closes in November, not December.
- Volumes step up with him: expect **90–120 ml per feed** by the end of this phase. Prepare each bottle at his measured number + ~15 ml headroom.
- Freeze one extra ~120 ml portion per day when output allows. **Target: ~10 frozen portions** (≈2 care days + the centre's emergency stash) by late December.
- Vary the context: bottle in the living room, after a walk — not only the evening feeding spot. The skill should belong to the bottle, not to one chair.

### Phase 3 — Dress rehearsal (Tue 22 Dec → first care day; weeks 8–10)
- **Three consecutive simulation days** before the centre start: a full care-day pattern at home — the care day is **12 hours (07:00–19:00)**, so bottles at ~09:00 / ~12:00 / ~15:00 / ~18:00, packed in the cooler bag in the morning, labelled (name, date/time expressed), fed from the packed bottles.
- Ervina runs a work-day pump schedule those days; Eric runs the morning pack (5 bottles — 4 feeds + 1 spare, ice packs, nappies, spare clothes) and **times it**, so the January morning routine is measured, not guessed.
- 2-month vaccinations in this window, ideally just before the first care day.
- The centre's own ramp-up (short days in week 1) sits on top of this — by then, bottles are routine and only the place is new.

### Done looks like
- A full bottle finished in ~15–20 minutes without a battle, 3 days running.
- Nappy counts on bottle-heavy days stay in the normal band (6+ wet, 3+ dirty) — the intake evidence.
- Ervina's pumped output per replaced feed ≈ his intake per feed (±~20 ml) — supply matched to the January demand.
- A written per-feed volume and daily total ready for the centre's intake sheet, from the log — not from memory.

### If it stalls
Refusal at any phase: drop back one phase for 3–4 days, change exactly one variable, retry. Persistent refusal, poor nappy counts, or weight concerns → PD (26 Nov visit is the scheduled checkpoint; sooner if the gauges move).

### Bottle stock and sizes for infant care (added 13 Nov 2026)

The centre does **not** wash or sterilise — every feed travels in its own pre-filled bottle, and all bottles come home dirty at night. So bottle count = feeds per day + spare, with two rotating sets at home (one set out / in the wash, one clean and ready).

| Age | Bottles sent / day | Per-feed volume | Bottle size to send |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2–4 months | 5 (4 feeds + 1 spare) | ~120 ml | 150 ml |
| 4–6 months | 5 (4 feeds + 1 spare) | ~150–180 ml | 260 ml |
| 6+ months (solids started) | usually 4 (3 feeds + 1 spare) | ~180 ml | 260 ml |

- **Home stock: 10 × 150 ml now** (two sets of 5). Seven is the floor (5 + 2) but leaves little margin for breakage or a missed evening wash. Add **5 × 260 ml** around month 4–5, when a feed outgrows the 150 ml bottle.
- The **spare** travels filled. If it comes home unused, still refrigerated and never warmed or fed from, it becomes tomorrow's first bottle (inside the 4-day EBM fridge window, §6 of [`breastfeeding.md`](breastfeeding.md)). An expressed-milk spare rotates like this far more easily than a made-up formula one (24 h fridge limit, §2).
- **Teats:** one per bottle plus spares; slow flow throughout the bottle program. Replace teats ~every 2 months, or sooner if sticky, torn or stretched (§4).
- Pump-side bottles and storage bags are separate kit — don't count them in the feeding-bottle stock.
