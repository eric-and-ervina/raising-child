# Baby Preparation Shopping List — First Baby Boy, 689 sqft HDB, Work-Pass Family

**Who this is for:** a Chinese-Indonesian couple in Singapore expecting their first child (boy) in about **11 weeks** (mum is in week 29 — EDD around **early December 2026**). Both parents are **Indonesian citizens here on work passes: dad holds an Employment Pass, mum an S Pass**. They are about to rent a **689 sqft, partially furnished HDB flat** — intentionally under-furnished so it doesn't feel cramped.

**What this document does:** lists what to buy (and what to skip), each item scored **0–10** for importance, with a **Buy / Buy later / Borrow / Skip** verdict, indicative prices in SGD, and Singapore-specific notes — plus a **paperwork track with legal deadlines (§10)** and a realistic cost picture for a foreign family (§11).

> ⚠️ **Read this first.** Because both parents are work-pass holders, your baby will **not** be a Singapore citizen. That single fact removes most of the standard "what Singapore gives you" advice and adds a deadline most checklists never mention:
>
> - **No** Baby Bonus cash gift, CDA grant/matching, Child Credits, or PSEA top-up.
> - **No** MediSave Maternity Package, no GST/CDC vouchers, no hospital or polyclinic subsidies, no subsidised childhood vaccines, no childcare subsidies.
> - Mum gets **12 weeks** maternity leave (8 paid, 4 unpaid) instead of 16; **dad has no statutory paternity or shared parental leave**.
> - In exchange you get a **hard immigration clock: the baby is only lawfully in Singapore for 42 days from birth** until they hold a passport and a Dependant's Pass (§10).
>
> None of that is a disaster — it just means **budget more, expect no subsidy, and treat the paperwork as a project with a deadline.** It also means the "buy less" half of this list matters more than it does for a citizen family.

> ⚠️ **Second reality check:** a newborn needs, for the first weeks, only a safe place to sleep, milk (and the gear to feed it), diapers, clothes, and a way to leave the house. Everything else can wait until you've met your baby and know your routine. In a small flat, **space is your scarcest resource — buy only for the next 2–3 months at a time.**

## 9. For mum (postpartum) — and the leave reality

> 🛂 **Two things to sort out with HR this week, not in December:**
> **1. Mum's leave.** With a non-citizen child, the Employment Act gives her **up to 12 weeks of maternity leave**: the **first 8 weeks paid by the employer** (if she has ≥3 months' continuous service), the **last 4 weeks unpaid** unless her contract says otherwise. The 16-week Government-Paid Maternity Leave applies only when the child is a Singapore citizen.
> **2. Dad's leave.** There is **no statutory paternity leave** for a non-citizen child — no 4-week GPPL, no Shared Parental Leave. His time off is whatever his contract or company policy provides. With no grandparents in town, negotiate now: annual leave, unpaid leave, or flexible work for those first two weeks.
>
> *(One silver lining: if the child becomes a Singapore citizen **within 12 months of birth**, the Government-Paid Leave schemes can apply — see §11. Realistically that only happens if your family gets PR/citizenship, which is a multi-year process.)*

## 10. 🛂 Immigration & paperwork: the 42-day plan

**This is the page to keep.** The baby born in Singapore to work-pass parents is **deemed to hold a Special Pass valid for 42 days from the date of birth**. That is the whole game: within those 42 days you must register the birth, get an Indonesian passport, and get a Dependant's Pass. **Any unauthorised stay beyond 42 days is an offence under the Immigration Act.**

| # | Step | Deadline | Who does it | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Register the birth** | Do it within **14 days** (registration fee $18; the general legal window is 42 days) | Either parent, via ICA / LifeSG birth registration | Bring both parents' passports, your **marriage certificate** (a certified English translation if it's in Indonesian), and the hospital's birth notification. You'll receive the **digital birth certificate** and an **ICA advisory note** — that note is what grants the baby the 42-day Special Pass. |
| 2 | **Report the birth to KBRI Singapore** | As soon as you have the SG birth certificate | Parents, via Portal Peduli WNI | Gives the Indonesian birth registration/akta lahir. ~2 working days if documents are complete. |
| 3 | **Apply for the baby's Indonesian passport** | Must be in hand before the DP is issued | KBRI Singapore | This step usually eats the calendar, so book the appointment the week you register the birth. Minors' applications need both parents' documents, and both parents are usually required to attend. |
| 4 | **Apply for the Dependant's Pass** | Immediately after step 1 — aim well inside 42 days | **Your employer** applies to MOM via the work pass portal | Sponsor must be an **EP or S Pass holder with a fixed monthly salary ≥ $6,000** (your salary alone, not household income). Processing is ~3 weeks but can be expedited for newborns. If the passport isn't ready yet, upload proof of the passport application — but the passport must be issued before the pass itself can be issued. |
| 5 | **CDA vaccination verification** | With the DP application (and renewed later) | You | Foreign-born children **12 and under** applying for a DP/LTVP must submit the **"Verification of Vaccination Requirements (for entry to Singapore)"** document issued by the Communicable Diseases Agency (covers **diphtheria and measles**). For a baby it is issued with a **short validity (3 months if the child is ≤6 months old)** and you renew it as the vaccination schedule progresses — so get a doctor to endorse the immunisation registration form and keep topping it up. |
| 6 | **If the passport is late** (Special Pass expiring within 7 days) | Before expiry | You, at ICA | Request an **extension of the Special Pass** — **the baby must be present**. MOM also has an online form to request an extension of a newborn's Special Pass. Do this as soon as you see it, not on day 42. |
| 7 | **If no DP is possible** (neither parent earns $6,000) | Before 42 days | You, at ICA Visitor Services Centre (Level 4) with the child | You must extend the Special Pass / apply for a visit pass in person with the baby. Plan this route **now**, because it is the version that goes wrong. |

**Also worth knowing**

- **If mum stops working**, her S Pass ends with the job. She can move onto a **Dependant's Pass** sponsored by dad (same $6,000 rule), and she can work again only if an employer obtains MOM's **Letter of Consent** — so time any resignation deliberately.
- **Keep a folder (digital + printed):** birth certificate, ICA advisory note, passport, DP card, CDA vaccination verification, insurance policy, employee benefits summary. You'll need subsets of it repeatedly.
- **Renewal traps:** the DP depends on the sponsoring parent continuing to meet the salary bar and on the child's passport validity. From **1 January 2027, EP minimum qualifying salary rises to $6,000 and S Pass to $3,600** ($4,000 for financial services) for new applications, with renewals following — check with HR that your pass (and therefore the baby's DP) stays clean.
- **Longer term:** if you apply for **PR as a family**, the child can be included in the application, and PR is the stepping stone to the benefits in §11. Registering with KBRI now keeps the Indonesian side (passport, documents, schooling if you ever return) intact — dual-nationality rules for children are their own topic; sort that out later, not in the first month.

## 11. 💰 Money: what you pay, what you don't get, and what changes later

**You will NOT receive** (because the child is not a Singapore citizen):

- Baby Bonus Cash Gift / the new SG Child Support Package, CDA First Step Grant and co-matching, annual Child Credits, PSEA top-up.
- MediSave Maternity Package (Medisave exists only for citizens/PRs), GST and CDC vouchers.
- Subsidised public-hospital and polyclinic rates, subsidised childhood vaccinations, ECDA infant-care/childcare subsidies (basic subsidy is for citizen children).
- Government-Paid Maternity Leave (16 weeks), Paternity Leave (4 weeks) and Shared Parental Leave (10 weeks for babies born from 1 April 2026) — all require a Singapore-citizen child.

**You DO get:**

- **12 weeks of maternity leave** under the Employment Act (8 weeks paid by the employer if she has ≥3 months' service; last 4 weeks unpaid unless the contract says otherwise).
- Whatever **both employers' medical/benefit plans** provide. Read both policies properly — dependant and newborn cover is where the money is; ask HR whether the baby can be added at birth, and how to claim the delivery.
- The full set of citizen benefits **if your child becomes a Singapore citizen** — MOM's Government-Paid Leave schemes allow it if the child becomes a citizen within 12 months of birth, and the CDA can be opened for a child who becomes a citizen before age 12. Realistically this follows PR → citizenship, i.e. years. Verify the current SG Child Support Package rules with MSF when you get there.

**Practical money moves**

1. **Get the hospital's written non-resident estimate and deposit figure.** This is the single biggest bill of the year and it is due regardless of what gear you skip.
2. **Ask both employers about dependant cover** before you shop for insurance — some group plans cover newborns from birth, others exclude maternity entirely.
3. **Don't try to buy maternity insurance now** — she's in week 29, and maternity benefits carry long waiting periods. Spend that energy on newborn cover and an emergency fund instead.
4. **Hold a cash buffer of about $20,000–25,000** for the delivery, paperwork, insurance and a job-change cushion. Work-pass life means your income can change faster than a citizen's, and the baby's DP depends on that salary bar.
5. **A live-in helper is legal for EP/S Pass holders** and is the standard answer to "two working parents, no family nearby" — but she needs acceptable accommodation, so decide honestly whether 689 sqft can give her a real sleeping space before you commit.

## 12. Space-saving rules for 689 sqft

1. **Room-share** — baby sleeps in your bedroom for 6–12 months. No nursery needed.
2. **Buy multi-purpose:** muslins do 5 jobs; a backpack is the diaper bag; a floor mat is the changing table.
3. **Collapsible > fixed:** collapsible bathtub, foldable stroller, stackable bins.
4. **Buy for the next 2–3 months only** — no clothing stockpiles, no high chair in month 1.
5. **Second-hand is your friend:** Carousell and SG parent Telegram groups for strollers, bouncers, clothes, toys. **Always buy new: mattress, car seat (crash history unknown), and anything electric with a safety recall.**
6. **Declutter as you go** — sell/give away outgrown items on Carousell; a small flat punishes hoarding.
7. **Storage trick:** under-bed boxes + ceiling-high wall shelves + door-mounted organisers turn dead space into a baby wardrobe.
8. **Plan the helper's corner before hiring** — a live-in helper (or confinement nanny for the month) has to sleep somewhere, and that somewhere is measured in this flat's square feet.

## 13. Do-not-buy list (quick reference)

- ❌ **Baby walker** — not banned in Singapore (Canada bans them), but it must meet EN 1273/ASTM F977 and is still a real injury risk with no benefit. Skip.
- ❌ **Bumper pads, pillows, loose blankets** — SIDS risk.
- ❌ **Sterilised baby water** — tap water is safe.
- ❌ **Cord blood banking** (unless medically advised).
- ❌ **Baby food maker / full weaning kit** — not needed until 6 months.
- ❌ **Multiple sizes of clothes up front** — buy as baby grows.
- ❌ **NB diapers in bulk** — outgrown in ~3–4 weeks.
- ❌ **Dedicated nursery furniture** — no space, no need.
- ❌ **Overnight-monitor socks / wearables** — anxiety machines.
- ❌ **Maternity insurance purchased in month 7** — waiting periods make it useless now.
- ❌ **Trying to "buy peace of mind" with gear instead of paperwork** — the 42-day clock, the passport and the DP are what can actually go wrong.
