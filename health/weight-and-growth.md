# Weight & Growth — Is He Getting Enough? (And Can He Get Too Much?)

> **For:** Eric + Ervina. Eldric, born Mon 26 Oct 2026 at **3,590 g**, 52 cm, head 35 cm. He was 3.59 kg at birth, which is average-to-slightly-above average for a term boy — a good starting point, not a buffer to spend.
> **Why this file exists:** "am I giving him too little, or too much?" is the question that ruins the first month, and the repo only answered it in fragments (a diaper table here, a 150 ml/kg line there, an empty weight-log table). This is the whole answer in one place: **the numbers to expect, how to measure at home without driving yourself mad, the difference between a real weight problem and a normal one, and what "too much milk" actually looks like.**

## 0. The 30-second answer

**"Is he getting enough?" is answered by three things, in this order — and nothing else.**

| Rank | Gauge | What "enough" looks like | How reliable |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Weight trend** | Loses ≤7% by Day 3–5, back to **3,590 g by Day 10–14**, then gains ~**20–30 g/day** in the first month | The only real evidence |
| **2** | **Nappies** | Day 5+: **6+ wet** and **3+ dirty** in 24 hours, pale urine, mustard-yellow seedy stool | Very good, free, and available at 3 a.m. |
| **3** | **Behaviour** | Content or sleepy after most feeds, wakes for feeds, has alert windows, strong cry, normal tone and colour | Supporting evidence only |

**Things that are NOT evidence** (all of these are normal in a well-fed baby, and all of them cause unnecessary formula top-ups):

- How often he cries, or how long he feeds.
- How full or soft your breasts feel.
- **How much you pump.** Pump output is a measure of the pump and your let-down, not your supply. Many women with plenty of milk pump 30 ml.
- Whether he wakes at night. **Waking is normal and protective at this age.**
- Cluster feeding in the evening. It's how supply is built, not proof of starvation.
- His weight going down in the first 3 days. **Every baby does this.**

**And "too much"?** At the breast, he regulates it himself — a breastfed newborn essentially cannot be overfed, because he controls the flow and stops. **Overfeeding is a bottle problem**, and it's real: signs in §5.

## 2. Weighing at home — how to do it without losing your mind

**First, the honest answer: you do not need a home scale in week 1.** The PD weighs him at Day 3–5 and again at 1 month, and those numbers are the ones that matter. But if the anxiety is the problem — and with the first baby it usually is — a home scale is cheaper than another week of 3 a.m. spirals. **Buy one, use it once a week, on a fixed day.**

### 2.1 The setup that gives usable numbers

1. **Same scale, same place, same time** — e.g. Sunday morning, before the first feed.
2. **Naked, or in a dry nappy only.** A wet nappy is 50–100 g; a grow suit is 100 g. Clothed weights are noise.
3. **Zero the scale with the towel on it** (or use the tare function), then put him down centred.
4. **Wait for him to be still.** A wriggling baby reads 30 g high on some scales.
5. **Write it down with the date and time.** No memory, no app you'll abandon — the log in §7.

### 2.2 What to buy

| Option | Accuracy | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Digital baby scale, 10 kg capacity, 5 g resolution** | ±5 g | ✅ **The right tool.** ~SGD 40–80. Lasts 2+ years, and is the single most anxiety-reducing purchase in this file |
| **Kitchen scale, 5 kg, 1 g resolution + a plastic basin** | ±1–2 g | ✅ Cheap and works for the first ~4–6 weeks (he's 3.6–4.5 kg). Zero the basin. Best-value option, but outgrown by ~2 months |
| **Bathroom scale, weigh yourself holding him, subtract** | ±100–200 g | ❌ Useless for a newborn. His whole weekly gain is 150 g. It will produce random numbers and 3 a.m. panic |
| **Smart sock / wearable monitor** | — | ❌ **Skip.** It measures nothing about weight, and false alarms are the documented problem ([`../preparation/baby-preparation-shopping-list.md`](../preparation/baby-preparation-shopping-list.md) §8) |

**One useful trick:** **1 ml of milk ≈ 1 g.** So a before-and-after feed weighing on a 5 g-resolution scale is a rough measure of how much he took at that feed (that's exactly what a lactation consultant's "weighted feed" does, with a better scale). It is not reliable for a single feed at home — but 3 feeds averaged tells you a lot. **Do it with the LC, not obsessively at 2 a.m.**

### 2.3 The two rules of home weighing

- **Once a week. Never daily.** His weight swings 30–80 g on a feed and a poo. Daily weighing turns normal noise into a fake emergency and makes parents top up unnecessarily.
- **A single reading is not a trend.** Look at **three readings over two weeks**, or the difference between this Sunday and last Sunday. If the direction is up, you're fine.

## 3. "How much is he actually getting?" — breastfeeding without a bottle

You cannot see the milk, so you use four indirect measures. Together they are as good as a number.

| Measure | What to look for | What it does **not** mean |
|---|---|---|
| **Swallowing** | Rhythmic **suck–suck–swallow–breathe**, audible gulps, a pause at the start of let-down. Roughly 10–20 minutes of active feeding per side is typical, but **efficiency beats duration** | A 45-minute feed is not "more"; a 10-minute feed in a 4-week-old can be plenty |
| **Nappies** | Day 5+: **6+ pale wet, 3+ dirty**, mustard-yellow and seedy. Pale urine = hydrated | A dry spell of 2 hours is not dehydration |
| **Behaviour after** | Milk-drunk: drowsy, relaxed hands, content, or settled for a while | Crying 40 minutes later is often wind or overtired, not hunger |
| **Weight trend** | §1 | — |

**Signs he is actually feeding well:** a wide latch, a visible "pull" on the areola, his ear and temple moving with the suck, audible swallowing, and your breast feeling softer afterwards (not necessarily empty).

**Signs transfer is poor — and worth a lactation call, not a panic:**
- Clicking, smacking, or losing suction repeatedly.
- Falling asleep 3 minutes in, every feed, and never waking to a full feed.
- Nipple pain through the whole feed, or creased/flattened nipples afterwards (a shallow latch signature).
- Feeds of 60+ minutes every time, with him still hungry.
- **Fewer than 6 wet nappies from Day 5, or urine that looks dark/orange ("brick dust").**
- Not back to birth weight by Day 14.

That cluster is a **lactation consultant appointment** — TMC ParentCraft **6251 4090**, or BMSG **6339 3558** (9am–9pm). Ask specifically for a **weighted feed**: they weigh him, you feed, they weigh again. It turns "I think he's not getting enough" into a number, in one visit.

## 4. The volume maths — enough to answer "is this bottle right?"

Use this only for bottles (expressed milk or formula). A breastfed baby at the breast needs no arithmetic.

**The rule the PDs use:** from about **Day 5–7**, roughly **150 ml per kg per day**, rising to ~**180–200 ml/kg/day** by 3–4 months, split across feeds. (Some PDs use 150 throughout — the tin's instructions and the PD's number beat this table.)

| His age | Approx weight | Daily total (150–180 ml/kg) | Per feed at 8 feeds | Per feed at 6 feeds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Day 5** | 3.4 kg | 500–610 ml | 60–75 ml | 85–100 ml |
| **Week 2** | 3.6 kg | 540–650 ml | 65–80 ml | 90–110 ml |
| **Week 4** | 4.5 kg | 675–810 ml | 85–100 ml | 110–135 ml |
| **Week 8** | 5.4 kg | 810–970 ml | 100–120 ml | 135–160 ml |
| **Week 12** | 6.3 kg | 945–1,130 ml | 115–140 ml | 155–190 ml |

**How to use it:**

- A **feed** should land near the per-feed number; a **day** near the daily number. Both drift.
- **Feeds get bigger and less frequent** as he grows — 8–12 feeds at week 1, 6–8 by month 2. Don't stretch gaps to hit a volume, and don't push volume to stretch gaps.
- **The number is a ceiling to check against, not a target to hit.** If he stops at 80 ml when the table says 100, he is right and the table is wrong. **Never chase the last 10 ml** ([`../food-and-feeding/formula-and-bottles.md`](../food-and-feeding/formula-and-bottles.md) §3).
- **Never** add extra water to "stretch" a feed, or extra powder to "make it count" — both are genuinely dangerous (electrolyte imbalance / dehydration).

## 5. "Am I giving him too much?" — what overfeeding looks like

### 5.1 With a bottle (the real risk)

Fast bottles let a baby swallow past the point where his own "full" signal fires, because sucking is a reflex at this age — a teat in the mouth keeps triggering it.

| Sign | What it means | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Large vomits after most feeds** (not a small curd-like posset) | Volume overload | Slow the feed; smaller, more frequent bottles |
| **Gulping down 90 ml in 5 minutes** | Teat too fast, or bottle too tilted | Slow-flow teat, bottle near-horizontal, **paced feeding** ([`formula-and-bottles.md`](../food-and-feeding/formula-and-bottles.md) §5) |
| **Screaming and pulling away mid-feed, then wanting more immediately** | Swallowed air, not hunger | Pause, burp, upright 10 minutes, then offer again *slowly* |
| **Distended, hard belly; squirming; green/frothy stools** | Gut overload | Slow down; mention at the PD visit |
| **Weight climbing far above the curve** (e.g. >1.3 kg in the first month with the above) | Genuine overfeeding | Reduce volumes with the PD's guidance — not by skipping feeds |
| **Frequent spit-up + arching + crying after feeds** | Could be reflux, made worse by volume | PD — this is a medical question, not a portion question |

**Paced feeding is the whole fix:** semi-upright, near-horizontal bottle, let him draw the teat in, pause every 20–30 sucks, swap sides halfway, finish in **15–20 minutes**, stop at the first stop-cue (turning away, splayed hands, milk dribbling out, going limp).

### 5.2 At the breast ("too much milk" is really *oversupply*)

A breastfed baby self-regulates volume. What goes wrong instead is **oversupply / forceful let-down**, which is about *speed and foremilk*, not quantity:

| Sign | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Forceful let-down** | Milk sprays or drips fast; he pulls off, coughs, splutters, clamps down, or clicks |
| **Green, watery, or frothy stools** | Often frequent and explosive |
| **Very gassy, uncomfortable, arching after feeds** | Lots of air from coping with the flow |
| **Rapid weight gain well above the curve** + engorgement that never settles | The supply side of the same picture |
| **Blocked ducts / mastitis recurring** | Oversupply that isn't draining evenly |

**What helps (with the LC, not alone):** laid-back/semi-reclined feeding so gravity slows the flow; feeding on one side per 2–3 hour block ("block feeding") so the breast is drained before switching; **not pumping extra** (pumping to "empty" tells the body to make more); hand-expressing a little if the let-down is overwhelming him.

**What does not help and can hurt:** cutting feeds, watering down anything, skipping sides for days without guidance, or switching to formula to "reset". Call the LC — oversupply is common, fixable, and easy to make worse by improvising.

### 5.3 The distinction that matters at 3 a.m.

**Spitting up is not vomiting, and neither is overfeeding proof.**

- **Posset/spit-up:** a tablespoon or two, curd-like, no effort, baby unbothered, happens after feeds and when burped. **Normal, no action.**
- **Vomit:** larger volume, forceful, sometimes projectile, baby may cry or arch. **Mention at the PD visit.**
- **Vomit that is green, bloody, or after every single feed:** **call now** ([`red-flags-and-emergencies.md`](red-flags-and-emergencies.md)).

## 6. "Am I giving him too little?" — the ranked red flags

Work down this list. The top two are the ones that get a same-day call.

| Priority | Sign | What it means | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Weight below 3,231 g (>10% loss)**, or still falling at Day 5, or not back to 3,590 g by Day 14 | Not enough intake getting in | **Call the PD today.** Same-day weigh |
| **2** | **Fewer than 4 wet nappies in 24 h after Day 4**, sunken soft spot, no tears, dry mouth, dark urine | Dehydration | **Call now / go in** ([`red-flags-and-emergencies.md`](red-flags-and-emergencies.md)) |
| **3** | **Too sleepy to wake and feed** — misses feeds, can't be roused, feeds 3 minutes and is out | Illness, jaundice, or a transfer problem | **Same-day PD** |
| **4** | **Under 6 wet or under 3 dirty nappies a day from Day 5** | Borderline intake | Call the LC today; weigh within 48 h |
| **5** | Refusing 2 feeds in a row | Illness until proven otherwise | Same-day PD |
| **6** | **Jaundice deepening or spreading to the legs**, with poor feeding | Bilirubin rising | Same-day PD |
| **7** | Feeding 60+ minutes every time and still hungry, with clicking or pain | Poor milk transfer | LC appointment this week |
| **8** | Feeds fewer than 8 in 24 h with long gaps (4 h+) in the first 2 weeks | Supply will quietly drop | Wake him; call the LC |
| **9** | Weight gaining <20 g/day over a week after Day 5 | Slow, not always wrong | PD visit with the log |
| **10** | You *feel* it's not working, and nothing here is abnormal | Anxiety is a real symptom, but not a diagnosis | Show the LC this file and the log — one visit settles it |

**The 48-hour rescue plan** (when the PD or LC says intake is low — do this with them, never instead of them):

1. **Feed every 2 hours in daylight, every 3 at night** — set an alarm rather than waiting.
2. **Wake him properly:** change the nappy first, undress to the vest, skin-to-skin, cool cloth on the back of the neck, express a few drops onto his lips.
3. **Breast first, always.** If a top-up is advised, give it **after** the breast, by paced bottle or spoon, and **pump to replace it** if supply is the goal ([`../food-and-feeding/formula-and-bottles.md`](../food-and-feeding/formula-and-bottles.md) §6).
4. **Re-weigh within 24–48 hours** on the same scale.
5. **Write down every feed and nappy** — the next clinician will need it.

## 8. Growth charts, in one paragraph

A growth chart plots his weight against thousands of other boys the same age. **The percentile is his position, not his score.** Around the 60th at birth means 40% of boys his age are heavier and 60% are lighter — that is average-to-above, and it is fine.

**Two patterns are normal:**
- **Following a line** — staying near the same percentile for months. This is what you want.
- **Shifting up or down one channel** in the first weeks as feeding settles. Common, especially after the initial dip.

**One pattern is a question for the PD:**
- **Crossing two or more channels downward** (e.g. 60th → 25th), or a **flat line for 2+ weeks**. That's a signal to look for a cause, not a verdict.

And the one thing the chart cannot see: **a baby who is happy, alert, and wetting nappies is doing well even if he's on the 10th percentile.** Small babies are usually small parents' babies.

## 9. The answer to the original question, in four lines

1. **Too little** is measured by **weight trend + nappies**, not by crying, feeding frequency, breast feel, or pump output. If those two are fine, he is getting enough — whatever it feels like at 3 a.m.
2. **Too much** is a **bottle** problem, not a breast problem. Paced feeding, slow teats, and never chasing the last 10 ml prevent it. At the breast, "too much" is oversupply — an LC problem, not a volume problem.
3. **He was 3.59 kg at birth and should be back to 3.59 kg by Day 10–14, then gain ~25 g a day.** That's the whole early scoreboard.
4. **Weigh weekly at most, on the same scale, and let the PD's chart own the verdict.** A single number is not a trend, and a trend is not a grade.

## 10. Sources and honesty notes

- **Weight-loss thresholds:** the 7% / 10% bands and the "back to birth weight by 10–14 days" checkpoint reflect standard neonatal practice (AAP *Newborn Feeding* guidance, UNICEF Baby Friendly standards, and Singapore PD practice). Local PDs vary slightly — theirs wins.
- **Volume formula:** ~150 ml/kg/day after the first week, rising with age, is the standard clinical rule of thumb for both formula and expressed milk. Breastfed intake is roughly the same and self-regulated.
- **Nappy counts:** the day-by-day table is in [`../food-and-feeding/breastfeeding.md`](../food-and-feeding/breastfeeding.md) §4 and is the standard Baby Friendly/CDC version.
- **Growth standards:** the expected ranges here are built on **WHO Child Growth Standards** (boys, weight-for-age) and rounded to practical ranges. They are not a substitute for Eldric's own plotted chart.
- **Oversupply:** the block-feeding and laid-back approaches are standard lactation-consultant practice; they are *not* self-diagnosis material — get the LC to look at a feed.
- **This is general information.** It cannot examine Eldric. If the numbers and your instinct disagree, the PD settles it — and one visit is cheaper than a week of guessing.
