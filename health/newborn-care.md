## The one rule for holding him

**Always support his head and neck** — he can't hold his head up for weeks. Pick up: one hand under head/neck first, other under his bottom, then lift. Put down: bottom first, head last, slide hands out.

- **Cradle** — head in the crook of your elbow, forearm along his back.
- **Shoulder / football hold** — chest to your chest, one hand behind head/neck, one under bottom. Best for burping and calming, least tiring for you.
- **Lap hold** — tummy-down across your forearm, head to the side. Calming only, while you're awake holding him. **Never a sleep position.**

You'll feel clumsy the first dozen times. He won't break. Gentle and confident beats hesitant.

## Diapering — step by step (8–12 a day at first)

1. Wash/sanitise hands. Lay him on a flat surface — **never leave him unattended on a raised surface, not for a second.**
2. Undo the dirty diaper; use its front to wipe the bulk off first.
3. Lift legs gently by the ankles (or a hand under his bottom). **Wipe front to back**, fresh wipe each pass.
4. Pat dry — most rash is just wetness sitting too long.
5. Clean diaper under him, back edge ~belly-button level, front up between legs, tabs snug (one finger fits under), leg cuffs flared *out*.
6. **Fold the front down below the cord stump** until it falls off.
7. Wash hands again.

**Boys:** clean around the foreskin, never retract it. Keep a cloth over his front mid-change — timing is legendary.

**What's coming out, in order:** Days 1–3 black tarry **meconium** → Days 3–5 green-brown transitional → Day 5+ yellow, seedy **milk stools**. Counts are your feeding gauge — the day-by-day table is in [`../food-and-feeding/breastfeeding.md`](../food-and-feeding/breastfeeding.md) §4.

**Rash:** change promptly, pat dry, 10–15 min air-dry on a towel when you can, thin zinc-oxide barrier at first redness. Bright-red, blistered, or spreading despite a day of barrier care → mention at the PD visit (sooner if it looks infected).

## Cord stump

Falls off in **1–3 weeks**. Keep it clean and dry — that's the whole job. Diaper folded below it. If dirty, dab with clean water, pat dry. No alcohol unless the PD says so. Don't pull it, even hanging by a thread. A few drops of dried blood when it drops is normal. **Red/swollen skin around it, pus, foul smell, or more than spot bleeding → call the PD** (also in the red-flags file).

## Bathing

**You decided: no bath until after 24 hours from birth** — first bath from the evening of Tue 27 Oct at the earliest. Until the cord stump falls off: **sponge baths only**, 2–3× a week is plenty.

- Warm room, everything laid out first. Keep him towel-wrapped, uncover one part at a time — newborns lose heat fast.
- Plain warm water for the face (separate cotton ball per eye, inner corner out). Mild fragrance-free soap for the body; neck folds, armpits, behind ears, diaper area last. Pat dry, especially folds.
- **After the stump falls:** tub with 2–3 inches of water at ~37–38 °C (elbow/wrist test: warm, not hot). One hand on him at all times. **Never leave him in water unattended — drowning is silent and fast; if you forgot the towel, take him with you or wrap him and carry him.**
- Daily baths aren't required and dry the skin. Short and warm beats long.

## Swaddling, dressing, nails

- **Swaddle:** snug around the chest, **loose around the hips** (legs must bend up and out — pinning legs straight is linked to hip dysplasia). **Stop at the first signs of rolling (~2–4 months)** and move to a sleep sack.
- **Dressing:** one more layer than you is the guide. Back of neck warm and dry = right; sweaty = too much; cool = add one. No hats indoors once home — overheating is a risk factor, not a comfort.
- **Nails:** soft but sharp — he'll scratch his face. **File, don't cut**, in the early weeks (soft emery board), while he sleeps or after a feed. Mittens are a stopgap only; he needs his hands free.

## Safe sleep — the non-negotiables

- **Back to sleep, every sleep.** Naps and nights, no exceptions.
- **Bare cot:** firm flat mattress, fitted sheet, sleep sack. No pillows, blankets, toys, bumpers, or positioners.
- **Room-share, don't bed-share:** in your room 6–12 months (already your setup). Never sleep with him on a sofa or armchair — if you're so tired you might drop off holding him, put him in the cot first. **A crying baby in a cot is safe; a sleeping baby in your arms on a sofa is not.**
- Room comfortable for a lightly dressed adult; check his neck for sweat.
- Smoke-free, always. Visitors who smoke: hands washed, shirt changed, before holding him.
- Tell grandparents these rules plainly when they help — older advice was different, and "you survived" is not a safety argument.

## Soothing — when he's fed, dry, and still crying: the 5 S's

1. **Swaddle** (if not yet rolling) 2. **Side/stomach hold** — in your arms, awake only; never placed that way to sleep 3. **Shush** — loud continuous white noise, as loud as his crying 4. **Swing** — small rhythmic jiggle, head supported ("shivering", not rocking) 5. **Suck** — clean finger; pacifier timing: if breastfeeding is being established, most lactation advice says wait ~3–4 weeks — confirm with TMC/PD.

Cycle and combine them. Walk the corridor. Some evenings nothing works — crying peaks ~6–8 weeks and it is miserable and normal.

**When you're fraying:** hand him over (tag-team in ~20-min blocks — grandparents on a shift count double), or **put him on his back in the cot, walk out, breathe, drink water, come back.** That's the correct procedure, not neglect. **Never shake him** — shaking can kill. Put down + walk away, every time the impulse appears.

**Feeding cues — catch them early:** crying is the *last* cue. Early: stirring, mouth opening, rooting, hands to mouth → feed now, calm baby. Mid: stretching, restlessness, hand-sucking. Late: crying, agitated, red — feed immediately, settling takes longer.

## What's normal (don't panic list)

- **Sleep 14–17h/day** in 2–4h fragments, no day/night sense for weeks.
- **Cluster feeding** in the evenings; **startle reflex** (arms fling out); constant **hiccups and sneezes** (sneezing clears his nose — not illness); noisy/grunting breathing from narrow airways.
- **Weight:** loses up to ~7–10% of birth weight in the first days, back to 3,590 g by ~2 weeks — PD tracks it.
- **Jaundice Day 2–4:** yellow tinge is very common; frequent feeding helps. Escalate if yellow spreads down the body, he's too sleepy to wake for feeds, or feeds poorly — see red-flags file.
- **Soft spot (fontanelle)** on his head is supposed to be there; gentle touching in baths is fine.

## Temperature

Learn it before you need it: rectal is the most accurate for newborns (ask a nurse to show you); armpit reads low. **≥38.0 °C under 3 months = hospital, immediately** — full thresholds in [`red-flags-and-emergencies.md`](red-flags-and-emergencies.md).
