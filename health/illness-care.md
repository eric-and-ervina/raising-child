# When He's Sick — Home Care That Isn't an Emergency

> **For:** Eric + Ervina. Eldric, born Mon 26 Oct 2026. **Under 3 months, this file is short by design**: most "sick" in a newborn is either nothing, or a same-day call. It covers the in-between — the snuffly nose at 4 a.m., the vomiting that turns out to be possetting, the rash that isn't meningitis — so you know what to *do* while you decide whether to call.
> **This file is NOT the emergency file.** [`red-flags-and-emergencies.md`](red-flags-and-emergencies.md) holds the go-now list and the phone numbers. **If anything there is true, stop reading this and go.**
> **Also not the rules file:** feeding safety is [`../food-and-feeding/formula-and-bottles.md`](../food-and-feeding/formula-and-bottles.md); crying that isn't illness is [`crying-and-soothing.md`](crying-and-soothing.md); the newborn baseline is [`newborn-care.md`](newborn-care.md).
> *General information, not medical advice. Your PD overrides everything here, including the doses.*

## 0. The rule that outranks this entire file

**Under 3 months old, a temperature of ≥38.0 °C (rectal) or ≥37.5 °C (armpit) means hospital, immediately — not a home-care decision.**

That single rule deletes most of the ambiguity in this file. A newborn with a fever is not "a bit unwell"; he is a baby who needs to be examined, because newborn sepsis looks like a sleepy baby with a temperature and nothing else. **No paracetamol-and-see.** No "let's check again in an hour". No waiting for morning.

**And a second, subtler rule:** in the first 8 weeks, **any breathing symptom with poor feeding** is a same-day call even without a fever. A "cold" in a 3-week-old is not the same illness it is in you.

## 1. The 60-second check — every time he seems "off"

Run this before you decide anything. It is the same five signals every time, and it takes under a minute.

| Signal | What normal looks like | What makes it a call |
|---|---|---|
| **Feeding** | Takes most of his usual feeds, has a strong suck | **Takes less than half** of 2 feeds in a row, or won't wake to feed |
| **Breathing** | 40–60 breaths/min at rest, quiet, no effort, no pauses | **>60 at rest**, ribs sucking in, nostrils flaring, grunting, pauses >10 s, blue/grey lips |
| **Colour & tone** | Pink, warm, moves all four limbs, flexed | Pale, mottled, blue; **floppy** or stiff; hard to rouse |
| **Nappies** | 6+ wet, 3+ dirty from Day 5 | **<4 wet in 24 h**, dry mouth, no tears, sunken soft spot |
| **Behaviour** | Wakes for feeds, has alert windows, cries normally | **Inconsolable high-pitched cry**, or the opposite — too quiet, whimpering, not himself |

**Any right-column item → [`red-flags-and-emergencies.md`](red-flags-and-emergencies.md), and call.** All five normal → he's probably not sick, or he's mildly sick and this file applies.

## 2. What a newborn actually gets (the short list)

You will not be diagnosing rare diseases. In the first three months, it is almost always one of these five:

1. **A cold / blocked nose** (§3) — the most common, and the most disruptive to sleep and feeding.
2. **A tummy thing** — possetting, wind, a day of loose stools (§4).
3. **A skin, eye, or mouth thing** — rash, sticky eye, thrush (§5).
4. **A vaccination reaction** (§7).
5. **A fever** (§6) — which, at this age, is a hospital rule, not a home-care problem.

And the one that masquerades as all of them: **hunger, wind, tiredness, or being too hot.** Check those first ([`crying-and-soothing.md`](crying-and-soothing.md) §4).

## 3. The cold — and why a 3-week-old is different

**What it looks like:** snuffly breathing, noisy nose, mild cough, feeding in short bursts then pulling off to breathe, worse at night and when lying flat, mild irritability. No fever (or a fever, in which case §0).

**In the first 8 weeks, "a cold" is a call to the PD — same day — even if he looks fine.** Not because it's dangerous in itself, but because the things that *start* like a cold (bronchiolitis, RSV, pertussis) are the ones that go badly in a newborn, and the window to notice is short. After ~3 months, a cold can be managed at home with the plan below.

### 3.1 The home routine that actually helps

| Step | How | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Saline first** | 1–2 drops of sterile saline in each nostril, wait 30–60 s | Thins and loosens the mucus so it can come out |
| **Then suction** | Bulb syringe or a mouth-suction aspirator, tip at the nostril opening only, **before feeds and before sleep** | He cannot breathe and suck at the same time — a clear nose is a feeding intervention |
| **Small, frequent feeds** | Half-feeds every 1.5–2 h rather than full feeds every 3 h | A blocked nose limits how long he can suck; total intake matters more than feed size |
| **Upright for 20–30 min after feeds, awake** | On your shoulder | Drainage, and less spit-up |
| **Humidify** | A cool-mist humidifier in the room, **cleaned daily**; or the bathroom with a hot shower running for 10 minutes, then sit in it | Dry air makes crusts and cracks. Never leave him alone in a steamy bathroom |
| **Keep the cot flat** | Flat mattress, no pillow, no propped mattress, no incline | Inclined sleep is a suffocation risk. "Helps the cold" is not worth it |
| **Extra feeds and cuddles** | Skin-to-skin, more holding | Comfort is treatment at this age |

### 3.2 What you must not do

- ❌ **Cough and cold medicines** — decongestants, antihistamines, "infant" syrups. **Nothing for a cold under 2 years** (and effectively under 6). They don't work, and they have caused infant deaths.
- ❌ **Vicks / camphor / menthol / eucalyptus / "wind oil"** on the chest, feet, or in the cot. Camphor and menthol can cause seizures and breathing problems in infants. This is the commonest well-meant mistake in a Chinese-Indonesian household — say it early, kindly, and once.
- ❌ **Essential oil diffusers**, scented plug-ins, or anything scented near his face.
- ❌ **Antibiotics** "just in case" — a cold is viral; antibiotics do nothing and cause harm.
- ❌ **Honey** — under 12 months, botulism risk. It hides in "natural" cough remedies.
- ❌ **Aspirin** — Reye's syndrome. Never.
- ❌ **Saline or water into the ears**, or cotton buds in the nose.
- ❌ **Suctioning more than a few times a day** — it irritates the lining and makes more mucus.

**Go up a level (A&E / 995) if:** breathing gets fast or laboured, he stops feeding, he goes floppy or hard to wake, there are pauses in breathing, or his lips look blue or grey.

## 4. The tummy — possetting, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation

**Possetting is not vomiting.** Get this distinction right and you'll save yourself a dozen panics.

| | **Posset / spit-up** | **Vomit** | **What to do** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Volume** | A teaspoon to a tablespoon | Larger, sometimes a whole feed | Posset: burp better, upright 20 min, smaller feeds. Nothing else |
| **Effort** | None — it just appears | Heaving, retching, sometimes projectile | Vomit once with a happy baby: watch. Vomit after most feeds: **call the PD** |
| **Colour** | Milky / curd-like | **Green, yellow-green, or blood-streaked = urgent** | Green/blood → **A&E now** (possible obstruction) |
| **Afterwards** | Unbothered, wants more | May cry, arch, be subdued | Persisting + arching + poor gain → reflux pathway ([`crying-and-soothing.md`](crying-and-soothing.md) §9.1) |

**Diarrhoea** in a newborn is a **same-day call** — not because of the stool, but because dehydration in a 4 kg baby happens fast. Watery, frequent, green, or mucusy, especially with a change in feeding or a rash.

**Constipation** is *not* "hasn't pooed today". A **breastfed** newborn can go several days between soft stools and be perfectly well. Constipation is **hard, dry, pellet-like stool with real distress** — and if that happens, call the PD rather than treating it. **Never** a suppository, enema, glycerine stick, or rectal stimulation without the PD's instruction.

**Dehydration check** (do this whenever he's vomiting or has diarrhoea):
- **Wet nappies** — fewer than 4 in 24 h.
- **Mouth** — dry, sticky, no saliva.
- **Tears** — none when crying.
- **Soft spot** — sunken.
- **Urine** — dark, or a faint orange "brick dust" stain.
- **Behaviour** — drowsy, floppy, hard to wake.

**Two or more of those → hospital, not home.**

## 5. Skin, eyes, and mouth — the things that need a call but not sirens

| What | What it probably is | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Bright red rash with satellite spots in the nappy area**, or in the mouth as white patches that don't wipe off | **Thrush** | **Same-day PD** — he needs treatment, and Ervina's nipples too if breastfeeding, or you'll ping-pong it |
| **Sticky, watery eye with redness or swelling of the lids** | Conjunctivitis (bacterial or viral) | **Same-day PD.** (A watery eye *without* redness is usually a blocked tear duct — see [`newborn-care.md`](newborn-care.md)) |
| **Spreading redness around a spot, pus, an angry ring, or a mark that's hot and tender** | Bacterial skin infection | **Same-day PD**, sooner if it's spreading fast or he's feverish |
| **Cord stump with redness spreading onto the belly, pus, or a foul smell** | Omphalitis | **Go now** — this is a red flag, not a "mention it" |
| **Cradle cap** (greasy yellow scales on the scalp) | Seborrhoeic dermatitis — harmless | Gentle washing, a little baby oil, soft brush. Not contagious. Mention if it spreads or weeps |
| **Baby acne, milia, erythema toxicum, Mongolian spots, stork bites** | All normal newborn skin | Nothing. The full table is in [`newborn-care.md`](newborn-care.md) |
| **Sudden crop of spots *with* a fever or a very unwell baby** | Anything from a viral rash to meningitis | **995 / A&E now** |
| **A blister or white patch in the mouth that isn't thrush** | Sucking blister from a latch | Check the latch; PD if it's stopping feeds |

**The standing rule for skin:** if a doctor didn't recommend it, don't put it on him. No powders, oils, herbs, coins, bindings, or traditional applications — especially not on the cord or the nappy area.

## 6. Fever — the only section where you don't get a choice

- **How to take it:** rectal is the number doctors trust; armpit reads low. Technique in [`care-techniques.md`](care-techniques.md) §8. Wait 20 minutes after a bath or after unwrapping him.
- **The thresholds at his age:** **≥38.0 °C rectal, or ≥37.5 °C armpit, under 3 months → hospital now.** Between 3 and 6 months, ≥38.5 °C is a same-day call; above 6 months, ≥39 °C or any fever lasting 3+ days is a call.
- **Do not** treat the number at home to see if it comes down. **Do not** sponge him with cold water or ice, and don't bundle him to "sweat it out" — both cause harm.
- **Do** undress him to a single light layer, keep the room 24–26 °C, and offer feeds more often.
- **Paracetamol** is not a way to avoid the hospital at this age. If the PD has given you a dose for him, use that dose — never an estimate.
- **A fever that responds to paracetamol is still a fever.** Response to medicine is not reassurance in a newborn.
- **A normal temperature does not rule out serious illness.** If he is floppy, grey, breathing oddly, or not feeding — go, whatever the thermometer says.

## 7. Vaccination day (the 1-month Hep B dose onward)

**Normal for 24–48 hours:** a sore, red, or slightly swollen thigh; mild irritability; a low-grade temperature; sleepier or fussier than usual; feeding a bit less.

**What helps:** more feeds, more holding, skin-to-skin, a cool (not cold) cloth on the site if it's hot, and letting him sleep. **Do not** rub or massage the injection site.

**What still applies:** a fever of **≥38.0 °C under 3 months is a hospital rule** — a recent vaccination does not change it. Paracetamol only at the PD's dose, and only if he's uncomfortable.

**Call the PD** if the redness spreads beyond a large area around the site, there's a hard lump that's growing after 48 h, he's inconsolable for hours, or he refuses feeds.

## 8. Medicines — what he can and cannot have

| Medicine | At his age |
|---|---|
| **Paracetamol** | Only at the PD's dose for his weight (typically 10–15 mg/kg per dose, max 4 doses/24 h). The PD's number is the number |
| **Ibuprofen** | **Not under 3 months** (or under ~5 kg) without the PD. Never for dehydration or vomiting |
| **Antibiotics** | Only prescribed, and finish the course. **Never** leftover or shared |
| **Cough/cold/decongestant syrups** | **No.** Not under 2 years — and no evidence of benefit under 6 |
| **Antihistamines** | No, unless the PD prescribes for a specific reason |
| **Teething gels with benzocaine/lidocaine** | No. (And he's not teething yet — that's usually 4–7 months) |
| **Gripe water, colic drops, "wind" remedies** | No, without the PD ([`crying-and-soothing.md`](crying-and-soothing.md) §8.2) |
| **Honey, herbal syrups, jamu, "just a taste"** | **No.** Honey under 12 months is a botulism risk; herbals are unregulated |
| **Aspirin** | Never, at any age in childhood |
| **Vitamin D drops** | Yes, if the PD recommended them — that's the one thing that belongs in the daily routine |

**Dosing mechanics:** use the syringe that came with the medicine (never a teaspoon), measure at eye level, squirt into the inside of the cheek slowly, and **write down the time and dose** — two tired parents double-dosing is the classic error ([`care-techniques.md`](care-techniques.md) §9).

## 9. The sick day, hour by hour

When he's unwell, only four things matter. Everything else can wait.

1. **Feeds in — small and often.** Never dilute formula, never stretch a feed with water, never withhold because he vomited. If he's too congested to feed, saline + suction first, then feed.
2. **Output out — count it.** Wet and dirty nappies per 24 h, written down. This is your early-warning system for dehydration, and it's the first thing any doctor asks.
3. **Temperature and breathing — twice a day, and any time he feels hot or looks odd.** Time, method, number, and what else was going on.
4. **Comfort — hold him.** Skin-to-skin regulates his temperature and heart rate, and it is not "spoiling a sick baby"; it is the treatment. Sleep will be worse; expect it, and take shifts ([`night-and-sleep.md`](night-and-sleep.md)).

**Do not:** take him to a crowded clinic waiting room if a house call or a phone consult is possible; stop breastfeeding (it's the best medicine he has — antibodies and fluid in one); give anything by mouth that isn't milk or a prescribed medicine.

## 11. The escalation ladder (memorise the shape, not the detail)

```
HE SEEMS OFF
│
├─ Run the 60-second check (§1)
│     └─ ANY red-flag item → red-flags file → 995 / A&E
│
├─ TEMPERATURE: ≥38.0 °C rectal or ≥37.5 °C armpit, under 3 months
│     └─ HOSPITAL NOW. This is the whole rule.
│
├─ No fever, but: won't feed, breathing changed, floppy, or a rash
│     └─ Call the PD now / A&E if it's worsening
│
├─ No fever, feeding OK, and it's a cold, a posset, a rash, or a jab reaction
│     └─ This file (§3–§7). Watch sheet on. Call the PD TODAY anyway
│        if he's under 8 weeks — a newborn with a cold is a call, not a wait
│
└─ All five signals normal, and it's wind, tiredness, or being too hot
      └─ Not illness. Back to the sweep in crying-and-soothing.md §4.
```

**The rule that beats every tree:** if your instinct says something is wrong, call. A first-time parent's unease is a real signal, and no doctor is annoyed by it.

## 12. Prevention, in order of how much it actually matters

1. **Hands.** Every adult, every time, before touching him. Nothing else comes close ([`newborn-care.md`](newborn-care.md)).
2. **Sick people stay away** — including grandparents, including "just a sniffle". A cold in a 5-week-old can mean an A&E trip; the same cold in an adult is nothing.
3. **No kissing his face or hands**, and no passing him around a gathering.
4. **Smoke-free, and no smoke on clothes.** Third-hand smoke counts.
5. **Caregivers vaccinated** — flu, and Tdap/whooping cough for anyone who will hold him regularly. Ask their GP.
6. **Avoid crowded enclosed places** in the first weeks. A pram walk or a sling keeps strangers' hands off him.
7. **Don't overheat him.** A hot, overdressed baby is a baby who cries and looks unwell; a neck that's sweaty means take a layer off.

## 13. Sources and honesty notes

- **Fever thresholds:** the ≥38.0 °C-under-3-months rule is standard neonatal practice (AAP *Fever in the Young Infant*, NICE *Fever in under 5s*, and Singapore PD practice). Local practice varies slightly on the exact armpit cut-off — the repo uses the conservative one, and the PD's instruction wins.
- **Cold and cough medicines:** the "nothing under 2" line reflects FDA/AAP safety advice after infant deaths; it is a safety rule, not a preference.
- **Camphor/menthol:** the seizure and respiratory risk in infants is documented in case reports and poison-centre data — the blanket "no" is deliberate.
- **Possitting vs reflux vs vomiting:** the volume/effort/colour framing is standard paediatric triage.
- **Dehydration signs** are the WHO/standard paediatric set.
- **This is general information.** It cannot examine Eldric, and the tables here are triage tools, not diagnoses. When this file and your PD disagree, the PD wins; when this file and your instinct disagree at 3 a.m., call.
