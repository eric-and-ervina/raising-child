# Red Flags & Emergencies — Read Once, Then Keep One Tap Away

> **Read this once now, bookmark it on your phone, and put the numbers box on the fridge.** In a real emergency you won't be calm enough to search.
> Consolidates the `pregnancy` repo's [Course Ch. 10 — Emergencies](https://github.com/eric-and-ervina/pregnancy/blob/main/course/10-emergencies.md). *Education, not medical advice. When in doubt, call — nobody at an emergency department judges a first-time parent for being careful.*

---

## The one threshold to memorise

**Fever ≥38.0 °C in a baby under 3 months = hospital, immediately.** No "wait till morning", no "let's see". Newborns can deteriorate very fast and show it poorly. Rectal temperature is the number doctors trust (armpit reads low).

## Baby — go now (995 or TMC / KKH emergency)

- Fever **≥38.0 °C** under 3 months (above)
- **Breathing difficulty** — fast or noisy breathing, ribs pulling in, nostrils flaring, pauses in breathing
- **Blue or grey** lips, face, or tongue
- **Refusing feeds** — skips two feeds in a row, or too sleepy to wake and feed
- **Dehydration signs** — fewer than 4 wet diapers in 24h after Day 4, sunken soft spot, no tears, very dry mouth
- **Seizures** or repetitive jerky movements
- **Forceful, repeated vomiting** (projectile / green bile / blood is worst — spit-up in small amounts is normal)
- **Bloody stool**
- **Cord stump** — spreading redness, swelling, pus, foul smell
- **Jaundice** spreading to the legs, deepening after Day 4, or paired with poor feeding / hard to wake
- **Bulge at the soft spot**, inconsolable high-pitched crying, or a baby who is limp / unresponsive

Trust your gut over this list. If something feels wrong, go. **If the crying won't stop, run the screen in [`crying-and-soothing.md`](crying-and-soothing.md) §1 before you try to soothe it** — that file covers the cry that is a symptom, the pain hunt, and the go-now/call-today thresholds in one place.

## Ervina — call 995 (don't drive her yourself if she's unstable)

- Soaking a full pad in **an hour**, or passing **large clots** (golf-ball size or bigger)
- **Fainting**, feeling about to faint, can't stay conscious; **seizure**
- **Severe headache with vision changes** (blur, spots, flashing lights)
- **Severe pain under the ribs** (right side especially); **chest pain** or **trouble breathing**
- **One-sided calf pain/swelling** (possible clot — do not massage it)
- **Fever ≥38 °C with foul-smelling discharge**, or a perineal wound that reopens / worsens sharply (her tear was 1 stitch — new severe pain, swelling, or discharge still counts)
- **Thoughts of harming herself or the baby** — call SOS **1-767** or IMH **6389 2222**, now, not tomorrow

## Choking — the sequence

Milk/mucus choking is the realistic case at this age.

1. **Sit down.** Baby **face-down on your forearm**, head lower than chest, your hand supporting jaw/head (don't press the throat).
2. **5 back blows** — heel of hand, firmly, between the shoulder blades.
3. Still blocked: turn him **face-up on the other forearm**, head still lower than chest.
4. **5 chest thrusts** — two fingers, centre of chest just below the nipple line, ~4 cm deep.
5. **Repeat 5-and-5** until it clears or he cries/breathes. **Call 995** as soon as you can — shout for someone else, or speakerphone while you work.
6. **Never** sweep fingers blindly in his mouth — you'll push it deeper.

## Infant CPR — if unresponsive and not breathing normally

1. Firm flat surface, on his back. **Call 995 first** (or have someone else call).
2. **30 compressions:** two fingers, centre of chest, ~4 cm deep, **100–120/min**.
3. **2 breaths:** head tilted slightly back, chin lifted, your mouth covering his nose + mouth, gentle breaths — just enough to see the chest rise.
4. **Repeat 30:2** until help arrives or he responds. Don't stop early.

Reading this is not training. **Book a hands-on infant first-aid/CPR course in the first months** (Singapore Red Cross Child First Aid; hospital ParentCraft classes also run them) and log the date here: _Course: ______ Date: ______ Who attended: _______.
