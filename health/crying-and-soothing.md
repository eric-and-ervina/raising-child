# Crying & Soothing — The Non-Stop Crying Playbook

> **For:** Eric + Ervina, and whoever is holding Eldric (born Mon 26 Oct 2026). Zero experience assumed. **Use at 3 a.m. with one hand.**

## 0. The 60-second version

When the crying starts and your brain stops working, do these **in this order**:

1. **Look at him for 5 seconds.** Breathing? Colour pink? Not limp, not stiff-jerking? No blood, no swelling, no bulge? → if any of those is wrong, **stop everything and go to §1.**
2. **Run the 9-point sweep** (§4): feed → wind → diaper → temperature → stimulation → sleep → contact/suck → pain hunt → illness. Not a guess — in order, out loud.
3. **Start the soothing stack** (§5): swaddle → side hold → shush → swing → suck. Then swap techniques every **3–5 minutes**, not every 5 seconds.
4. **At 20 minutes of no change:** tag-team. Hand him over using the script in §13.3, or put him in the cot and leave the room for 10 minutes.
5. **At 90+ minutes, or if anything in §1.1 or §11 is true:** call the PD / go in. That is not weakness; it's the protocol.

**The one line that outranks everything:** a crying baby on his back in an empty cot is safe. **Never shake him. Ever.** Put him down and walk away — that is the correct procedure, not neglect.

## 1. First: is this a medical cry? (the checks that stop the clock)

Do this **before** any soothing technique. Soothing a sick baby wastes the time you don't have.

### 1.1 Go now — 995 / nearest A&E (no waiting, no calling around)

| Sign | Why it can't wait |
|---|---|
| **Fever ≥38.0 °C under 3 months** (rectal is the trusted number; **≥37.5 °C armpit** counts as a call at this age) | Newborn sepsis looks like "just crying" until it doesn't |
| **Breathing:** fast, grunting, ribs pulling in, nostrils flaring, pauses >10 s, blue/grey lips or tongue | Oxygen problem, not a mood |
| **Inconsolable high-pitched cry** (see §11.1) — especially with a **bulging soft spot**, arched back, or a stiff body | Meningitis / raised pressure |
| **Limp, floppy, unresponsive, or hard to wake** | Same |
| **Seizure** — rhythmic jerking, eye deviation, smacking lips, cycling limbs | Same |
| **Refusing 2 feeds in a row**, or too sleepy to wake and feed | Dehydration and illness in one sign |
| **Forceful/projectile vomiting**, green bile, or blood | Obstruction |
| **Bloody stool**, or a **firm, swollen, red scrotum** | Gut injury; testicular torsion (surgical clock) |
| **A new bulge in the groin or at the navel that is hard and tender** | Incarcerated hernia (surgical clock) |
| **Any fall, drop, or impact** — even from the bed, even if he "seemed fine" | Injuries and bleeds don't always show immediately |
| **Crying after a known injury** to the head, or a **swollen limb** he won't move | Fracture / bleed |
| **A button battery or magnet** anywhere near him, or missing | Chemical burn in minutes — A&E now, don't wait for symptoms |

### 1.2 Call the PD today (same day, not tomorrow)

| Sign | Why |
|---|---|
| Crying **>3 hours total in a day**, for **3+ days in a week**, and getting worse — especially with feeding or weight worries | Meets the colic definition, and needs the treatable causes excluded (§8–§10) |
| **Weak, whimpering, or "different" cry** that doesn't sound like him | Illness, pain, or exhaustion |
| **Crying that stops being consolable even briefly** — no calm window at all in 2+ hours | Pain until proven otherwise |
| **Vomiting after most feeds**, arching, back-arching with feeds, poor weight gain | Reflux vs obstruction vs CMPA (§9) |
| **Fewer wet diapers** (<4/24 h after Day 4), sunken soft spot, no tears, dry mouth | Dehydration |
| **Diarrhoea**, or a change in stool with crying | Infection / CMPA |
| **Rash with the crying**, especially spreading, blistering, or with fever | Infection / allergy |
| **Eye:** red, swollen, pus, or watering with light sensitivity | Corneal abrasion or infection |
| **Ear:** discharge, or crying worse lying flat and better upright | Ear infection (uncommon under 3 months — which is why it's a call) |
| **Cord:** spreading redness, swelling, pus, foul smell | Omphalitis |
| **A cry that only happens with one movement** — e.g. lifting one arm, bending a leg | Injury / clavicle / joint |
| **He seems to be in pain but you cannot find why, and it has gone on >24 h** | That is exactly what the PD is for |

### 1.3 The 30-second physical check (do it even when nothing looks wrong)

Undress him to the diaper in a warm room and **look, then feel, head to toe**:

- **Head/face:** soft spot (flat or slightly sunken is normal; **bulging** is not), scalp for a hair wrapped around, eyes for redness/discharge, nose for blockage.
- **Ears:** discharge, redness, tenderness when you touch the tragus.
- **Mouth:** white patches that **don't** wipe off (thrush), a blister, a tongue-tie that's making feeds painful, anything he could have swallowed.
- **Neck/collarbone:** swelling, a crackle under the skin, or a cry when you lift that arm.
- **Chest/breathing:** count breaths for 60 s while he's calm (normal newborn ~40–60/min).
- **Abdomen:** firm, distended, tender, or a visible bulge.
- **Groin:** a bulge that appears when he cries and disappears when calm (hernia), swelling of the scrotum.
- **Diaper area:** rash, a **hair tourniquet** at the base of the penis, a swollen testicle.
- **Fingers and toes:** a hair or thread wrapped around — look for a red, swollen, or dusky digit. **Every single finger and toe.**
- **Skin folds:** a tag, a thread, a stray elastic, a scratch, a bite.
- **Temperature:** if he feels hot, take it properly ([`care-techniques.md`](care-techniques.md) §8) — hands and forehead are not thermometers.

If the check is clean, the crying is very likely **normal** — and normal crying still needs the full soothing effort.

## 2. What normal crying actually looks like

### 2.1 The numbers

| Age | Typical total crying per 24 h | What's happening |
|---|---|---|
| First 24–48 h | 1–2 h | Often quiet and sleepy; crying is mostly hunger, cold, or the birth itself |
| Days 3–7 | 1.5–2.5 h | **Milk coming in, weight dip, cluster feeds** — the first "he's always hungry" stretch |
| Weeks 2–3 | 2–3 h | Awake windows get longer; evening cluster feeding starts |
| **Weeks 4–8** | **2–3 h average, up to 5 h in the worst cases** | **The peak.** Around 6 weeks for most babies, up to 8 for some |
| Weeks 8–12 | Falling | Crying gets shorter and more "answerable" |
| 3–4 months | 1 h or less | Evening fussiness usually fades; sleep changes arrive instead |

**The shape matters more than the number:** it usually **builds through the late afternoon and evening (5–11 p.m.), peaks in the evening, and settles at night.** That is the "witching hour", and it is a **normal developmental pattern**, not a sign that you're doing it wrong or that he's in pain. See §7 for the protocol.

### 2.2 Why it peaks — the PURPLE explanation (say this out loud to each other)

- **P**eak — crying peaks around **6 weeks**, then declines.
- **U**npredictable — a calm day tells you nothing about tonight.
- **R**esists soothing — some crying continues **no matter what you do**. This is the part that breaks parents.
- **P**ain-like face — screwed-up eyes, red face, arched back. It **looks** like pain and usually isn't.
- **L**ong-lasting — up to **5 hours a day** is within the normal range.
- **E**vening — it clusters in the evening.

**The point of PURPLE:** nothing is broken, nothing is being done wrong, and it ends. It exists so that "it didn't work" stops feeling like a verdict on you.

### 2.3 The two facts to hold onto

1. **Responding to a newborn's crying does not spoil him.** At this age there is no "manipulation" circuit in his brain. You cannot create a bad habit by picking him up. (Same point, longer form: [`../parenting/parenting-guide-raising-a-healthy-grounded-boy.md`](../parenting/parenting-guide-raising-a-healthy-grounded-boy.md) §attachment.)
2. **The danger is not the crying — it's the adult at the end of it.** Infant shaking injuries peak at **6–8 weeks**, exactly when crying peaks. That correlation is the whole reason this file has a §13.

## 3. The cry decoder — what each one usually means

Newborn cries are not a language, but they have patterns. Use this as a **hypothesis list**, not a diagnosis.

| Sounds like | Usually | First move |
|---|---|---|
| Short, low-pitched, rhythmic, escalating; **sucking on hands**, rooting, turning head | **Hunger** (but crying is the *last* cue — see §4.1) | Feed |
| Sudden loud squawk, then fussing, then a pause | **Startle / discomfort** — a wet diaper, a cold wipe, a tag | Check diaper and clothing |
| Fussing, grunting, squirming, knees to chest, red face, then a pause and a softer cry | **Wind / gas / about to poop** | Burp, bicycle legs, tummy massage |
| Continuous, whiny, **eyes closed or rubbing face**, yawning, turning away from your face | **Overtired / overstimulated** | Dark, quiet, swaddle, shush — *less* input, not more |
| High-pitched, sharp, sudden, **arched back**, stiff legs, pulls away from the breast | **Pain** — reflux, ear, injury, hernia; rarely something serious | Stop feeding, do the §1.3 check |
| A **shrill, piercing, "not-him" cry** that you'd describe as a scream | **Medical** until proven otherwise | §1.1 / §11.1 |
| Weak, breathy, whimpering, or a cry that fades | **Illness or exhaustion** — this one is quieter and easier to miss | Temperature, feeding, §1.2 |
| Fussing that stops the moment you pick him up, then restarts when you put him down | **Contact need** — normal, and strongest in the evening | Carrier, skin-to-skin, tag-team |
| Crying that starts **20–40 minutes after a feed**, with back-arching and spit-up | **Reflux** | Upright 20–30 min, smaller feeds, PD if it persists (§9) |

**Practical use:** name the hypothesis out loud ("that's a tired cry") and test it for **5 minutes**. If the test fails, **move to the next hypothesis** — do not repeat the same failing action for 40 minutes. Repetition of a failing move is how parents end up frantic.

## 4. The 9-point sweep — in order, every time

The order is chosen so that the **cheap, common, and dangerous** causes get checked first. It takes about 3–5 minutes with practice.

### 4.1 Feed — but check the cue, not the clock

- **Real hunger cues:** stirring, mouth opening, rooting, hands to mouth, sucking noises, turning toward your chest. **Crying is late** — a crying baby latches worse because he's already dysregulated.
- **In the first weeks:** 8–12 feeds/24 h. A baby who fed 40 minutes ago and is rooting again may be **cluster feeding** (normal, especially 6–9 p.m. and around growth spurts at ~2–3 weeks and ~6 weeks) — feed him.
- **But:** not every cry is hunger. If he fed well 20 minutes ago, is not rooting, and is arching away from the breast/bottle, **food is not the answer** — going straight to the bottle every time is how overfeeding, more gas, and more crying happen.
- **Bottle-fed:** paced feeding, semi-upright, stop at the first "no" cue ([`../food-and-feeding/formula-and-bottles.md`](../food-and-feeding/formula-and-bottles.md) §5). A 90 ml feed that took 5 minutes is a gas bomb.
- **Never** add cereal, water, honey, or anything to a bottle.

### 4.2 Wind — the most under-diagnosed cause in the first 8 weeks

- Burp **mid-feed** (if he squirms, gulps, or pulls off) and **after** every feed — three positions, 2–3 minutes each ([`care-techniques.md`](care-techniques.md) §3).
- If nothing comes up after ~5 minutes, stop trying — not all crying is trapped air.
- **Upright 15–20 minutes after a feed**, especially if he spits up.
- **Bicycle legs**, tummy massage (§5.11), and the **colic hold** (face-down along your forearm, head to the side, awake only) move gas.

### 4.3 Diaper

- Wet or dirty — change it. But **do not** change a sleeping baby "just in case" at 3 a.m.; that's how you create a wake window you didn't need.
- Check the **fit**: leg cuffs flared out, tabs not digging in, nothing tight across the belly, no tag or thread inside the suit.
- Check the **skin**: redness, a hair tourniquet, a scratch, a spot.

### 4.4 Temperature — too hot is more common than too cold

- **Back of the neck:** warm and dry = right. Sweaty/hot = **take a layer off**. Cool = add one thin layer.
- Cold hands and feet are **normal** and are not a reason to add blankets.
- Room: comfortable for a lightly dressed adult. In a Singapore flat with aircon, ~24–26 °C is a reasonable target; **no hat indoors, no beanies in the cot, never over-bundle** (overheating is a SIDS risk factor, not a comfort measure).
- A crying baby who is **sweating, flushed, and hot to the touch** → undress to a diaper, cool the room, re-check in 10 minutes; if the temperature is **≥38 °C**, §1.1.

### 4.5 Stimulation — most evening crying is an overloaded baby

Newborns have a **very low** input budget. By 6 p.m. he may have had: a bath, three visitors, a photo session, a car ride, and a bright living room. The result is a baby who cannot settle and cries harder the more you jiggle him.

**Overstimulated looks like:** turning his head away, splaying fingers, hiccuping, yawning, startling at sounds, a "glazed" stare, then escalating crying.

**Fix — subtract, don't add:**
- Dim the lights (or turn them off and use a hallway light).
- **Stop talking.** No shushing *and* singing *and* the TV. One sound only.
- Remove him from the room with people in it — 689 sqft means "the other room" is 4 steps away, and that's enough.
- No eye contact for a minute or two, then reintroduce your face slowly.
- Swaddle + hold still + shush. **Stillness is a technique.**

### 4.6 Sleep — the overtired trap

- A newborn can be **awake for only 45–60 minutes** before he's overtired. Past that, cortisol rises and he cries *harder* the more tired he gets. This is the single most common cause of "he's been screaming for two hours and won't sleep".
- **Tired cues:** yawning, staring into space, slowing movements, turning away, red eyebrows, fussing that escalates when you engage.
- **The fix is boring:** dark, swaddle, shush, still hold, cot. **Do not** add a car ride, a new position, and a song at this point — you're adding input to a baby who needs less.
- **Wake windows by age:** 0–4 weeks ~45–60 min; 4–8 weeks ~60–75 min; 8–12 weeks ~75–90 min. These are guides, not rules; his cues beat the clock.

### 4.7 Contact and suck — the needs that aren't "bad habits"

- **Contact:** he spent nine months against a body. Being put down is a change in temperature, sound, smell, and pressure all at once. Carrying him is meeting a need, not creating one. A carrier (§5.10) buys you hands.
- **Suck:** non-nutritive sucking is genuinely calming. Clean finger, or a pacifier if you've decided to use one. **If breastfeeding is still being established (first 3–4 weeks), most lactation advice is to wait before introducing a pacifier** — confirm with the TMC LC/PD, and if you use one, use it *after* a feed, not instead of one.
- **Pacifier safety:** one-piece, no cord, no clip, never dipped in anything sweet, don't reinsert it if it falls out during sleep, and don't force it.

### 4.8 Pain hunt — head to toe, with your hands

Run §1.3 again, but this time **look for the small stuff**:

- **Hair tourniquet** — the classic miss. A single hair around a toe, finger, or the base of the penis; the digit goes red, then swollen, then dusky. **Look at all 20 digits every time** a baby cries inconsolably with no other explanation. Remove with oil + unwinding, not by pulling; if it's embedded or the digit is dusky → A&E.
- **Scratched eye** — watery, red, light-sensitive, cries when you open it. Same-day PD.
- **Nappy rash that has become broken skin or thrush** — cries at every change.
- **Cord stump** catching on the diaper, or a red ring around it.
- **Mouth:** thrush (white patches that don't wipe off), a blister from a bad latch, a tongue-tie making feeds painful.
- **Nose:** blocked with mucus — he can't breathe and suck at the same time, so he pulls off and cries. Saline + suction ([`care-techniques.md`](care-techniques.md) §6), especially before feeds.
- **Clothing:** a tag, a thread around a toe inside a sock, a too-tight cuff, a scratchy label, a poppers tab digging in.
- **Car seat / carrier:** straps too tight, the crotch buckle pinching, the head support pushing his chin down.
- **Vaccination site:** sore, red, swollen for 24–48 h after a shot — normal, and cuddles + feeds are the treatment; paracetamol only if the PD says so.
- **Hernia:** a groin bulge that appears on crying — **same-day**, and **now** if it's hard/tender.

### 4.9 Illness — the quiet check

- Temperature (§1.3), breathing rate and effort, colour, feeding, wet diapers, stools, vomiting, rash, tone.
- **Any** of the §1.1 signs → stop, go.
- **If everything is clean and the crying continues** → this is **normal crying**, and §5–§7 is the treatment.

## 5. The soothing repertoire — every technique, with failure modes

**How to use this section:** pick from the **stack order** (5.1) first. If that fails, work **down the list**, 3–5 minutes each. Do not run all 25 in ten minutes — that's overstimulation with extra steps.

**The universal rules:**
- **One change at a time.** If you add motion + sound + a new position + a diaper change at once, you learn nothing about what worked.
- **3–5 minutes per technique.** Newborns take ~90 seconds to register a change.
- **Calm is contagious; panic isn't.** If your shoulders are at your ears, he feels it. One slow exhale before you start.
- **If it works, keep doing the exact same thing** — don't "upgrade" a settling baby.

### 5.1 The core stack — the 5 S's, done properly

The one-liner is in [`newborn-care.md`](newborn-care.md). Here is the version that works, and **how each one fails**.

| S | How | Failure mode | Safety line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Swaddle** | Snug across the chest and arms, **loose at the hips** (legs bent up and out), two fingers slide under the chest fabric. Full 5-step in [`care-techniques.md`](care-techniques.md) §2 | Too loose → startle reflex wakes him; too tight at the hips → hip risk; arms out too early → he fights | **Stop at first sign of rolling (~2–4 months).** Never swaddle for sleep with the head covered |
| **Side / stomach hold** | Hold him on his side or face-down along your forearm, head supported, **in your arms, awake** | Doing it too gently — he needs firm, full contact; or leaving him that way | **Never place him on his side/stomach to sleep.** This hold soothes; the sleep position kills |
| **Shush** | Loud, continuous "shhhh" right at his ear, **as loud as his cry**, slowing as he calms | Too quiet to register, or a sharp "SHH!" that startles | Not into the ear canal; don't shout |
| **Swing** | Small, fast, rhythmic jiggle — "shivering", head supported, ~1–2 cm of movement | Big rocking (doesn't work and is dangerous), or no head support | **Never shake.** No head whipping, no throwing, no bouncing on a knee |
| **Suck** | Clean finger, pacifier, or a breastfeed | Using it to replace a feed when he's actually hungry | Pacifier: one-piece, no cord, don't force, don't dip |

**The order inside the stack:** swaddle first (it removes the startle), then the hold (contact), then shush (sound), then swing (motion) — and only then suck, because sucking puts him to sleep and you want the other four already in place.

### 5.2 Containment and pressure

| Technique | How | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Firm swaddle** | §5.1 | Startle, overtired, evening fussing |
| **Chest-to-chest, upright** | His ear over your heart, one hand on his back, your breathing slow and exaggerated | Almost everything; the default hold |
| **Side-lying on your forearm** | Belly along your forearm, head in your hand, other hand on his back | Gas, after feeds |
| **"Burrito" hold** | Swaddled, held horizontally, close, your arm along his length | Overtired, needs stillness |
| **Hand on the chest** | Flat palm, firm but not pressing, in the cot | Settling in the cot without picking up |
| **Your body as a wall** | Lie him against you on the sofa **while you are awake** — never for sleep | Cluster evenings |

### 5.3 Motion

| Technique | How | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Walking** | Corridor, room to room, 5–10 minutes, steady | Often the only thing that works at 9 p.m. In a 689 sqft flat: corridor + kitchen loop is enough |
| **Rocking in arms** | Small amplitude, head supported, ~1 Hz | Never head-snapping |
| **Yoga/birthing ball** | Sit, hold him chest-to-chest, gentle bounce | Saves your back; effective |
| **Pram** | Flat, in the corridor or void deck | Never as an unsupervised sleep surface |
| **Carrier** | TICKS fit ([`care-techniques.md`](care-techniques.md) §11) | Hands-free; the single best evening tool |
| **Car ride** | **Last resort**, and only with a second adult awake | Never leave him in the car; never drive exhausted; never as a sleep solution (§12) |
| **Baby swing / rocker** | Only awake and supervised | **Never a sleep surface** — inclined sleepers have caused deaths |

### 5.4 Sound

| Technique | How | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Shush** | As loud as the cry, then gradually quieter and slower | The original white noise |
| **White noise** | Continuous, steady, no melody. **≤50 dB at his ear** — roughly the volume of a quiet shower, or a free decibel app showing ~50 at the cot. Place the source **≥1–2 m away**, never on the cot rail or in the crib | The most evidence-supported sleep/soothing aid. Turn it down or off once he's asleep; don't run it at max all night |
| **Heartbeat / womb sounds** | Recorded, low volume | Same rules as white noise |
| **Humming / singing** | Low, slow, one song, on repeat | Your voice is the thing he already knows. Keep it quiet and monotonous at night |
| **Shower / tap / fan** | Run the bathroom fan or a tap; stand in the bathroom with him | Changing the acoustic environment is itself soothing |
| **Vacuum / hairdryer recording** | Only as a recording, not a live appliance near him | Never leave a live appliance in reach |

**Volume warning:** "as loud as his crying" is a *starting* point for shush in your arms, not a target for a speaker in his cot. Chronic loud noise is a hearing risk and a stressor for everyone.

### 5.5 Suck and feeding strategies

- **Clean finger**, pad up, nail down, let him suck — the most controllable option.
- **Pacifier**, if you've decided to use one (§4.7).
- **Cluster feeding is a feature, not a failure.** In the evening he may want to feed every 30–60 minutes for 2–3 hours. That is normal and it is also how supply is built. Park yourself, get water, use a pillow, and let the shift partner handle everything else.
- **Paced bottle**: if a bottle is being used to settle him, slow it down — semi-upright, teat horizontal, pauses, stop at the first stop-cue. Fast bottles → swallowed air → more crying → more bottles.
- **Do not** feed to the point of vomiting. If he's taking a bottle and turning away, stop.

### 5.6 Touch, temperature, and skin

| Technique | How | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Skin-to-skin** | Diaper only, on your bare chest, a blanket over both | Regulates his temperature, heart rate, and crying. Works on fathers too — this is not a mother-only intervention |
| **Warm bath** | 37–38 °C, with you or with a hand on him at all times | The transition *out* of the bath is often the calming part; have the towel warm |
| **Warm towel on the tummy** | Warm (not hot) cloth, tested on your wrist, 5 minutes | Gas. Never leave it on unsupervised or over the face |
| **Baby massage** | Slow, firm strokes — legs, belly clockwise, arms, back | §5.11; after a feed, not during crying that's escalating |
| **Cool room, light layer** | Aircon 24–26 °C, one layer | Overheating causes crying; check the neck |
| **Change of clothes** | Fresh suit, no tags | Sometimes it's just a damp collar |

### 5.7 Scene and sensory change

- **Change rooms.** The single most underrated move in a small flat.
- **Step outside** — corridor, void deck, 5 minutes of night air. Singapore heat means short and shaded; watch for mosquitoes and direct sun.
- **Reduce to one sense.** Dark room + shush only. No eye contact, no talking.
- **Add one sense.** If he's under-stimulated (rare in the evening, common in the morning), try a face, a song, a black-and-white card — but only one.
- **The bathroom trick:** fan on, lights dim, hold him chest-to-chest. The sound + humidity + echo works on a lot of babies.

### 5.8 Gas-specific routine (do this in order)

1. **Burp** — three positions (§4.2).
2. **Bicycle legs** — gentle, both legs, 10–15 cycles, then knees to chest and hold 5 seconds.
3. **Tummy massage** — clockwise, using the "I Love U" stroke: down his left side (your right), across the top, down the left again. Firm, not ticklish.
4. **Colic hold** — face-down along your forearm, head to the side, **awake only**.
5. **Warm bath** — the water and the change of state relax the abdominal wall.
6. **Upright 20 minutes** after the next feed.

**Do not:** push his legs forcefully, press hard on the belly, use a rectal thermometer or "gas tube" to release wind (perforation risk; only if the PD specifically instructs), or give gripe water/colic drops/herbal oils (§8.4).

### 5.9 The failure-mode table (why "the 5 S's don't work")

| Symptom | Likely reason | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| He cries harder when swaddled | Too loose, or he's already past the point | Re-swaddle firmly, arms in, then hold still for 2 minutes |
| Shush makes him worse | Too loud/sharp, or he's overstimulated | Quieter, slower, one sound only |
| He calms in arms, screams on put-down | He's not in deep sleep yet, or the cot is cold | Hold **15–20 min until the arm hangs limp**, warm the sheet, hands **still** on him for 30 s, withdraw in stages — the full 7-step sequence is [`night-and-sleep.md`](night-and-sleep.md) §2.1, and the cot ladder is §2.2 |
| It worked for 10 minutes then failed | Normal — the effect wears off | Repeat the same technique, don't switch everything |
| Nothing works at all | Possibly pain, illness, or the 6-week peak | §1.3 check, then §13 tag-team, then call the PD |

### 5.10 Carriers and prams (the evening survival gear)

- **Carrier fit = TICKS** (tight, in view, close enough to kiss, chin off chest, back supported). Chin-to-chest can close an airway in minutes.
- **Never** forward-facing before head control; **never** cook, run, or hold hot drinks while wearing him.
- **Never** use the carrier as a sleep surface for long stretches — if he falls asleep, keep him in view, check his breathing, and move him to the cot when you can.
- **Pram:** flat, shaded, never as an unsupervised sleep space, never with a blanket over the canopy (heat trap).

### 5.11 Baby massage (the 6-minute version)

Warm room, warm hands, after a feed (not during a screaming fit), no oil needed or a plain edible oil if you want glide — **no essential oils, no menthol, no camphor, no "wind oil"** on a newborn.

1. Legs: stroke down, then gentle bicycle.
2. Belly: clockwise circles, then the "I Love U" stroke.
3. Arms: stroke down, open the hands.
4. Chest: outward from the sternum.
5. Back: long strokes down, small circles either side of the spine (not on the spine).
6. Stop the moment he protests; 6 minutes is plenty.

## 6. The decision flow — minute by minute

```
CRYING STARTS
│
├─ 5-second look: breathing / colour / tone / blood / bulge
│     └─ ANY concern → §1.1 GO NOW. Stop reading.
│
├─ 0–5 min: 9-point sweep (§4)
│     ├─ Found a cause (hungry, wet, cold, hair tourniquet, tag, hernia…) → fix it
│     └─ Nothing found → normal crying
│
├─ 5–20 min: the 5 S's, in order, 3–5 min per technique (§5.1)
│     ├─ Settling → keep doing exactly that, do not add anything
│     └─ Not settling → move down the stack (§5.2–5.8)
│
├─ 20 min: TAG-TEAM (§13.3) — fresh adult, handover script
│     └─ Alone? → 10-minute safe break: cot, back, door, timer (§13.2)
│
├─ 45 min: try the "big three" — carrier + walk + shush; bath; skin-to-skin in a dark room
│
├─ 90 min: STOP troubleshooting. This is now a "ride it out in shifts" situation.
│     ├─ Any §1.2 sign → call the PD now
│     └─ No sign → continue tag-team, 20 min each, until he settles or feeds again
│
└─ The crying stops → feed/change if due, then put him down awake-but-drowsy,
      on his back, in the cot. Write it in the log (§14). Eat something. Sleep.
```

**Timing rule of thumb:** most bouts resolve in **10–30 minutes** with the right technique. A bout that goes **past 90 minutes** is either the evening peak (§7), colic (§8), or something medical (§11) — those are the three buckets, and each has its own section.

## 7. The evening peak — the 5 p.m. to 11 p.m. protocol

This is the shift that breaks first-time parents, and it is **predictable**, which means you can plan for it.

### 7.1 What's happening

- Crying builds from ~5 p.m., peaks ~7–9 p.m., and settles by ~11 p.m.
- Cluster feeding is normal and overlaps with it.
- His nervous system has hit its daily input limit; **less is more** after 6 p.m.
- It is **not** caused by something you did at 4 p.m., and it is **not** hunger if he fed well and is now arching away.

### 7.2 The pre-emptive plan (start at 5 p.m., not 8 p.m.)

| Time | Move |
|---|---|
| **4:30–5 p.m.** | Feed, burp, change. Close the curtains. Lights down. **No visitors after 5 p.m.** in the first 8 weeks — say it now, as a couple |
| **5–6 p.m.** | One parent takes him in the carrier and walks. The other cooks/eats/showers. **Both of you eat before 7** — hungry parents make bad decisions |
| **6–7 p.m.** | Bath (if it's a bath day), then skin-to-skin in a dim room. No screens, no music with lyrics, no conversation over his head |
| **7–9 p.m.** | **The peak.** Tag-team in 20-minute blocks. The carrier is the default. Cluster feeds are allowed to happen |
| **9–10 p.m.** | The "handover feed" — whoever is taking the night shift feeds him; the other goes to sleep **now** |
| **10–11 p.m.** | Dim, boring, minimal talk. The goal is not "sleep through" — it's "start the night calm" |

### 7.3 The rules for the peak

- **Do not troubleshoot at 8 p.m.** By then, the sweep has been done and the answer is "ride it out". Re-checking his temperature every 20 minutes is anxiety, not care.
- **Do not introduce new things** at the peak — no new bottle, no new swaddle, no new position, no visitors holding him.
- **Do not compare evenings.** Tuesday calm, Wednesday hell, Thursday calm is the normal pattern.
- **Do not decide anything important after 9 p.m.** — not about feeding, not about work, not about each other.
- **Do decide, in daylight:** who has which block, who gets the 4-hour sleep, who taps out at what point. Write it on the fridge (§18).

## 8. Colic — what it is, what actually helps

### 8.1 The definition (Wessel)

Crying **≥3 hours/day, ≥3 days/week, for ≥3 weeks**, in an otherwise healthy, well-fed, growing baby, starting in the first weeks and resolving by ~3–4 months.

- **It is a description, not a diagnosis.** Before anyone says "it's colic", §9 and §10 have to have been considered.
- **It is not caused by bad parenting**, and it is not caused by "too much wind" alone.
- **It resolves.** Every time. The average is around 3–4 months.

### 8.2 What the evidence actually supports

| Intervention | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Swaddling, shushing, swinging, sucking, holding** | ✅ First-line | Low risk, real effect on settling |
| **White noise** | ✅ First-line | ≤50 dB, not on the cot |
| **Carrier / motion** | ✅ First-line | Effective, and saves your arms |
| **Responsive holding, not "spoiling"** | ✅ Do it | No evidence of harm at this age |
| **Reducing stimulation in the evening** | ✅ Do it | The lowest-risk intervention there is |
| **Paced feeding, good latch, burping** | ✅ Do it | Reduces swallowed air and overfeeding |
| **Maternal dairy/soy elimination** (breastfed) | ⚠️ Only with the PD/dietitian, 2–4 weeks, then reassess | Only if there are other CMPA signs (§9.3). Don't do it blind — it's hard on Ervina and can reduce her intake |
| **Extensively hydrolysed formula** | ⚠️ Only if CMPA is diagnosed | Not a colic treatment |
| **L. reuteri probiotic** (breastfed babies) | ⚠️ Modest evidence | Ask the PD before buying; not for formula-fed as a first move |
| **Simethicone drops** | ⚠️ No clear benefit, low risk | Reasonable to try once, not to keep buying |
| **Gripe water** | ❌ No | No evidence, variable ingredients (some contain alcohol/bicarbonate), contamination risk |
| **Fennel/ginger/“colic teas”** | ❌ No | Herbal products are unregulated; nothing but milk by mouth |
| **Chiropractic / spinal manipulation** | ❌ No | Real risk of harm in infants, no evidence |
| **"Wind oil", medicated balms, camphor, menthol** | ❌ No | Can cause seizures and skin reactions in newborns |
| **Cutting a feed, watering down formula, switching formula repeatedly** | ❌ No | Dangerous and makes things worse |
| **"Cry it out"** | ❌ No | Not appropriate at newborn age, and never for a hungry or unwell baby |

### 8.3 What to ask the PD at the visit

Bring the log (§14) and ask, in these words:

1. "Does this meet the colic definition, or could it be reflux or a milk-protein allergy?"
2. "Is his weight gain and diaper count reassuring?"
3. "Is there anything about the cry itself that worries you?" (Play the recording — §11.1.)
4. "If we want to try a probiotic or a dairy elimination, what exactly would you recommend and for how long?"
5. "What would make you want to see him again before the next scheduled visit?"

### 8.4 The colic evening, in practice

- **Carrier from 6 p.m.** Don't wait for the crying to start.
- **One parent on, one off, 20 minutes each.** The off parent is not "resting" — they're doing the dishes, the laundry, the bottles. That's the deal.
- **Feed on cue, burp properly, then upright.**
- **Dark, quiet, still.** The peak is not the time for a developmentally enriching experience.
- **At 10 p.m., the crying usually breaks.** Plan the night shift around that, not around 7 p.m.

## 9. The look-alikes — colic vs reflux vs CMPA vs gas vs overfeeding

"Colic" is the label people reach for when they've stopped looking. **Look first.** These four account for most of what gets called colic, and three of them are treatable.

| | **Colic** | **Reflux (GER)** | **CMPA** (cow's-milk protein allergy) | **Gas / overfeeding** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **When** | Evening, predictable, weeks 2–8 | During/after feeds, and lying flat | Any time, often with feeds | After fast or large feeds |
| **Cry** | Intense, evening, settles eventually | Arching, pulling off, crying at the breast | Persistent, worsening, with feeds | Sudden, squirming, then a burp or a poop |
| **Vomit** | None, or normal spit-up | **Frequent spit-up**, sometimes large | **Projectile or frequent**, sometimes blood-streaked | Small spit-ups; volume-related |
| **Stool** | Normal | Normal | **Mucus, blood, diarrhoea, or constipation** | Normal, may be loose after overfeeding |
| **Skin** | Normal | Normal | **Eczema, rash, hives** | Normal |
| **Weight** | Normal | Usually normal; **poor gain if severe** | **Poor gain** | Normal or rapid |
| **Other** | Healthy, growing | Hiccups, cough, hoarse cry | Swelling, wheeze, family history of allergy | Distended belly, relieved by wind |
| **What to do** | §7–§8 | Upright 20–30 min, smaller/more frequent feeds, burp well; **PD if it persists, is painful, or weight stalls** | **PD** — maternal dairy/soy elimination trial or hydrolysed formula, 2–4 weeks, then reassess | Slow the feeds, pace the bottle, burp mid-feed |

**Do not** diagnose CMPA yourself and switch formula five times in a week. **Do not** start a maternal elimination diet without the PD — the mother's nutrition matters too.

### 9.1 Reflux — the practical rules

- **Normal:** posseting after feeds, hiccups, a wet burp. No treatment.
- **Troublesome:** frequent large vomits, arching, crying with feeds, poor weight gain, cough, hoarse cry.
- **Do:** upright 20–30 minutes after feeds, smaller and more frequent feeds, burp well, avoid overfeeding, keep the cot flat (never prop the mattress or let him sleep inclined).
- **Don't:** put him to sleep on an incline, add cereal to the bottle, or use over-the-counter reflux "remedies".
- **Medication** is the PD's call and is not for normal reflux.

### 9.2 Gas and stools

- **Breastfed newborns can go several days without a stool** and still be normal — if the stool is soft when it comes and he's feeding and growing. Straining and grunting is normal; **crying with a hard, dry stool is constipation.**
- **Formula-fed:** firmer stools are normal; pellets are not.
- **Never** give water, juice, or sugar water. **Never** use a suppository, enema, or rectal stimulation without the PD.

### 9.3 CMPA red flags (call the PD, don't self-treat)

Blood or mucus in the stool, persistent diarrhoea, eczema that won't settle, swelling of lips/eyes, wheeze, vomiting after every feed, poor weight gain, or a strong family history of allergy/asthma/eczema. **Any breathing or swelling sign → 995.**

## 10. The head-to-toe pain hunt, in detail

Run this when the crying is inconsolable and §4.8 found nothing obvious. It takes 2 minutes and has caught a lot of hair tourniquets.

| Area | Look for | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Scalp** | A hair wrapped around a finger-width of skin, a scratch, a bump | Unwind with oil; if the skin is broken or swollen → PD |
| **Eyes** | Redness, watering, discharge, light sensitivity, a scratch on the cornea | Same-day PD; don't put anything in the eye |
| **Ears** | Discharge, redness, tenderness, a fold of skin that's raw | Same-day PD if discharge or fever |
| **Nose** | Blockage, noisy breathing | Saline + suction, especially before feeds |
| **Mouth** | White patches that don't wipe off (thrush), blisters, a swollen tongue, a cut | Same-day PD for thrush; A&E if swelling affects breathing |
| **Neck / collarbone** | Swelling, a crackle, a cry when the arm is lifted | A&E / PD same day — possible fracture from birth |
| **Chest / breathing** | Retractions, grunting, rate >60, pauses | §1.1 |
| **Abdomen** | Firm, distended, tender, a visible loop | A&E |
| **Groin** | A bulge that appears on crying (hernia), swelling of the scrotum | **A&E now if hard/tender**; same-day PD otherwise |
| **Penis** | A hair at the base, redness, swelling | Remove hair, or A&E if dusky |
| **Testicles** | Firm, swollen, red, high-riding, very tender | **A&E now** — torsion is a surgical emergency |
| **Anus** | A fissure, a hard stool, a tag | PD; never force anything |
| **Fingers** | Hair/thread tourniquet, a swollen red tip, a cut | Remove; A&E if dusky |
| **Toes** | Same, especially inside socks | Same |
| **Legs / hips** | A cry when one leg is moved, unequal skin creases, a "clunk" | PD — possible hip issue or fracture |
| **Skin folds** | A tag, a thread, a scratch, a bite, a rash | Remove the tag; PD for spreading rash |
| **Diaper area** | Broken skin, thrush, a rash that bleeds | Barrier cream, air time; PD if it doesn't improve in 48 h |

**If you find nothing and he's still crying:** it's normal crying or colic. Close the hunt, open §13, and stop looking — repeated undressing and probing is itself overstimulating.

## 11. When the cry itself is the symptom

### 11.1 The cry that should worry you

| Cry | What it can mean | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **High-pitched, shrill, piercing — "not his cry"** | Neurological irritation, meningitis, raised pressure, pain | **Same hour** — A&E if with fever, bulge, stiffness, or lethargy |
| **Weak, breathy, whimpering, fading** | Serious illness, dehydration, exhaustion | **Same day, now if with poor feeding or fever** |
| **Continuous monotone scream, no pauses, no calm window** | Pain (including torsion, hernia, injury) | **A&E** if >1–2 h with no calm window |
| **Cry on movement of one limb** | Fracture, joint, clavicle | **A&E / same-day** |
| **Cry that stops abruptly and he goes limp/pale** | A serious event (apnoea, seizure) | **995 now** |
| **Cry with arching, back stiffening, and eye deviation** | Seizure / neurological | **995 now** |
| **Cry that only happens at feeds, with pulling off and arching** | Reflux, CMPA, oral pain, nasal blockage | PD same day |
| **Cry with a rash, swelling, or breathing change** | Allergy / infection | **995 if breathing/swelling**; PD otherwise |

**Record it.** A 20-second video of the cry, with the time, is worth more to the PD than any description. Do it once, not for every bout.

### 11.2 After a fall or a bump

Any fall from a bed, sofa, changing table, or your arms — **even if he seems fine** — is a call. Go to A&E if there was loss of consciousness, vomiting, a seizure, a bulging soft spot, unequal pupils, drowsiness, or a swollen/bruised head. If he's completely normal, still call the PD for advice the same day. **Never** "wait and see" after a head impact in a baby under 3 months.

### 11.3 After a vaccination

Soreness, mild fever, fussiness for 24–48 h is normal. **Fever ≥38 °C under 3 months is still the hospital rule** — vaccination doesn't change it. Paracetamol only at the PD's dose.

## 12. The never list

Every one of these has hurt a real baby. They are not style opinions.

| Never | Why |
|---|---|
| **Shake him** — even once, even for a second, even "gently" | Shaking causes bleeding in the brain and can kill or cause lifelong disability. It peaks at the same age as crying |
| **Throw him up, bounce him hard, or jiggle his head** | Same mechanism |
| **Leave him to "cry it out" at newborn age** | Not developmentally appropriate; never for a hungry or unwell baby |
| **Put him on his side or stomach to sleep** — including "just for the colic" | SIDS and suffocation risk; the colic hold is an **awake** hold |
| **Sleep with him on a sofa, armchair, or beanbag** — even for a minute | The highest-risk sleep surface there is |
| **Prop a bottle, or leave him feeding alone** | Choking and ear infections |
| **Put cereal, water, honey, juice, or herbs in a bottle** | Choking, botulism, water intoxication, unregulated ingredients |
| **Use gripe water, wind oil, camphor, menthol, or "colic drops" without the PD** | No evidence, real harms |
| **Put anything in his cot** — pillow, blanket, bumper, toy, positioner, nest | Suffocation |
| **Use a car seat, swing, rocker, or bouncer as a sleep surface** | Positional asphyxia |
| **Drive with him when you're exhausted** | A drowsy driver is the other half of the risk; if you're too tired, don't drive |
| **Leave him in the car** — "just to pay", "just for a minute" | Heat stroke in minutes in Singapore |
| **Take your frustration out on him in any way** — including a hard put-down | The cot is safe; your arms when you're furious are not |
| **Give him to a frustrated visitor/helper and walk away without a check** | Anyone can snap. Tell every caregiver the rule: put him down, walk away, call you |
| **Stop breastfeeding / switch formula repeatedly because of crying, without the PD** | Crying is usually not a feeding problem; repeated switching creates one |

## 13. The parent protocol — the part that actually keeps him safe

### 13.1 What crying does to you (so you can name it)

- Your heart rate rises, your jaw tightens, your hearing narrows. That's a **mammalian alarm response**, not a character flaw.
- Prolonged crying is one of the most reliable triggers of **anger and intrusive thoughts** in otherwise gentle parents. Having the thought does not make you a danger; **acting on it does**.
- Sleep deprivation amplifies everything by a factor of three. At 3 a.m., your judgment is genuinely impaired — treat yourself as impaired.
- **You are not being judged by the baby.** He has no concept of "good parent". He has a nervous system and so do you.

### 13.2 The safe put-down (the single most important skill in this file)

When you feel the urge to **shake, shout, squeeze, or throw** — or when you've been at it for 90 minutes and your hands are shaking:

1. **Put him on his back in the cot.** Empty cot, no blanket, no pillow. Say "I'm putting you down safely."
2. **Walk out.** Close the door.
3. **Set a 10-minute timer.** Breathe: in for 4, out for 6. Drink water. Splash your face. Step outside if you can.
4. **Go back at 10 minutes** — or sooner if the crying changes (a sudden stop, a choke, a different sound).
5. **Repeat if needed.** He can cry in a safe cot for 10 minutes while you reset. He cannot be safely handled by a parent who is about to snap.

**In a 689 sqft flat, "away" means:** the corridor outside the door, the bathroom with the fan on, or the kitchen with the door shut. You do not need a garden. You need **four walls and ten minutes**.

**Earplugs are not neglect.** Musician's earplugs or noise-reducing buds take the edge off the volume while still letting you hear him. Many parents use one ear only, so they can still hear the monitor.

**Never** put him down on a bed, sofa, or changing table "for a second" while you cool off. The cot, on his back, is the only correct place.

### 13.3 The tag-team handover (say it in this order)

> "He last fed at ___, diaper at ___, temp ___. I've done ___ and ___. He's not settling. **I need 20 minutes.** Take him and walk."

Then the other person **takes him and walks** — no questions, no "what did you try", no debate about whose turn it is. Twenty minutes, then swap. A roster beats a negotiation at 9 p.m.

**Write the roster on the fridge** (§18): who has 6–10 p.m., who has 10 p.m.–2 a.m., who has 2–6 a.m., and who gets the **uninterrupted 4-hour block** (this is Ervina's, per [`postpartum-recovery.md`](postpartum-recovery.md)).

### 13.4 When it's more than frustration

| What you notice | What to do |
|---|---|
| Rage, shaking, or a thought of hurting him that shocks you | **Put him down safely. Walk out. Call someone.** Tell your partner tonight, not "when things calm down" |
| Thoughts of harming yourself, or that he'd be better off without you | **Call SOS 1-767 (24 h) or IMH 6389 2222 now.** This is a medical symptom, not a failing |
| Can't sleep even when he sleeps; can't eat; crying every day; feel nothing for him | Tell the PD at the next visit — postpartum depression/anxiety screening ([`postpartum-recovery.md`](postpartum-recovery.md) §6) |
| You're the one who nearly snapped, and you're ashamed | Say it out loud to your partner and to the PD. Shame kept silent is the risk factor; shame spoken is a safety plan |
| A visitor or helper is frustrated with him | Take him back. Immediately. No explanation needed |

**The deal to make with each other, in daylight:** "If either of us says *I need to tap out*, the other takes him, no questions." Agree it now, before the first bad night.

### 14.1 What the log tells you

2. Any **new** symptom in the week — vomit, rash, stool change, fewer wet diapers?

4. Are **you two** sleeping in blocks? If not, fix the roster before fixing the baby.

## 15. Calling the PD — the script

Have this open when you call. It turns a rambling 3 a.m. call into a 4-minute one.

> "This is Eldric Lim, born 26 October, now ___ weeks, weight ___ kg. He's been crying for ___ minutes/hours, starting at ___. He has/hasn't fed normally — last feed ___, ___ ml/minutes. Wet diapers in the last 24 h: ___. Stool: ___. Temperature ___ °C, taken ___ (armpit/rectal) at ___. No fever / fever. He is/isn't consolable. **Out of hours:** TMC 24 h **+65 6250 2222**; KKH Children's Emergency **+65 6225 5554**; **995** for anything in §1.1. When in doubt, go — nobody at A&E judges a first-time parent for being careful.

## 16. Age map — what changes, and when

This file is newborn-first, but crying changes shape. Here's the whole first year in one table, so you know when to stop using the newborn playbook.

| Age | What the crying is usually about | What changes in the plan |
|---|---|---|
| **0–4 weeks** | Hunger, wind, temperature, overstimulation, contact | This file as written. Swaddle. Feed 8–12×/day. No CIO |
| **4–8 weeks** | **The peak** — evening colic, cluster feeding, growth spurts (~6 weeks) | §7 and §8 are the main tools. Tag-team is not optional |
| **8–12 weeks** | Same, declining; social smile arrives ~6 weeks and helps *you* | Fewer techniques needed; start watching for tired cues instead of fixing |
| **3–4 months** | Evening colic fades; **sleep regression**, overtiredness, developmental fussiness | **Stop swaddling at first roll.** Move to a sleep sack, darker room, earlier bedtime |
| **4–6 months** | Teething, hunger (growth), new awareness, **separation** | Teething: cold (not frozen) teether, gum massage, cuddle. No teething gels with benzocaine; no amber necklaces (strangulation) |
| **6–9 months** | Separation anxiety, object permanence, **sleep protest** | Peekaboo, a consistent leaving routine, quick returns. **Now** gentle sleep-work methods become reasonable — with the PD, and never for hunger/illness |
| **9–12 months** | Independence, frustration, **tantrums begin**, teething, illness | Name the feeling, stay calm, don't negotiate with a 10-month-old. Crying is communication, not manipulation |
| **12 months+** | Tantrums, limits, language frustration | Different file, and the parenting guide takes over ([`../parenting/`](../parenting/)) |

**What never changes across the whole first year:** back to sleep, bare cot, feed on cues, hands washed, never shake, never leave him alone in water/car/raised surface, and **respond to him** — the research on "spoiling" does not support it at any point in the first year.

## 19. The 10 rules, if you read nothing else

1. **Screen first, soothe second** — §1 takes 5 seconds and can save his life.
2. **Sweep, don't guess** — the 9 points, in order, every time.
3. **3–5 minutes per technique, one change at a time.**
4. **Name the hypothesis, test it, move on.** Don't repeat a failing move for 40 minutes.
5. **Evening crying is normal and predictable** — plan the 5–11 p.m. window in daylight.
6. **Most "colic" is reflux, gas, overfeeding, or overstimulation** — look before you label.
7. **Check every finger and toe for a hair** when nothing else explains it.
8. **At 20 minutes, swap.** At 90 minutes, stop troubleshooting and ride it out in shifts.
9. **Put him down safe and walk away** whenever you feel the snap coming. Every time. No exceptions.
10. **Never shake him.** The crying ends. The injury doesn't.

## 20. Sources and honesty notes

- **Normal crying curve / PURPLE:** Barr RG, "Crying behaviour and its impact" and the National Center on Shaken Baby Syndrome's PURPLE program — the peak at ~6 weeks and the "resists soothing" framing.
- **Shaking peak:** the 6–8 week correlation between infant crying peaks and abusive head trauma is well documented in the paediatric literature.
- **Colic definition:** Wessel's "rule of three" (≥3 h/day, ≥3 days/week, ≥3 weeks).
- **5 S's:** Harvey Karp's framework; the individual components (swaddling, motion, sound, sucking) have reasonable supporting evidence, the branded package less so. The safety limits here come from the AAP, not from the method.
- **White noise:** AAP guidance — keep it low, keep it away from the cot, don't run it all night at volume.
- **Probiotics/simethicone/gripe water:** the evidence is weak-to-absent for most colic products; the "what works" table reflects that, not enthusiasm.
- **This is general information.** It cannot examine Eldric. If your instinct says something is wrong, that instinct outranks every table in this file — call the PD or go in.
