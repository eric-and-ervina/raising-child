# Dos & Don'ts — The Whole Baby-Care Rule Set on One Page

> **For:** Eric + Ervina, and anyone who holds Eldric (born Mon 26 Oct 2026). This is the *short version of everything* — the rules, the reasons, and nothing else. The how-to detail lives in [`care-techniques.md`](care-techniques.md); the day-to-day plan lives in [`first-six-weeks-program.md`](first-six-weeks-program.md).
> **How to use:** read it once properly, then keep it open when a relative or helper is holding him. When someone asks "can I…?", the answer is almost always in here — and "our PD said no" is a complete sentence.
> *General information, not medical advice. Your paediatrician overrides anything here.*

---

## The ten that matter most

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Back to sleep, every sleep, every time | Stomach or side sleeping, ever |
| Bare cot: firm mattress, fitted sheet, sleep sack | Pillows, blankets, bumpers, toys, nests, positioners |
| Room-share in your room (6–12 months) | Bed-share or fall asleep with him on a sofa/armchair |
| Feed on early cues, 8–12× / 24 h | Wait for crying, or stretch feeds to a schedule |
| Hands washed before every hold | Kiss his face or hands |
| Anyone sick stays away | "It's just a sniffle" |
| Everything by mouth is milk only (until ~6 months) | Water, honey, rice cereal, herbal anything |
| Water ≥70 °C for formula; exact level scoops | Lukewarm water, heaped scoops, re-offering a part-drunk bottle |
| Fever ≥38 °C under 3 months → hospital now | "Wait till morning" |
| Frustrated? Put him in the cot and walk away | Ever shaking him |

## Holding & carrying

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Support head and neck with a hand, every pick-up | Lift him by the arms or without head support | His neck can't hold his head for weeks |
| One hand on him whenever he's on any raised surface | Step away "for a second" — changing table, sofa, bed | Newborns can't roll, then suddenly can |
| Keep him upright, chest-to-chest for carrying | Bouncy/rough jiggling, throwing him up | Shaking can cause fatal bleeding in the brain |
| In a sling/carrier: tight, in view, close enough to kiss, chin off chest, back supported | Forward-facing, chin-on-chest, or loose slings | Blocked airway and hip problems are the real risks |

## Sleep

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Own bare cot next to your bed | Sofa, armchair, beanbag, car seat, rocker as a sleep surface | Suffocation risk is highest on soft/sofa surfaces |
| Room comfortable for a lightly dressed adult; check his neck for sweat | Hat indoors, thick blankets, or being over-bundled | Overheating is a SIDS risk factor |
| Swaddle snug at the chest, **loose at the hips** (legs bent up and out) | Swaddling tight around the hips or legs pinned straight | Hip dysplasia |
| Stop swaddling at the first sign of rolling (~2–4 months) | Swaddle a baby who can roll | He can end up face-down with no free arms |
| Feeds at night in a dim room, boring and quick; bright, chatty days | Play or long chats at 2 a.m. | Day/night confusion is made worse by a 24-hour party |
| A crying baby in a cot is safe — walk away to breathe | Hold him while you're falling asleep | Dropping/rolling onto him is a real cause of death |

## Feeding

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Feed at early cues — rooting, hands to mouth, stirring | Treat crying as the signal | Crying is the *last* cue; feeding then is harder |
| 8–12 feeds / 24 h in the first weeks, including at night | Stretch feeds to 4 hours to "get him on a schedule" | Supply = demand; long gaps quietly cut the milk |
| Latch deep: wide mouth, more areola above than below | Push through pain — it's a latch problem, not a rite of passage | Nipple damage → less feeding → less milk |
| Burp mid-feed and after, upright | Lay him flat straight after a full feed | Spit-up and discomfort |
| Bottle: semi-upright, near-horizontal, paced, stop cues respected | Prop the bottle or chase the last 10 ml | Choking, ear infections, overfeeding |
| Expressed milk: fridge ≤4 days, freezer ≤6–12 months, never refreeze | Re-offer a bottle he already drank from after 2 hours | Warm milk + saliva is a bacterial culture |
| Formula: make one, feed one, wash one | Microwave, reheat twice, or dilute/concentrate | Hot spots scald; wrong dilution is dangerous |
| Wash and sterilise bottles and teats; air-dry, don't tea-towel | Leave milk residue sitting overnight | Fast bacterial growth in a warm climate |
| If a top-up is needed: breast first, bottle after | Replace breastfeeds with bottles if you want supply to hold | A bottle replacing a feed tells the body to make less |

## Cord, skin, and body care

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Keep the cord stump clean and dry; diaper folded below it | Alcohol, powders, oils, herbs, coins, bindings, or pulling it off | Infection; traditional applications on the cord are actively harmful |
| File his nails (soft emery board) while he sleeps | Cut or peel nails in the early weeks; leave him scratching his face | Skin is thin and bleeds easily |
| Plain water for the face; wipe each eye with its own cotton wool | Shared cloths, soap in the eyes, cotton buds inside nose or ears | Cross-infection and injury |
| Clean **around** the foreskin, no retraction | Pull the foreskin back to clean under it | Causes tearing and scarring |
| Bath water ~37–38 °C, hand on him the entire time | Leave him in the tub alone — even to grab a towel | Drowning is silent and takes seconds |
| Sponge baths 2–3× a week while the cord is on; short tub baths after | Daily long baths, or anything you're unsure of on his skin | Dry skin; "if a doctor didn't suggest it, don't put it on him" |
| Never squeeze swollen breasts/nipples (yes, boys too) | Squeeze them "to release the milk" | Causes infection |
| Trim/check nails, wipe spit-up and drool, change wet diapers promptly | Sprinkling powder into diaper folds | Caking and irritation |

## Visitors, illness, and the household

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Every person washes hands before holding him | "I just washed them at home" | Hands are the main transmission route |
| Short visits, 1–2 people, outside feeding/sleep windows | Passing him around a gathering, kissing him | A cold in a 5-week-old can mean A&E |
| Sick = stays away, including grandparents, including "just a sniffle" | Visit anyway and "keep a distance" | He can't cough, blow his nose, or tell you anything |
| Smokers: shirt changed, hands washed | Smoke on clothes/hands near him | Third-hand smoke counts |
| Caregivers up to date on flu + whooping cough (Tdap); he needs a smoke-free home | Rely on "we were all fine" | Protects him before he can be vaccinated |
| Ervina can decline any visit, no reason given | Host, cook, or entertain visitors in week 1–2 | Her rest is a medical input |

## Fever, illness, and when to stop watching

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Have a working thermometer from day one; learn both techniques | Guess from a kiss or a hand on the forehead | Under 3 months the number decides the next step |
| **Rectal/armpit ≥38.0 °C under 3 months → hospital now** | Give paracetamol and "see how tonight goes" | Newborns deteriorate fast and show it poorly |
| Call if unsure — TMC 6250 2222, KKH 6225 5554, 995 for emergencies | Worry about being a nuisance | Nobody is surprised by a first-time parent calling |
| Treat "off" behaviour as a sign: won't wake, refusing 2 feeds, fewer than 6 wet diapers from Day 5 | Wait for him to look really sick | Behaviour changes come before obvious illness in newborns |

## Outings and travel

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Pram or sling; sling also keeps strangers' hands off him | Crowded enclosed places in the first weeks (malls, MRT at peak, parties) | His immune system is inexperienced, not strong |
| Rear-facing car seat or carrier for cars **and Grab/Gojek/Tada** | Hold him on your lap in a moving car — or leave him in the seat when you get out | Law for private-hire cars; never leave a baby in a car |
| Shade, hats, long sleeves, pram cover — no direct sun | Sunscreen under 6 months (unless the PD says so) | Shade beats lotion at this age |
| Time outings around feeds; diaper bag packed before you leave | A full day out in week 2 | A hungry, overstimulated newborn is a miserable trip |

## Home safety

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cot away from curtains, cords, windows, and the aircon/fan's direct blast | Cot against a window or under a hanging cord | Strangulation and overheating risks |
| Anyone who cooks/carries hot drinks knows he's in a sling on you | Drink hot coffee while wearing him | Scalds are a common infant burn |
| Keep plastic bags, coins, small objects, and medicines out of reach | Let pets near him unsupervised | Suffocation/ingestion, always out of nowhere |
| Know your water temperature (storage heater set so taps can't scald) | Test bath water with your hand only | Elbow/wrist, or a bath thermometer |

## Soothing, crying, and you

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 5 S's: swaddle, side/stomach **hold**, shush, swing, suck | Place him on his side/stomach to *sleep* | The hold soothes; the sleep position kills |
| Tag-team in 20-minute blocks when nothing works | Take the crying personally or keep going alone | Crying peaks ~6–8 weeks and it is normal |
| Put him on his back in the cot, walk out, breathe, come back | Ever shake him, or leave him in an unsafe spot in anger | Shaking can kill; the cot is the safe pause |
| Accept help as tasks: cook, laundry, hold him while you sleep | Accept "help" that means hosting | Help that doesn't reduce load isn't help |

**Full playbook:** [`crying-and-soothing.md`](crying-and-soothing.md) — the 60-second screen, the cause sweep, all the techniques, the 5–11 p.m. peak, colic vs reflux, and the safe-put-down plan when you're the one who's fraying.

## Advice to refuse, politely and early

These all come with love attached. Say them once, plainly, as a couple — it's easier in week 1 than in month 3.

- ❌ **Water or rice cereal in a bottle** — no water as a drink under 6 months, no cereal before ~6 months.
- ❌ **Honey** — under 12 months, botulism risk (including in "natural" cough remedies).
- ❌ **Sleeping on the stomach, or in an adult bed** — the two most dangerous bits of advice he'll be offered.
- ❌ **Propping a bottle**, or leaving him to feed alone.
- ❌ **Anything on the cord stump** — powders, oils, coins, bindings, tight belly wraps.
- ❌ **Squeezing his nipples or foreskin**, or "pushing back" the foreskin.
- ❌ **Herbal remedies, jamu, or "just a taste" of anything** without the PD.
- ❌ **Rubbing alcohol/ice for fever, cold baths, or bundling a feverish baby** — fever is treated with correct clothing and medical advice, not shock.
- ❌ **"Let him cry it out"** at newborn age — that advice is for much older babies and never for a hungry or unwell one.

## If you only remember one line

**Back to sleep, bare cot, room-share, feed on cues, hands washed, milk only in his mouth, ≥38 °C means hospital, and never shake him.** Everything else in this repo is detail under those eight.
