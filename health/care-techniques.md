# Care Techniques — Step by Step

> **For:** Eric + Ervina, zero experience assumed. Eldric, born Mon 26 Oct 2026 (3,590 g, TMC). This is the **hands** file: the physical skills, in numbered steps, for the tasks that don't yet have instructions anywhere else in the repo.
> **Use it:** read the section *before* the first time you attempt the task, with the baby in the other parent's arms — not one-handed at 3 a.m. The rules behind these techniques are in [`dos-and-donts.md`](dos-and-donts.md); holding, diapering, cord care, bathing and safe sleep are summarised in [`newborn-care.md`](newborn-care.md) and expanded into full steps here; getting him to *stay* asleep is [`night-and-sleep.md`](night-and-sleep.md); feeding technique is in [`../food-and-feeding/breastfeeding.md`](../food-and-feeding/breastfeeding.md) and [`../food-and-feeding/formula-and-bottles.md`](../food-and-feeding/formula-and-bottles.md).
> *General information, not medical advice. Ask a TMC nurse or the PD to watch you do any of these once — a 2-minute correction beats a week of doing it wrong.*

---

## 0. The two habits under every technique

1. **One hand on him, always.** Flat surface, not a bed he can roll off. If you must step away, he comes with you or goes in the cot.
2. **Wash your hands before every hold and every change** — 20 seconds, soap, including backs of hands and between fingers; sanitiser (≥60% alcohol) if you can't get to a sink. Same for anyone else who touches him.

## 1. Holding him — pick-up, the four holds, and how to still be holding him an hour later

### 1.1 Picking up and putting down

**Up:** slide one hand under his head and neck (fingers spread, thumb and fingers on either side of the neck), the other under his bottom → bring him close to your chest → lift, keeping head higher than body → settle his head into the crook of your elbow or against your shoulder.

**Down:** bottom first, then back, head last; keep your hand under his head until it's on the mattress; slide hands out slowly. **After a feed:** keep him upright on your shoulder for 15–20 minutes if he spits up a lot, then cot. The full sleep-transfer version (warm sheet, stillness, staged withdrawal) is in [`night-and-sleep.md`](night-and-sleep.md) §2.1 — this is the mechanical part.

### 1.2 The four holds, and which to use when

| Hold | How | Best for | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cradle** | Head in the crook of your elbow, forearm along his back, other hand free | Feeding, carrying around the flat, showing him to someone | His head can slide down the crook — keep your forearm slightly tilted up |
| **Shoulder (upright)** | Chest against your chest, his chin above your shoulder (never pressed into it), one hand behind his head, one under his bottom | **Burping, winding, the evening crying peak, walking** — the most effective and the least tiring | Don't let his chin drop to his chest; keep his nose clear of your shirt |
| **Football / clutch** | Body tucked under your arm along your side, head in your hand, back along your forearm | Right after a feed, a sore or gassy tummy, twin-style transfers, caesarean recovery | Supports the body but **not** the head as firmly — keep your hand cupped under the skull |
| **Lap / colic hold** | Face-down along your forearm, head turned to one side, your hand between his legs | Gas, a hard crying bout, a tired arm | **Awake and supervised only.** Never a sleep position |

### 1.3 Holding him for an hour (the real skill)

Nobody tells you that the hard part isn't lifting him — it's that at 9 p.m. you will hold him, walking, for **60–90 minutes**, on no sleep, with a sore back. Make it survivable:

- **Sit before you're tired, not after.** The first 10 minutes of a hold decide whether your shoulders survive it.
- **Elbows in, baby on your sternum.** Carry him high and close, not hanging off your forearms — that's the difference between a 10-minute and a 60-minute hold.
- **Feet flat, knees under your hips, back supported** if you're sitting on the sofa. A cushion under each elbow (or the feeding pillow) takes the whole weight off your arms.
- **The feeding pillow is a holding tool too**, not just for feeds.
- **The yoga/birthing ball** saves your back for bouncing — and it's quieter than walking the corridor at 2 a.m.
- **The sling/carrier is the answer for anything over 20 minutes** ([`night-and-sleep.md`](night-and-sleep.md) §4, fit in §11 below). Hands free, back supported, both of you warmer and calmer. Learn to put it on alone, in the dark, before you need it.
- **Swap holds every 15–20 minutes** — cradle → shoulder → football — so no single set of muscles does the whole hour.
- **Never hold him while you're drowsy.** The rule and the protocol are in [`night-and-sleep.md`](night-and-sleep.md) §4.1 — this is the one place where being tired can kill him.

### 1.4 Passing him to another person

Most drops in the first months happen in a handover, not a lift.

- **Say it out loud:** "I'm passing him to you now" — and the receiver says "I've got him" **before** the hands change over.
- **Both people support him for a full second** — receiver's hand on the head/neck and bottom first, then the giver lets go.
- **Never pass him over a hard floor or a doorway**, and never across a table or a hot drink. Sit down, or hand over at the cot.
- **Nobody takes him out of a cot or off a bed without saying so** — especially with visitors, and especially in a room where someone is half asleep.
- **Never pass him to someone who has been drinking, is sick, or is angry**, however much they want to hold him. "Not right now" is a complete sentence.

### 1.5 Holding a baby who is arching, stiffening, or fighting you

- **Firm beats gentle.** A crying newborn arching backwards will feel worse if you hold him loosely. Wrap him close, hand spread across his upper back and shoulders, and hold **still**.
- **Turn him away from the room**, not into your chest — he needs air and a clear nose.
- **Never hold him by the arms or around the ribs while he arches.** Support the head and the bottom; let the arch happen.
- **If he's arching away from the breast or bottle with a red face:** stop feeding, sit him upright, and burp ([`crying-and-soothing.md`](crying-and-soothing.md) §9.1).

## 2. Swaddle — the 5-step version

Every swaddle fails in the same place: too tight at the hips, too loose at the chest.

1. Spread a large muslin in a diamond, fold the top corner down ~15 cm.
2. Lay him on his back with his neck **on** that fold; his left arm down along his side, slightly bent.
3. Take the left corner, pull it across his chest, and tuck it under his right side and bottom — **snug over the chest and arms.**
4. Bring the bottom corner up over his feet, leaving a hand's width of room for his legs to bend **up and out**.
5. Bring the right corner across and tuck it under his left side.

**Check:** two fingers slide under the chest fabric (snug, not tight); his legs can still bend at the knees; hands can reach his mouth if he works at it; no fabric over his chin or nose. **Stop swaddling at the first sign of rolling (~2–4 months)** — unwrap one arm first for a few nights, then switch to a sleep sack.

### 2.1 When the swaddle doesn't work (the four real problems)

| Problem | Why | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **He screams harder when you swaddle him** | Either it's too loose (so the startle still fires and he feels trapped), or he's already past the point | Re-swaddle **firmly** — arms pinned to his sides, chest snug, then hold him **still** for 2 full minutes before adding anything else. Half-hearted swaddling is the worst of both worlds |
| **He gets an arm out in under a minute** | Blanket too small, or the tuck is shallow | Use a bigger muslin (**at least 120 × 120 cm**); tuck the first corner right **under his far side and bottom**, not just under the shoulder. A velcro/Halo-style swaddle is legitimate and much faster at 3 a.m. |
| **He's sweaty, flushed, or hot in it** | Singapore + a muslin + your body heat + aircon off | Use a **single thin muslin or a 0.5 TOG sack**, no hat, no extra layer underneath, room 24–26 °C. **Check the back of his neck**, not his hands. Overheating is a SIDS risk, and a hot baby cries more ([`newborn-care.md`](newborn-care.md)) |
| **He fights it every single time** | Some babies genuinely hate the feeling of pinned arms | Try **arms-out** or **one arm out** for sleep; try swaddling only the body; try a sleep sack with a snug chest. Keep the hips loose either way. You are allowed to stop swaddling — the startle will wake him a bit more, and that's a fair trade |

**Never:** swaddle so tight the hips can't flex (hip dysplasia), pin the legs straight, cover the head or chin, put a swaddle over the car-seat harness, or swaddle a baby who has started rolling.

### 2.2 Coming off the swaddle (2–4 months)

1. **Watch for the first roll** — tummy-to-back or back-to-tummy, even once, even on your bed.
2. **One arm out for 3–4 nights**, starting with the arm he doesn't sleep on.
3. **Then both arms out**, still using the sack around the body.
4. **Sleep sack from then on** — the same TOG rating you were using.

Expect 2–4 worse nights. That's the cost of the transition, not a sign you did it wrong. **A rolling baby in a swaddle is the actual danger**; a tired week is not.

## 3. Burping — three positions, two minutes each

Burp **mid-feed** if he's squirming or gulping, and **after** every feed. Two to three minutes in one position, then try another. If nothing comes up after ~5 minutes, stop — he may not have swallowed much air.

- **Over the shoulder.** Burp cloth on your shoulder. Hold him upright against you, his chin clear of your shoulder (not pressed into it), his stomach against your chest. Pat or rub firmly upward on his back with a cupped hand.
- **Sitting.** Sit him on your lap facing away, one hand supporting his jaw and chest (thumb and fingers on either side of the jaw, **never the throat**), the other patting his back. Lean him forward slightly.
- **Face-down on your lap.** Lay him tummy-down along your thighs, head turned to one side and **higher than his chest**, pat his back. Watch his face the whole time.

**After burping, hold him upright for a few minutes** before laying him down. **Never** put him to sleep on his side or stomach to "help the wind".

## 4. Diaper change — the sequence that avoids rashes

Already in [`newborn-care.md`](newborn-care.md) — condensed here so this file is usable alone:

1. Hands washed, flat surface, everything within reach first (clean diaper, wipes, barrier cream, a cloth over a boy's front as you open the old one).
2. Open the dirty diaper, wipe the bulk with its own front, then **front to back**, fresh wipe each pass, folds and creases included (scrotum, base of the penis, behind the testicles, the buttock crease).
3. Pat dry, then air-dry 10–15 seconds. Thin zinc-oxide barrier at the first sign of redness.
4. Clean diaper underneath, back edge at belly-button level, front up between the legs, tabs snug (one finger fits under), **leg cuffs flared outward** so they don't leak.
5. **Fold the front below the cord stump** until it falls off.
6. Set him down safely, wash hands.

**Count it:** every wet and dirty diaper for the first weeks — it is the feeding evidence the PD asks for ([`../food-and-feeding/breastfeeding.md`](../food-and-feeding/breastfeeding.md) §4).

### 4.1 The station (set it up once)

**Change him on the floor, on a mat, for the first weeks.** In 689 sqft with a wriggling newborn, a raised surface means one hand is always on him and the other is doing the work alone — and the falls happen in the half-second you reach for a wipe. The floor is not a compromise; it's the safer default until he can be trusted not to roll.

Everything within arm's reach, before you start: **clean diaper open and ready · wipes (or cotton wool + warm water) · barrier cream · a cloth or wipe for the boy's front · the bin · a spare grow suit.** If you have to stand up mid-change, you're already doing it wrong.

### 4.2 The four changes that are harder than the diagram

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Meconium (Days 1–3)** — black, tarry, glue-like, and it does not wipe off | **Oil first.** A little baby oil or petroleum jelly on the cotton wool (or on his bottom) dissolves it; dry wipes just smear it. Expect 3–5 a day, and expect it on the diaper, the grow suit, and you. A wipe warmer is unnecessary; warm water on cotton wool is cheaper |
| **A blowout** (up the back, out the legs) | Do **not** undress him over his head — that's how it ends up in his hair. Wipe what you can **while he's still on the mat**, then slide the dirty suit **down** his body and off his feet. Have the clean suit already open and underneath him before you start. If it's on his hands, wipe them before they reach his mouth |
| **He pees mid-change** | Cover his front with a cloth **the second** the diaper opens — cool air triggers it almost every time. If it lands on him, wipe and dry the skin; if it lands on the wall, laugh and move on. It stops being funny around week 3 |
| **He poos while you're wiping** | Wait 10–20 seconds for it to finish, then wipe once, front to back, and don't chase it. If it's a real stream, put the clean diaper under him **first** and catch it there. Never hold his legs up high and wait — that's how the mess travels |

### 4.3 When the diaper leaks (it's almost always fit, not the diaper)

| Leak pattern | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Up the back** | Back edge too low, or the diaper is too small | Back edge up to **belly-button level or slightly above**; size up if the tabs barely reach the landing zone |
| **Out the legs** | **Leg cuffs folded in** — the most common mistake in the first week | Run a finger around each leg opening and flick the cuff **outward** so it forms a seal |
| **Front, in a boy** | Penis pointing sideways or up | Point him **down** before closing the diaper |
| **Red marks on the thighs** | Tabs too tight | One finger should slide under the waistband and each leg |
| **Constant small leaks overnight** | Nappy changed too rarely, or a wet nappy left too long | Change before the long stretch; a bigger night size is fine once he's past ~4 kg |

**Never** make a diaper tighter to stop a leak, and never use two diapers at once.

### 4.4 Rashes, and what actually fixes them

Most newborn rash is **wetness sitting too long**. The routine: change promptly → **pat** dry, don't rub → **10–15 minutes of air** on a towel when you can → a **thin** zinc-oxide barrier at the first redness (thick paste only if the skin is broken).

**Same-day PD** if: it's bright red with satellite spots (thrush), blistered, bleeding, spreading despite 2 days of barrier cream, or he screams at every change.

**At night:** change the dirty ones, leave a wet one until after the next feed, keep the light dim, and don't unswaddle the top half ([`night-and-sleep.md`](night-and-sleep.md) §2.4).

## 5. Bathing — sponge bath, then tub

**Before the cord falls off: sponge baths only, 2–3× a week.** Warm room (24–26 °C), no draught, everything laid out first: two towels, cotton wool balls, plain water bowl (cooled boiled water for the face), mild fragrance-free wash, clean grow suit, diaper, bin within reach.

1. Wrap him in a towel, head and all, and uncover one part at a time — newborns lose heat fast.
2. **Face first, plain water only:** each eye with its own cotton ball, **from the inner corner outward**, one stroke, then a new ball. Ears: outer folds only with a damp cloth, **never inside**. Nose: only what you can see at the entrance.
3. Then body, top down: neck folds, arms, hands, chest, legs, feet — then the diaper area **last**. Pat dry as you go, especially every fold.
4. Hair last: a damp cloth or a little wash, then dry the scalp.

**After the cord falls off: tub baths.** Water **5–8 cm**, **37–38 °C** (test with your wrist or elbow, or a bath thermometer — warm, not hot). Support his head and neck on your forearm with your hand around the far shoulder; with your free hand wash him in the same top-down order. Wash the hair last, then lift him straight into a towel on your lap and dry the folds.

**The rules with no exceptions:** never leave him in water — if you forgot the towel, take him with you; empty the tub immediately after; never fill a tub with him in it; never take your hand off him. Daily baths are not needed and dry the skin.

## 6. Nose, eyes, ears, mouth

| Part | How | Never |
|---|---|---|
| **Nose (blocked, snuffly)** | 1–2 drops of sterile saline in each nostril (or a saline spray for babies) → wait 30–60 seconds → squeeze the bulb syringe **before** inserting, put just the tip at the nostril opening, release slowly | Cotton buds inside, decongestant drops/sprays, essential oils, sucking harder because it's not clearing |
| **NoseFrida-style aspirator** | Put the tip at the nostril opening, seal gently, suck through the mouth tube (the filter stops anything reaching you), stop when the sound clears | Going deep, doing it more than a few times a day, using it on a dry nose |
| **Eyes (watery/crusty, blocked tear duct)** | Cooled boiled water, one cotton ball per eye, **inner corner outward**, one wipe, discard | Reusing a ball, wiping back toward the tear duct, soap in the eyes |
| **Ears** | Damp cloth on the outer ear and behind it only | Anything inside the ear canal, ear drops, syringing |
| **Mouth** | Nothing needed — milk does the cleaning | Wiping with honey, glycerine, or herbs; picking at the white patches that **don't** wipe off (that's thrush → same-day PD) |

If the blocked-tear-duct eye is red, swollen, or has pus, that's a same-day call — see the skin/eye table in [`newborn-care.md`](newborn-care.md).

## 7. Nails

He will scratch his own face in the first hours of life, and the scratches look far worse than they are.

1. Do it while he's asleep or milk-drunk, in good light, with someone else nearby if you can.
2. Hold one finger steady, press the fingertip pad down and away from the nail, and **file downward with a soft emery board** — no back-and-forth sawing.
3. Round off any snag; don't file down to the pink (it's sensitive and can bleed).
4. From a few weeks on, blunt-tip baby scissors or clippers are fine: hold the pad down, cut **straight across**, then file the corners. Never cut a curved nail, never cut in the dark, never peel a nail off.
5. Mittens only as a stopgap when he's actually scratched — remove them when he's awake. He needs free hands to learn about them.

**If you nick the skin:** press a clean cloth on it for a minute or two. It will stop. Don't panic, and don't dig for it further.

## 8. Taking his temperature — the correct way

**≥38.0 °C under 3 months = hospital, immediately** ([`red-flags-and-emergencies.md`](red-flags-and-emergencies.md)). The technique matters because a wrong reading is worse than no reading.

**Armpit (axillary) — the everyday one.**
1. Clean, dry digital thermometer with a clean tip.
2. Put the tip in the **centre** of the armpit, skin against skin, arm down and held gently against his side.
3. Hold it there until it beeps (~1 minute with a newborn digital thermometer).
4. Note the number **and** the method. **Armpit reads lower than true temperature** — treat **37.5 °C or more in the armpit** as a call-the-doctor number in a baby under 3 months, and never use "it's only armpit" to talk yourself out of calling.

**Rectal — the number doctors trust.**
1. Lubricate the tip with petroleum jelly. Lay him on his back with his legs gently bent to his chest, or on his side.
2. Insert **only the tip — about 1–1.5 cm (½ inch)** into the rectum. Never push against resistance.
3. Keep your hand on it so it can't be pushed deeper; hold until it beeps.
4. Wash the thermometer with soap and water (or alcohol wipe) before and after.

**Never:** a forehead strip or "smart patch" as your only check (they're the least reliable); an ear thermometer as your answer under ~6 months (newborn ear canals make it inaccurate); a reading taken right after a bath or while he's heavily bundled (wait 20 minutes, undress him to a light layer, re-check); guessing a fever from how hot his hand or forehead feels.

**Write down:** time, method, number, and anything else going on. Fever plus behaviour changes is the story that makes a doctor act.

## 9. Giving medicine

1. **Only what the PD prescribed or OK'd, at the dose the PD gave by his weight.** Under 3 months, never treat a fever at home and "wait and see" — that call comes first. A common paracetamol dose range is 10–15 mg/kg every 4–6 hours, max 4 doses in 24 hours, but the PD's number for Eldric is the number you use.
2. Use the **syringe or dosing cup that came with the medicine** — teaspoons and "half a bottle cap" are how babies get the wrong dose. Measure at eye level, over a sink.
3. Hold him semi-upright, in the crook of your arm. Slide the syringe into the inside of his cheek and squirt slowly in small amounts, letting him swallow; hold the cheek closed for a second if it's running out.
4. **Don't** squirt into the back of his throat (coughing/choking), don't add it to a bottle of milk unless the PD says so, and don't redose because he spat some up without asking.
5. **Never** aspirin, adult tablets, half-doses of anything, "baby syrup" bought without asking, or cough/cold medicines — these are not for babies and several are dangerous under 12 months.
6. **Write it down:** time, medicine, dose. Two tired parents double-dosing is the most common medicine mistake of the first year.
7. Keep all medicines out of the cot area and out of reach — including your own.

## 10. Dressing and checking he's the right temperature

- **Dress him how you're dressed plus one layer.** Indoors: a grow suit / bodysuit is usually enough in Singapore — no hat, no mittens, no beanies while sleeping.
- **Check the back of his neck**, not his hands: warm and dry = right; sweaty and hot = take a layer off; cool = add one. Cold hands and feet are normal (his circulation is still figuring itself out).
- **The grow suit drill:** unbuckle/snap, slide his legs in, arms in without pulling hard across the face, snap from the neck down, run a finger around the neck and armholes for tight elastic, and check that nothing is twisted under his back.
- **No ribbons, drawstrings, loose ties, or long cords** — and no cords, clips or dummies pinned to the cot.
- **Changing a diaper at night:** keep the lights dim and the talk to nothing — dim, quick, back to the cot.

## 11. Carriers and slings — fit matters more than the brand

The fit test is **TICKS**:

- **T**ight — snug enough that he can't slump or shift.
- **I**n view at all times — you can always see his face; nothing covers his head or nose.
- **C**lose enough to kiss — his head is right under your chin.
- **K**eep the chin off the chest — two fingers of space between chin and chest, and no head flopping forward. A chin-to-chest position can close an airway in minutes.
- **S**upported back — his back should be in its natural curve, not bent or flat-squashed.

Also: knees should sit **higher than his bottom**, legs out in a wide "M" (this is what protects his hips — narrow, dangling legs are the wrong shape); never forward-facing before he has head control (and never with a floppy head); no running, cooking, or hot drinks while wearing him; **never** use a sling or carrier as his sleep surface for long stretches — if he falls asleep, keep him in view and check his breathing is unobstructed; check on him every few minutes. Older, loose "bag slings" are the ones with the deaths attached — use a structured carrier or a properly tied wrap.

## 12. Car seat and leaving the house

- **Rear-facing, in the back seat, from the first trip home.** Singapore law requires a child restraint for children under 1.35 m in cars **and private-hire vehicles (Grab, Gojek, Tada)**; taxis are the only exemption, and only with the child in the rear seat.
- **Harness snug:** no slack you can pinch at the shoulder, straps flat, chest clip (if your seat model has one) at armpit level, no bulky coat or thick swaddle under the straps — strap him in, then a blanket *over* the top.
- **Never** in front of an airbag; **never** alone in the car, not for one minute (even "just to pay"); **never** on top of a shopping trolley.
- **Not a sleep surface:** short naps happen, but anything long — use the cot. If a car ride goes over **~2 hours**, break it, take him out and let him stretch and feed.
- **Before you lock the door:** keys, phone, wallet, baby. Say it out loud. Every time.

**Outing bag (pack before you leave, not at the lift):** 2 diapers + wipes + changing mat, a change of grow suit, 1–2 bottles (or a nursing cover), burp cloth, pacifier if used, a hat, and your own water. In the Singapore heat, add a spare cloth for sweat and keep the pram shaded.

## 13. Tummy time — the how

Start in week 1 on your chest. Then, on a firm mat or folded blanket on the floor while you're awake and watching:

1. Lay him on his tummy, arms bent up near his shoulders (not trapped underneath).
2. Get down to his level — face-to-face — and talk. Roll a small towel under his chest if he face-plants.
3. **Short and often:** 1–2 minutes at first, several times a day, immediately after a change or before a feed (never right after a full feed — he'll spit up).
4. Stop at the first real protest; more small sessions beat a long one he hates. The progression targets are in [`../development/first-three-months.md`](../development/first-three-months.md).

**Never** leave him unattended on his tummy, and never let him fall asleep there.

## 14. Hand hygiene and the other people

- **You and every visitor:** wash hands before holding, after diapers, after the toilet, after coming home, before expressing or handling bottles.
- **Bottles/teats/pump parts:** wash with hot soapy water and a bottle brush (teats inside and out and under the rim), then sterilise; air-dry on a rack — not a tea towel. Full detail in [`../food-and-feeding/formula-and-bottles.md`](../food-and-feeding/formula-and-bottles.md) §4.
- **Wipes and creams** are for his body, never his mouth or eyes.

## 15. Getting checked out — the skills to have watched once

Ask a TMC nurse, the PD, or the lactation consultant to watch **you** do each of these (before discharge if possible, otherwise at the Day 3–5 visit). Tick it when it's been done:

- ☐ Hold him and pick him up from the cot
- ☐ Change a diaper, front to back, with the cord fold
- ☐ Swaddle so the hips stay loose
- ☐ Burp him in at least two of the three positions
- ☐ Take an armpit temperature; ask to be shown the rectal method
- ☐ Saline drops + bulb syringe on a stuffy nose
- ☐ File his nails
- ☐ Bathe him (sponge bath first, then the tub after the cord falls off)
- ☐ Watch a hands-on infant CPR/choking demonstration — and book the course ([`red-flags-and-emergencies.md`](red-flags-and-emergencies.md))

> You will feel clumsy for the first dozen attempts. He is more robust than he looks, and the goal is not perfect technique — it's calm, supported, unhurried hands. Slow is smooth; smooth is fast.
