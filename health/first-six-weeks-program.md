# The First Six Weeks — The Program

> **For:** Eric + Ervina. Eldric, born Mon 26 Oct 2026 (TMC, 3,590 g). Week 1 starts on his birth day: **Week 1 = Mon 26 Oct – Sun 1 Nov 2026**, Week 6 = Mon 30 Nov – Sun 6 Dec 2026, Day 42 = **Mon 7 Dec 2026**.
> **What this is:** the *program* layer the other files assume — the 24-hour cycle, who is on duty when, the week-by-week focus, the feeding phases, and the checkpoints where you stop and verify. The reference files explain *what things are* ([`newborn-care.md`](newborn-care.md), [`dos-and-donts.md`](dos-and-donts.md)); this file is *what you do, day by day, for six weeks*.
> **How to use it:** put §3 (shifts) and §4 (daily checklist) on the fridge. Read the current week in §5 on Sunday night. Log in §11 — a notebook at the feeding spot is enough.
> *General information, not medical advice. The PD's dates and instructions override this program.*

---

## 1. The program in one page

- **One cycle, repeated all day and all night:** feed → burp → change → a short awake window → soothe → sleep. Nothing else is required of you this month.
- **One number to defend:** Ervina's **4 uninterrupted hours of sleep in every 24**. That is the medical intervention that holds this whole program together.
- **One record to keep:** feeds and diapers, every 24 hours. It answers every "is he okay?" question.
- **One focus per week** (§5) — feeding, hygiene, admin, decisions — so nothing important is left to memory at 3 a.m.
- **One review every Sunday, 20 minutes** (§8), to change what isn't working before it becomes a crisis.

## 2. The 24-hour cycle

At this age he does roughly **8–12 feeds in 24 hours, every 2–3 hours**, including overnight. A full cycle is:

| Step | Roughly | Who | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Feed** | 20–40 min | Ervina (breast) or either (bottle) | Feed at early cues — rooting, hands to mouth, stirring. Crying is late ([`../food-and-feeding/breastfeeding.md`](../food-and-feeding/breastfeeding.md) §1) |
| **Burp** | 2–5 min | Eric wherever he's awake | Upright, then hold upright a few minutes if he spits up ([`care-techniques.md`](care-techniques.md) §3) |
| **Change** | 5 min | Eric | Full change for a dirty diaper; a wet one can wait till after the feed |
| **Awake window** | 15–30 min | Whoever's on duty | **45–60 minutes total from the start of the feed** is the whole alert window at this age. Tummy time, a face, a voice — then he's done |
| **Soothe → sleep** | 10–30 min | Whoever's on duty | Swaddle (until he rolls), shush, hold, cot on his back |

**The cycle rules:** don't wake a feeding baby to play, and don't try to keep an overtired one awake. **Wake him to feed** every 2–3 hours (and at night) until the PD says weight gain lets you stop. **Shape day and night from week 1:** bright, normal-noise, chatty days; dim, quiet, boring nights — no playing, no long chats at 2 a.m.

**What the day is not:** a schedule he must hit. The clock is a boundary for feeds and for your shifts, not a timetable for him.

## 3. The shift roster — the part that keeps you both sane

Two parents, one newborn, no shifts = two exhausted people and a 3 a.m. fight. Write this on the fridge and keep it until it stops working.

**The blocks (adjust the hours, keep the shape):**

| Block | On duty | Off duty | What "on" means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **19:00–21:00** (cluster-feed window) | Eric | Ervina rests/feeds only | Eric runs the evening: fetch him, burp, change, resettle, walk the corridor. Ervina is in the room between feeds, not hosting |
| **21:00–01:00** | Eric | **Ervina — protected 4-hour block** | Eric brings the baby **to her** for feeds and takes him away the second the feed ends. She does not burp, change, or resettle |
| **01:00–05:00** | Ervina (+ Eric if it breaks down) | Eric sleeps | Keep it dim and wordless. If two hours pass and nothing works, **wake Eric** — don't endure a second hour alone |
| **05:00–08:00** | Eric | **Ervina — sleep until 08:00** | Take him after the early feed; walk him, sling him, or hold him while she sleeps |
| **09:00–13:00** (if grandparents/helper are around) | Grandparents/helper | Ervina (sleep between feeds) | This is a task, not a visit: hold him, feed **her** nothing, do the laundry, cook |

**Phase A (weeks 1–2, no bottle):** because Ervina is breastfeeding on demand, her 4-hour block is protected by *taking everything except the feed away from her* — she feeds side-lying with her eyes closed where possible, Eric does all the rest. The **09:00–13:00** block is usually the easiest real one to protect.

**Phase B (week 3+, once one bottle a day is established):** Eric or a grandparent takes **one entire night feed** (e.g. the 04:00) with expressed milk or formula, and Ervina sleeps through it — that's a true 4–5 hour block. Ervina pumps to replace that feed if supply is the goal ([`../food-and-feeding/formula-and-bottles.md`](../food-and-feeding/formula-and-bottles.md) §6).

**The rules that make it work:** "off" means earplugs, another room, no chores; don't both stay awake for the same feed; whoever is on duty owns the resettle; write down what happened in the log so the next person isn't guessing; and nobody counts a shift they didn't actually take.

## 4. Daily checklist (five minutes morning, five minutes night)

**Morning:** log overnight feeds + diapers · check the cord stump (clean and dry? any redness/pus?) · nails (any snags?) · vitamin D drops if the PD said so · one tummy-time session · open curtains, get daylight and air through the flat · wipe the changing table down · Ervina: water bottle filled and within reach, pad changed, shower/sitz if she wants one.

**Night:** restock the feeding station (water, snack, burp cloths, phone charger) · wash and sterilise the bottles and pump parts used today · make sure the **next bottle or the feeding spot** is ready for the 3 a.m. shift · check tomorrow's appointments · set the shift blocks out loud ("you're on till 1, I've got 1 to 5") · write one line in the log.

**Never skip:** hands before every hold · back-to-sleep in the bare cot · the ≥38 °C rule · the 4-hour block.

## 6. The feeding program — phases, not a schedule

Feeding is demand-led; the *program* is what **you** do in each phase. Detail: [`../food-and-feeding/breastfeeding.md`](../food-and-feeding/breastfeeding.md) and [`../food-and-feeding/formula-and-bottles.md`](../food-and-feeding/formula-and-bottles.md).

| Phase | When | The plan | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Hospital** | 26–28 Oct | Skin-to-skin, colostrum, feed on cues, an observed feed with the TMC lactation consultant before discharge. Nothing by bottle unless it's medically needed | Any latch problem caught while help is in the room |
| **1 — Establish** | Day 2–5 (27–31 Oct) | 8–12 feeds/24 h, wake him, log diapers **and** stools by day; manage engorgement; **the Day 3–5 weigh-in** | Milk in; stools turning yellow; jaundice |
| **2 — Protect supply** | Week 1–2 (to ~8 Nov) | Feed on cues day and night, no unnecessary top-ups; if a top-up is needed, breast first, bottle after; back to birth weight by Day 10–14 | Weight regain, diaper counts, sore nipples |
| **3 — Open the door to a bottle** | Week 3–4 (9–22 Nov) | **One bottle a day**, given by someone other than Ervina, slow teat, paced, ~15–20 min; pump to replace that feed if supply is the goal | Flow preference (gulping, refusing the breast), supply dip |
| **4 — Rhythm** | Week 5–6 (23 Nov – 6 Dec) | Feed pattern holds through the 6-week spurt; portions of 90–120 ml in the fridge/freezer for the handover; decide the return-to-work feeding plan on purpose | Crying peak being mistaken for hunger; stash growing but supply dropping |
| **5 — Return prep** | Week 7–12 (Dec – 18 Jan) | Pumping ramp (§7), caregiver practice with bottles, a trial half-day away | Mastitis from skipped pumps; baby refusing bottles from the caregiver |

**If the plan becomes mixed feeding,** the switch is gradual: replace one feed at a time, over days, so Ervina doesn't get engorged or blocked ([`../food-and-feeding/formula-and-bottles.md`](../food-and-feeding/formula-and-bottles.md) §1). **Never** stop breastfeeding abruptly to "switch".

## 7. Weeks 7–12 — the pumping ramp and the exit plan (Dec → 18 Jan 2027)

Only if Ervina is returning to work on **~Mon 18 Jan 2027** (12 weeks from birth).

| When | Pumping step | Stash target |
|---|---|---|
| **Weeks 7–8** (7–20 Dec) | One pump a day, right after the first morning feed (10–15 min, or until 5 minutes after the flow stops). Whenever a bottle replaces a feed, pump to replace it | 2–4 portions of 90–120 ml |
| **Weeks 9–10** (21 Dec – 3 Jan) | Add a second pump (midday or after the evening cluster) | 4–6 portions |
| **Weeks 11–12** (4–18 Jan) | Rehearsal: one half-day out while the caregiver gives 1–2 bottles; one full work-hours rehearsal; finalise childcare | A day's worth (5–6 portions) + a written caregiver sheet |
| **Back at work** | Pump every 3–4 hours (e.g. 10:00, 13:00, 16:00), 15–20 min both sides; fridge at work; cooler bag home | Rotate: oldest milk first |

**Storage rules stay the same** — fridge ≤4 days, freezer best within 6 months, never refreeze, never microwave, and in a >25 °C HDB room treat the 4-hour room-temperature window as a ceiling ([`../food-and-feeding/breastfeeding.md`](../food-and-feeding/breastfeeding.md) §6).

## 8. The Sunday review — 20 minutes, both parents, coffee included

Answer these out loud, write the answers in §11, change one thing:

1. **Diapers:** average wet and dirty per day this week — are we above the floor in the feeding file §4?
2. **Weight:** weighed this week? (If a scale exists at home, use the same one, naked, same time of day — otherwise wait for the PD. Home scales bounce; the PD's chart decides.)
3. **Feeds:** how many per 24 h, and did any gap go past ~4 hours?
4. **Ervina's 4-hour block:** how many days this week did it actually happen? If under 5, the roster changes today, not next week.
5. **Red flags:** anything from [`red-flags-and-emergencies.md`](red-flags-and-emergencies.md) that we explained away?
6. **Visitors/help:** who actually reduced the load, and who added to it? Adjust the guest list.
7. **Admin:** what's the next paperwork step, and whose name is on it?
8. **One change for next week:** exactly one. Write it down.

## 10. When the program is failing — change something, don't tough it out

- **Under 6 wet diapers a day from Day 5, no weight regain by Day 14, or jaundice spreading →** call the PD today. That's the program's tripwire, not a personality test.
- **Ervina hasn't had a 4-hour block for 3 days →** change the roster today, or call in grandparents/pay for help this week. Sleep loss is the fastest route to a crisis ([`postpartum-recovery.md`](postpartum-recovery.md) §5).
- **Feeds creeping under 8 a day with a sleepy baby →** wake him, undress him for feeds, and call the lactation consultant.
- **Top-ups becoming the norm without a decision →** decide, as a couple, whether supply is the goal. Both answers are fine; drifting into one is not.
- **Nothing logged for 3 days →** restart with a notebook at the feeding spot. Memory is not evidence, and the PD will ask.
- **Both of you angry most evenings →** that's a shift-roster problem (and often a sleep problem), not a relationship verdict. Rewrite §3, then re-read [`postpartum-recovery.md`](postpartum-recovery.md) §6.
